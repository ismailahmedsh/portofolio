## R1: ETF universe, selection criteria, and concrete candidates (US- and Canada-listed)

### Takeaway
A long-term core for a Canadian investor can be built from four to six very cheap index ETFs (total US, total ex-US developed, emerging, Canada, aggregate bonds) or from a single all-in-one asset-allocation ETF (XEQT/VEQT at 0.17% management fee, 0.19-0.24% MER). Canadian-listed funds that hold stocks directly (XUU, XEF, XEC, XIC) avoid the second "wrap" layer of US withholding tax that VFV/ZSP-type wrappers carry; US-listed VTI/VOO/VT/VXUS are cheapest (0.03-0.07%) but only fully tax-efficient inside an RRSP. Fees across the core universe are now so low (0.03-0.25%) that tax structure, account type, and discipline matter more than the last basis point.

### Cited Findings

**Selection criteria (what the sources say matters)**
- Expense ratio: Morningstar India advises favouring lower expense ratios; low trading volume widens bid-ask spreads while high volume narrows them — [Morningstar India](https://www.morningstar.in/posts/62027/5-things-look-etf.aspx)
- Tracking difference (fund return minus index return) is the practical measure of how well the fund delivers its index; a rule-of-thumb is that tracking difference should stay within ~0.10% of the expense ratio; a positive tracking difference is rare and usually comes from securities-lending income — [fffinstill ETF due diligence checklist](https://fffinstill.com/learning/concepts/etf-due-diligence-checklist); [fundsupermart](https://secure.fundsupermart.com/fsm/article/view/14972/here-s-how-we-select-the-best-etfs-and-how-you-can-too)
- Bid/ask spreads are a real trading cost; "expenses from getting into and out of an ETF position can stack up if the ETF is trading at wide bid/ask spreads"; AUM and volume are only initial indicators — check spreads and premiums/discounts to NAV; check spreads during normal hours, not at the open or close — [Schwab Asset Management, How to evaluate ETFs](https://www.schwabassetmanagement.com/content/how-to-evaluate-etfs); [etfbeacon selection framework](https://etfbeacon.com/learn/how-to-pick-an-etf)
- AUM threshold: one guide suggests at least US$100 million AUM plus meaningful daily volume to limit closure risk — [etfbeacon](https://etfbeacon.com/learn/how-to-pick-an-etf)
- The same ETF listed on different exchanges can trade at very different spreads; check the listing you will actually use — [quantroutine (UCITS S&P 500 guide)](https://quantroutine.com/learn/how-to-choose-sp500-ucits-etf/)
- ETF.com's general selection framework (index/exposure first, then cost, then liquidity/structure) — [ETF.com, How Do You Choose The Right ETF?](https://www.etf.com/sections/etf-basics/how-do-you-choose-right-etf)
- BlackRock's due-diligence checklist covers exposure, cost, liquidity, structure — [BlackRock Singapore, Choosing the right ETF](https://blackrock.com/sg/en/etfs-simplified/choosing-the-right-etf)

**Canadian-specific structural criterion: foreign withholding tax (FWT) and domicile**
- Level I FWT = the 15% US withholding on dividends paid to a Canadian fund/investor; Level II = a second layer when a Canadian-listed ETF holds a US-listed ETF ("wrap"). Holding a US-listed ETF (e.g., VOO) in a TFSA or RESP loses the 15% for good; the RRSP exemption covers US tax on US-listed ETFs held directly — [Advisor.ca / PWL Bender-Bortolotti white paper summary](https://www.advisor.ca/practice/your-business/foreign-withholding-taxes-how-to-estimate-the-hidden-tax-drag-on-u-s-and-international-equity-index-funds-and-etfs/); [Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/industry/strategies/etfs-and-foreign-withholding-taxes/)
- VFV's main holding is VOO, so VFV is a wrapper carrying Level II drag; Bender's (2014) worked example put VFV's all-in RRSP cost at ~0.46%/yr vs. 0.17% MER alone at the time (MER has since fallen to 0.09%, but the structural point stands) — [Advisor.ca, The trouble with foreign withholding taxes](https://www.advisor.ca/investments/market-insights/the-trouble-with-foreign-withholding-taxes/)
- For registered accounts, Bender: a Canadian-listed ETF holding international stocks directly is "always preferable" to a similar US-listed one; in an RRSP a US-listed ETF is more tax-efficient than a Canadian-listed ETF holding US equities — [Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/industry/strategies/etfs-and-foreign-withholding-taxes/)
- Canadian Couch Potato's FWT explainers (2014, 2016 updates) — [CCP 2014](https://canadiancouchpotato.com/2014/09/12/foreign-withholding-taxes-in-international-equity-etfs/); [CCP 2016 revisited](https://canadiancouchpotato.com/2016/07/11/foreign-withholding-taxes-revisited/)
- Globe and Mail: one-stop (asset-allocation) ETFs are "great despite the tax drag" — [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/education/article-one-stop-etfs-are-great-despite-the-tax-drag)
- WealthNorth lists XEQT among Canadian-listed ETFs suited to a TFSA (secondary source; cost table unverified) — [WealthNorth](https://wealthnorth.ca/investing/etfs/tax-on-us-etfs-in-canada/)

**Home bias for Canadians**
- Vanguard Canada (2023 paper, Bilal Hasanjee) concludes 30% Canadian / 70% foreign equities is a reasonable "optimal" split for Canadians using minimum-variance analysis; volatility starts rising once foreign exceeds ~70%. Canadian stocks are ~3.4% of global market cap; Canadians held 52.2% of equities at home as of June 30, 2022 (>15x overweight); top-10 Canadian stocks = 36.9% of the broad domestic market; the market is overweight energy and financials; the analysis did not model the dividend tax credit — [Investment Executive summary](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/industry/focus-on-products/how-much-home-bias-is-too-much/); [Findependence Hub](https://findependencehub.com/?p=205458); primary PDF: [Vanguard Canada CHBP_062023](https://www.vanguard.ca/content/dam/intl/americas/canada/en/documents/CHBP_062023_V8_secure.pdf)

### Inferences
- The ex-Canada world is now accessible for 0.03-0.07% (US-listed) or 0.07-0.22% (Canada-listed). At these levels a 0.10% fee difference on C$100k is C$100/yr; account-type FWT drag (0.2-0.5%/yr on US/intl equity in a TFSA via wrappers) is a bigger lever than fee shopping.
- For a monthly contributor, Canada-listed funds avoid CAD->USD conversion costs and Norbert's-gambit friction on every deposit; US-listed ETFs make most sense for large RRSP balances where the 15% Level I drag is exempt.
- XEQT and VEQT are near-substitutes: same 0.17% fee, ~45% US, 25-30% Canada; XEQT has slightly less Canada and more EM-light; VEQT has more Canada and more EM. Performance differences over 1-3 years are within noise (<1 pt).
- XUU (0.07%, holds US stocks directly via iShares US ETFs) is the structurally cleaner Canada-listed US-total-market choice vs VUN (0.17%, wraps VTI); VFV/ZSP (0.09%) wrap VOO/IVV-like exposure.

## R2: Return and stability history of candidate ETFs and indices; 2025 and 2026 YTD; concentration risk

### Takeaway
US large caps compounded ~10% nominal (6.6% real) a year since 1928 but with -47%/-55%/-34%/-25% drawdowns in 2000-02, 2007-09, 2020 and 2022 and a lost decade (2000s: +1.5%/yr nominal, -1.1% real). 2025 was a +17.9% year for the S&P 500 but +31-34% for EAFE/EM and +20.5% (CAD) for XEQT; through Oct 30, 2026 the S&P 500 is +11.7% total return, VXUS +9.9%, VT +12.7%, and the top 10 S&P 500 stocks are ~39% of the index (record ~41% in late 2025), with Nasdaq-100 top 10 at ~45-47%.

## R3: Long-term allocation strategies, factor/dividend/leverage/trend evidence, and 2025-2026 capital market assumptions

### Takeaway
The weight of evidence favours a simple, low-cost, globally diversified equity-heavy core (three-fund or a single all-in-one such as XEQT/VEQT); academic lifecycle work (Anarkulova-Cederburg-O'Doherty, 2023-2024) even argues for 100% equities with ~33% domestic / 67% international for life. Factor tilts (small-cap value, profitability) have long-run support but multi-year underperformance stretches (AVUV trailed VTI over 3 years to mid-2026); dividend screens have not beaten the total market (SCHD lagged VTI ~1.6-2 pts/yr since 2011); daily-reset leveraged ETFs are regulator-flagged as unsuitable beyond one session (TQQQ -81% in 2022 vs Nasdaq-100 -33%); trend overlays (Faber 200-day SMA GTAA) cut drawdowns (max -11.7% through Mar 2025) at the cost of lower CAGR (6.05%). Forward 10-year expectations are muted: Vanguard 4.8-6.8% US equities (Sep 30, 2026 run), JPM 6.7% US large cap / 6.4% 60-40 (Oct 2025), AQR 3.4% real for global 60/40 (Dec 31, 2025), Research Affiliates ~0.8% real US equities (secondary).

### Inferences
- Across Vanguard, JPM, AQR and RA, forward 10-year US equity expectations (≈5-7% nominal, ≈1-3% real) are well below the 9.9%/6.6% 1928-2025 record, while bonds (5-6% nominal) are forecast within ~1 pt of equities. For a 15+ year accumulator this argues for diversifying away from US-only (ex-US and EM forecasts are 0.5-1 pt higher at JPM/Vanguard) more than for abandoning equities.
- Lifecycle evidence (Cederburg et al.) and the Vanguard Canada home-bias paper point in a consistent direction for a Canadian: equity-dominant, roughly 25-33% domestic, the rest global — which is close to what XEQT (25% Canada) and VEQT (30% Canada) already implement.
- Factor and dividend tilts are "maybe" improvements with real cost (0.25% fee, tracking regret, extra FWT on dividends); leveraged ETFs and tactical overlays carry documented path-dependency and implementation (rebalance-date, whipsaw, tax) risks that a monthly contributor does not need to take to reach long-run equity returns.
