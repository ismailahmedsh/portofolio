## Key question 1: Egypt (highest priority)

### Takeaway
Egypt is now on Interactive Brokers' own "Available Countries and Territories" list (read 2026-11-02 on both the IBKR Canada and IBKR UK sites), so an Egypt resident can apply; which IBKR entity carries the account is still unconfirmed. The FRA position found is unchanged since Decision 37 of 2014: local brokers may not deal in foreign unlisted securities for Egyptian or resident-foreign clients, and no 2023-2026 FRA decision reversing this was found; Egypt-serving apps therefore route US stocks through non-Egyptian entities (Thndr via its ADGM company Thndr Financial Ltd; EFG Hermes ONE via a Bahrain subsidiary). The EGX has one listed ETF (Beltone's EGX30 ETF, trading at EGP 55.49 on 2026-11-02, AUM not published) and nine gold/silver funds that are open-ended, not exchange-traded (FRA data to end-June 2026). On tax, the primary treaty text is now read: Article 4 uses the standard OECD tie-breaker, Article 10 caps dividend withholding at 15% (with a 20%-average ceiling on Egypt's general income tax for Canadian-resident individuals), Article 13 has no "other property" clause, and Article 22 gives both countries a credit method. Egyptian domestic law taxes a resident individual's foreign-source income only if Egypt is their "centre of commercial, industrial or professional activities" (undefined on the PwC/Deloitte pages; an EY summary of the executive regulations says it is where decisions are taken and vital interests are located), non-residents are taxed only on Egyptian-source income, and since 29 July 2026 (Law 151/2026) gains on EGX-listed securities are not subject to capital gains tax. FX: the CBE's 2017 removal of the US$100,000 transfer cap still stands, there is no general prohibition on moving legitimately sourced funds abroad, but banks apply AML source-of-funds checks; no written rule on outward portfolio-investment transfers was found.

#### (b) FRA rules on residents buying foreign-listed securities
- FRA (then EFSA) Decision No. 37 of 2014, dated 5 March 2014, "regulating the trading activities of securities brokerage and portfolio management firms in foreign securities not listed on Egyptian exchanges": dealing in such securities is barred for Egyptian and resident-foreign clients; permitted only for non-resident clients, with the Authority's prior approval, with execution "restricted to non-resident foreigners" and payment "from foreign currency sources abroad"; firms must keep a register of such trades. The full official text was not retrieved (regalert.today returned HTTP 429 twice; summary is from the search index of that page) (secondary summary of a primary decision). — [regalert.today Decision 37/2014](https://regalert.today/document/2c01cc49-2a50-4fbf-8a07-bba6b25e1b9e/text)
- Contemporary reporting of the same decision: brokers may trade foreign shares "on behalf of non-Egyptian investors who do not reside in the country and who use financing from abroad"; Egyptian firms "remained banned from trading stocks not listed in Egypt on behalf of local investors or on their own accounts" (2014). — [Daily News Egypt, 5 March 2014](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/2014/03/05/egypt-lifts-ban-brokerage-firms-trading-foreign-stocks/); [Ahram Online 2014](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/95964.aspx)
- FRA Decision No. 303 of 2024 (reported 22 January 2025) amends Decision 94/2018 on securities not listed on the EGX: raises the threshold for EGX Operations Committee approval of unlisted-securities ownership transfers from EGP 20m to EGP 60m and extends the bank-deposit recognition window from one to two months. It does not address residents trading foreign-listed securities. — [Daily News Egypt, 22 Jan 2025](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/2025/01/22/fra-introduces-new-rules-for-securities-not-listed-on-egx/)
- FRA Board Resolution No. 226 of 2025 (published 22 October 2025, amending Resolution 61/2017): licensed custodians must "ensure that their clients possess accounts with a bank registered with the Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)" when opening a securities trading account, with MCDR notified of the details; existing clients get a one-year grace period; adds e-wallet transfers as a dividend payment method. It covers domestic trading accounts only and does not mention foreign securities or foreign currency. — [FRA, 22 Oct 2025](https://fra.gov.eg/en/?p=18786725)
- Searches for 2023-2026 FRA decisions on residents' foreign-securities dealing (queries on fra.gov.eg, "foreign securities" + Egypt 2024/2025/2026, Decision 37/2014 amendments) returned no decision amending or repealing Decision 37/2014; one regalert entry references a decision repealing Decision 79 of 2016, which could not be linked to Decision 37 (search result only). — [regalert.today Egypt securities topic](https://regalert.today/topics/securities/EG)
- Context: the 2012 ban was imposed after "Egyptians took losses from a sharp drop in Facebook shares," citing risks Egyptian authorities could not monitor (2012). — [Ahram Online 2012](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/43847.aspx)

#### (e) Egyptian tax and the Canada-Egypt treaty (primary text read)
- Treaty identity: "Convention between the Arab Republic of Egypt and Canada for the Avoidance of Double Taxation... signed on 30 May 1983," published in Egypt's Official Gazette No. 48 on 29/11/1984, modified by the MLI (Egypt's MLI in force 1 January 2021; Canada's 1 December 2019). The Egyptian Tax Authority's synthesised text is "only a guiding text"; the authentic texts prevail. Canada's record: in force 2 October 1984 (Canada Treaty Series E3-1984/31). — [ETA synthesised text PDF](https://eta.gov.eg/sites/default/files/2024-09/canda.pdf); [Publications Canada](https://publications.gc.ca/site/eng/9.823669/publication.html)
- Article 4(1): "resident of a Contracting State" means any person "liable to tax therein by reason of his domicile, residence, place of management or any other criterion of a similar nature." Article 4(2) tie-breaker for individuals: (a) permanent home; if in both, "the State with which his personal and economic relations are closer (centre of vital interests)"; (b) habitual abode; (c) nationality; (d) mutual agreement. — [ETA synthesised text PDF](https://eta.gov.eg/sites/default/files/2024-09/canda.pdf)
- Article 10(2): source-state tax on dividends "shall not exceed 15 per cent of the gross amount of the dividends" when the recipient is the beneficial owner. Article 10(3): "dividends paid by a company which is a resident of Egypt to an individual who is a resident of Canada may in Egypt be subject to the general income tax levied on net total income. However, the general income tax thus imposed shall in no case exceed an average of 20 percent of the net dividends payable to such individual." — [ETA synthesised text PDF](https://eta.gov.eg/sites/default/files/2024-09/canda.pdf)
- Article 11(2): interest withholding capped at 15%. — [ETA synthesised text PDF](https://eta.gov.eg/sites/default/files/2024-09/canda.pdf)
- Article 13 (capital gains) has four paragraphs only: (1) immovable property may be taxed where situated; (2) movable property of a permanent establishment or fixed base; (3) ships/aircraft taxable only in the alienator's state; (4) replaced by MLI Article 9(4): shares or comparable interests deriving more than 50% of value from immovable property in the other state may be taxed there. There is no residual paragraph reserving gains on other property (such as ETF units or ordinary shares) to the residence state. — [ETA synthesised text PDF](https://eta.gov.eg/sites/default/files/2024-09/canda.pdf)
- Article 22 (elimination of double taxation): (1)(a) Canada deducts "tax payable in Egypt on profits, income or gains arising in Egypt" from Canadian tax, subject to Canadian law; (4) Egypt allows "a deduction from the tax on the income of that person an amount equal to the tax paid in Canada," limited to the Egyptian tax attributable to the Canadian income; (5) income taxed in the other state "in accordance with this Convention shall be deemed to arise from sources in that other State." — [ETA synthesised text PDF](https://eta.gov.eg/sites/default/files/2024-09/canda.pdf)
- Deloitte, "Egypt Highlights 2025": individuals are resident if present in Egypt "for more than 183 days in a fiscal year," deemed to have "a permanent abode in Egypt," or are Egyptian nationals employed abroad but paid from an Egyptian source. "Basis: Resident individuals are taxable on their worldwide income if Egypt is the 'center of their commercial interests.' Nonresident individuals are taxed only on their Egyptian-source income." Individual capital gains rate "10%/Progressive rates up to 27.5%"; EGX-listed shares 10% in a separate pool for residents (pre-July 2026); "Capital gains realized by nonresident individuals from the sale of shares listed on the Egyptian stock exchange or from the disposal of treasury bills are not subject to tax"; unlisted Egyptian shares at progressive rates up to 27.5% for residents and non-residents. Dividends: 10% WHT, 5% if the payer is EGX-listed, for residents and non-residents; distributions from Capital Market Law investment funds to resident natural persons 5%. — [Deloitte Egypt Highlights 2025 PDF](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-shared/docs/services/tax/2025/dttl-tax-egypthighlights-2025.pdf)
- PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Egypt individual (last reviewed 17 August 2026): residents are taxed on foreign income when the individual's "centre of commercial, industrial, or professional activities is in Egypt" (the page does not define the test); "Capital gains realised from foreign securities that fall within the scope of Egyptian taxation are subject to the applicable individual income tax rates"; the page is silent on foreign (non-Egyptian) dividends; residence tests: permanent home in Egypt, or more than 183 days continuous or intermittent within 12 months "taking into consideration the double taxation treaties." — [PwC income determination](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/individual/income-determination); [PwC residence](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/individual/residence)
- Law No. 30 of 2023 (amending Income Tax Law 91/2005): per an EY tax alert, Egypt must be the individual's "centre of commercial or industrial activities" for foreign-source income to be taxable, and "the executive regulations defined the centre of commercial or industrial activities to be the seat where the individual's decisions necessary for conducting one's activities take place and where one's vital interests are located" (search snippet of the EY alert; the alert itself and the PwC Law 30/2023 page returned 403 and were not read in full) (secondary). — [EY alert](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/egypt-introduces-amendments-to-the-income-tax-law); [PwC Law 30/2023 (403)](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/services/tax/me-tax-legal-news/2023/egypt-law-no-30-of-2023-issued-by-the-egyptian-government.html)
- Second Tax Facilitation Package, Laws 148-155 of 2026: Laws 148-154 published 28 July 2026 and effective 29 July 2026; Law 155 published 2 August 2026, effective 3 August 2026; "Certain provisions remain subject to executive regulations." — [PwC alert, 8 Aug 2026](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/services/tax/middle-east-tax-news-alerts/2026/egypt-second-tax-facilitation-package.html)
- PwC Egypt corporate summary (last reviewed 17 August 2026), on Law 151/2026: "Capital gains realised from the disposal of securities listed on the EGX are not subject to capital gains tax," effective 29 July 2026; non-residents' gains on "non-governmental securities listed on the EGX" are exempt; the exemption does not apply where the disposal results in delisting; unlisted shares remain at 22.5% (corporate); resident companies' gains on "shares invested abroad" are taxed at 22.5% with a foreign tax credit. Stamp duty on EGX-listed securities was amended by Law 153/2026 (stamp tax still applies to listed-share trades). — [PwC corporate income determination](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/corporate/income-determination); [Deloitte on listed-share gains](https://www.deloitte.com/middle-east/en/services/tax/perspectives/capital-gains-on-listed-shares-in-egypt.html)
- Regfollower reports Law 151/2026 also waives unpaid capital gains tax on EGX-listed share disposals from 16 June 2023 to 29 July 2026 (secondary). — [Regfollower](https://regfollower.com/egypt-updates-income-tax-law-with-capital-gains-listing-changes/)

### Inferences
- Decision table, dated 2026-11-02 (each row names its basis):

| Question | Scenario A: Canadian factual resident with Egyptian family ties, no permanent home in Egypt, under 183 days/yr in Egypt | Scenario B: 183+ days in Egypt in a 12-month period, or a permanent home in Egypt |
|---|---|---|
| Egyptian tax residence | Not triggered (PwC/Deloitte tests). | Triggered under domestic law. If also Canadian factual resident, treaty Art 4(2) decides: permanent home, then centre of vital interests, then habitual abode, then nationality. |
| Egyptian tax on Canadian-held ETF dividends and gains | Non-resident: Egyptian-source income only (Deloitte 2025). Foreign ETF income is outside scope. | Only if Egypt is the "centre of commercial, industrial or professional activities" (Law 91/2005 as amended by Law 30/2023). A salaried or retired person whose decisions and vital interests are in Canada is likely outside scope, but the test is undefined on the regulator-facing pages found; if in scope, gains at progressive rates up to 27.5% with Canadian tax credited under Art 22(4). Treatment of foreign dividends is not stated anywhere found. |
| Canadian tax | Worldwide income; TFSA/RRSP/FHSA unaffected. | If treaty tie-breaker lands in Egypt, CRA treats the person as a deemed non-resident (round 1), with the usual departure consequences for non-registered holdings (not researched here). |
| Holding EGX-listed shares or the EGX30 ETF | Egypt: 5% dividend WHT on listed payers (domestic law, below the 15% treaty cap); Art 10(3) permits Egypt's general income tax up to an average 20% of net dividends; capital gains exempt for non-residents (Deloitte 2025) and, from 29 July 2026, for everyone on listed securities (Law 151/2026), stamp duty aside. Canada taxes with FTC. | Same Egyptian treatment as a resident: 5% WHT on listed dividends; listed gains exempt from 29 July 2026; stamp duty applies. |
| Market access from Egypt | n/a (Canadian broker). | IBKR lists Egypt as an available country; Thndr US market via ADGM entity; EFG Hermes ONE via Bahrain entity. Local FRA-licensed brokers may not deal in foreign unlisted securities for residents (Decision 37/2014, no reversal found). |
| Moving USD out | n/a | No cap since June 2017; banks apply AML source-of-funds checks; card/cash limits (US$10k) are travel-related, not transfer limits. No written rule specific to outward portfolio investment found. |

- Because treaty Article 13 has no residual "other property" clause, Egypt is not treaty-barred from taxing a treaty-resident-of-Canada's gains on, say, Egyptian unlisted shares; for Canadian-listed ETFs held by a Canadian resident this is moot (no Egyptian source), but it matters in Scenario B where the domestic "centre of activities" test alone decides whether Egypt taxes foreign gains.
- For an Egypt-resident family member wanting to replicate the Canadian plan, the realistic route is an IBKR account (Egypt is on the list; entity unconfirmed) funded by bank transfer with a documented source of funds, or Thndr's US-market wallet; the EGX30 ETF is tradeable (47,599 units on 2 Nov 2026) but is a single-country fund with no published AUM, and the gold funds are open-ended products with a 3.5% amortising redemption fee rather than ETFs.

## Key question 4: Canadian advice rule (NI 31-103 business trigger) and AI-assisted personal research

### Takeaway
Registration as an adviser under NI 31-103 is triggered by "advising in securities for a business purpose" (Companion Policy 31-103CP s.1.3); the CSA's factors are activities similar to a registrant, intermediating trades, repetition/regularity/continuity, being or expecting to be remunerated, and soliciting, and none of them is met by an individual producing research with an AI tool solely for their own portfolio. The current CP (effective 2026-01-01, published 2026-01-30 per BCSC) was not text-readable this session; the business-trigger wording quoted is from the 2014 consolidation, which search snippets indicate is unchanged apart from added start-up guidance.

### Cited Findings
- Companion Policy 31-103CP, s.1.3 "Fundamental concepts," "Business trigger for trading and advising" (consolidated to 22 September 2014, FCNB copy): "We refer to trading or advising in securities for a business purpose as the 'business trigger' for registration. We look at the type of activity and whether it is carried out for a business purpose to determine if an individual or firm must register." Factors: "(a) Engaging in activities similar to a registrant... Examples include promoting securities or stating in any way that the individual or firm will buy or sell securities"; "(b) Intermediating trades or acting as a market maker"; "(c) Directly or indirectly carrying on the activity with repetition, regularity or continuity... The activity does not have to be their sole or even primary endeavour"; "(d) Being, or expecting to be, remunerated or compensated... It does not matter if the individual or firm actually receives compensation or in what form"; "(e) Directly or indirectly soliciting... Contacting anyone to solicit securities transactions or to offer advice may reflect a business purpose." "This is not a complete list. We do not automatically assume that any one of these factors on its own will determine whether an individual or firm is in the business of trading or advising in securities." — [31-103CP consolidated 2014-09-22 (FCNB PDF)](https://www.fcnb.ca/sites/default/files/2020-02/31-103-CP-CONS-2014-09-22-E.pdf)
- Same CP, "Business trigger examples": professionals such as lawyers, accountants, engineers, geologists and teachers who provide advice on securities incidentally are not advising for a business purpose because they "do not regularly advise on securities," "are not compensated separately for advising on securities," "do not solicit clients on the basis of their securities advice," and "do not hold themselves out as being in the business of advising on securities." Investment fund managers are subject to a registration trigger, "not... the business trigger." — [31-103CP consolidated 2014-09-22 (FCNB PDF)](https://www.fcnb.ca/sites/default/files/2020-02/31-103-CP-CONS-2014-09-22-E.pdf)
- The adviser registration requirement applies to a person or company that "engages in, or holds itself out as engaging in, the business of advising others on investing in or buying or selling securities" (OSC 2007 request-for-comment notice on NI 31-103; search snippet). — [OSC notice 2007](https://osc.gov.on.ca/sites/default/files/pdfs/irps/rule_20070220_31-103_rfc-reg-require.pdf)
- A later CSA amendment "added guidance in section 1.3 of 31-103CP to clarify the application of the business trigger to start-up entities" (search snippet of an amendment notice; the start-up text itself not read). — [OSC amendment notice 2014](https://www.osc.ca/sites/default/files/pdfs/irps/ni_20140918_31-103CP_change-registration-requirements.pdf)
- Current versions per the BCSC index (read 2026-11-02): NI 31-103 published 2026-06-16, effective 2026-01-01; 31-103CP published 2026-01-30, effective 2026-01-01; a CP amendment advance notice published 2026-06-11 takes effect 2027-01-01. The OSC's unofficial-consolidation URL returned 404. — [BCSC 31-103 index](https://www.bcsc.bc.ca/securities-law/law-and-policy/instruments-and-policies/3-registration-requirements-related-matters/current/31-103)
- CSA public guidance (round 1): verify registration of anyone giving investment advice via AreTheyRegistered.ca. — [FCNB/CSA, June 2025](https://fcnb.ca/en/news-alerts/canadian-securities-administrators-encourage-canadians-to-look-out-for-deceptive-social-media)

### Inferences
- A Canadian who builds an AI-assisted research pipeline and uses its output only to manage their own TFSA/RRSP/taxable holdings is not "advising others" and meets none of the five CP factors (no clients, no compensation, no solicitation, no holding-out, no intermediation); no adviser or dealer registration is required under NI 31-103. Trading frequently for one's own account is "trading," but the CP's own-account context and the absence of intermediation or remuneration keep it outside the dealer business trigger.
- The line is crossed, on the CP's wording, when the research is published or sent to others in a way that "offers advice," is done "with repetition, regularity or continuity" for others, or is monetised (subscriptions, tips, referral fees), or when the person "holds [themselves] out" as advising; a public blog of general, non-tailored commentary is the grey zone the CP does not resolve, so any shared output should carry an explicit "not advice, personal research" framing and no solicitation, consistent with the round-1 no-advice posture and the data-licence "personal use" terms.

### Gaps
- The current (effective 2026-01-01) 31-103CP text was not read; section 1.3 wording is quoted from the 2014 consolidation (FCNB), and the "start-up entities" addition is known only from an amendment-notice snippet.
- No CSA staff notice specifically addressing publication of general investment research, blogs or newsletters without registration was found; the general-advice vs tailored-advice distinction is not addressed in the sections read.
- Quebec operates its own regime under the AMF; not researched.
