**Headline finding that changes the plan:** the Canada-UAE treaty (Article 4(1)(b)(i), Justice Laws consolidated text) defines a UAE-resident *individual* as "an individual who is a national of the United Arab Emirates" with substantial presence/permanent home/habitual abode there and closer ties to the UAE. A Canadian citizen on a UAE residence visa is therefore not a "resident of the UAE" for treaty purposes, so (i) the treaty tie-breaker and the s.250(5) "deemed non-resident" route do not apply, (ii) Canadian non-residency can only be achieved factually, by severing residential ties, and (iii) treaty rate reductions (15% dividends, Article 13(5) residence-only taxation of gains) are not available; Canadian-source income of a non-resident Canadian in the UAE is subject to the statutory 25% Part XIII rate.

## Key question 1: Canadian tax status while living in the UAE

### Takeaway
Whether the owner is still a Canadian tax resident turns entirely on the factual residential-ties test (Folio S5-F1-C1), because the Canada-UAE treaty's residence article covers only UAE *nationals*, so the treaty tie-breaker cannot make a Canadian citizen a deemed non-resident. If ties are severed, departure tax applies to non-registered holdings at the departure date (TFSA, RRSP, Canadian real property excluded; T1161 above $25,000 FMV; T1244 deferral), the TFSA can be kept (no new room, 1%/month tax on any contribution while non-resident), the RRSP can be kept (withdrawals as a non-resident withheld at 25%, no treaty reduction for the UAE), and on return the CRA deems property to be reacquired at FMV on the arrival date with an optional unwinding of the departure-tax deemed disposition for property still held.

### Cited Findings

#### Residence tests (CRA, primary)
- Income Tax Folio S5-F1-C1 (effective 26 Nov 2015; page updated 2026-10-30): ¶1.10 the key test for a person leaving Canada is "whether the individual maintains residential ties with Canada while abroad"; ¶1.11 the ties that "will almost always be significant residential ties" are the dwelling place, spouse or common-law partner, and dependants; ¶1.14 "it would be unusual for a single secondary residential tie with Canada to be sufficient on its own"; ¶1.17 "there is no particular length of stay abroad that necessarily results in an individual becoming a non-resident"; ¶1.22 non-residency generally begins on "the date on which the individual severs all residential ties with Canada"; ¶1.32 a non-factual-resident who sojourns 183 days or more in a calendar year "is deemed to be resident in Canada for the entire year"; ¶1.37-1.38 s.250(5) applies "if the tie-breaker rules in a tax treaty" place the individual in the other country, and then the individual "will be deemed to be a non-resident of Canada for all purposes of the Act"; ¶1.54-1.55 Form NR73 is something a departing taxpayer "should consider completing"; the CRA opinion "is not binding on the CRA" and is "based entirely on the facts provided by the taxpayer". — [CRA Folio S5-F1-C1](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-5-international-residency/folio-1-residency/income-tax-folio-s5-f1-c1-determining-individual-s-residence-status.html)
- NR73 "Determination of Residency Status (leaving Canada)" page (updated 2025-04-24): "Complete this form if you have left or are planning to leave Canada temporarily or permanently"; the page does not state that filing is mandatory. — [CRA NR73](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/nr73.html)

#### Canada-UAE treaty (primary text, Justice Laws schedule and Finance Canada reference copy)
- Identity and status: Convention signed at Abu Dhabi 9 June 2002 (Protocol same day); implemented by the Canada-United Arab Emirates Tax Convention Act, 2002 (S.C. 2002, c. 24, s. 4), "current to 2026-10-19 and last amended on 2004-07-10"; the Act's footnote records the Convention in force 25 May 2004 (Canada Gazette Part I, Vol. 138, p. 1918) (search-index quote of the Act's note). Finance Canada's electronic copy "is provided for convenience of reference only and has no official sanction". — [Justice Laws C-10.55](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-10.55/page-2.html); [Finance Canada reference copy](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/tax-policy/tax-treaties/country/united-arab-emirates-convention-2002.html)
- Article 4(1)(a) (Canada): a person "liable to tax in Canada by reason of the person's domicile, residence, place of management" or similar, excluding persons taxed only on Canadian-source income. Article 4(1)(b)(i) (UAE, individuals), verbatim: "an individual who is a national of the United Arab Emirates, provided that the individual has a substantial presence, permanent home or habitual abode in the United Arab Emirates and that individual's personal and economic relations are closer to the United Arab Emirates than to any other State". Article 4(3) tie-breaker: permanent home, then centre of vital interests, then habitual abode, then nationality, then mutual agreement, and it applies only "Where by reason of the provisions of paragraph 1 an individual is a resident of both Contracting States". — [Justice Laws C-10.55 p.2](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-10.55/page-2.html)
- Article 10(2): dividends capped at 5% (company beneficial owner controlling 10%+ of votes), 10% (NRO investment corporation case), and "15 per cent of the gross amount of the dividends in all other cases". Article 11: interest capped at 10%. — [Finance Canada reference copy](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/tax-policy/tax-treaties/country/united-arab-emirates-convention-2002.html)
- Article 13(5): "Gains from the alienation of any property, other than that referred to in paragraphs 1, 2, 3 and 4, shall be taxable only in the Contracting State of which the alienator is a resident." Article 13(6): paragraph 5 does not affect either State's right to tax gains of an individual "who is a resident of the other Contracting State and has been a resident of the first-mentioned Contracting State at any time during the five years immediately preceding the alienation of the property". Protocol ¶8: an individual taxed in Canada on a deemed disposition may elect to be treated as having sold and repurchased the property at FMV for UAE purposes. Article 22(1) (Other income): items of income of a resident "wherever arising, not dealt with in the foregoing Articles... shall be taxable only in that State". Article 24(1)(a): Canada credits UAE tax on UAE-source income subject to Canadian law; Article 24(2): UAE relief follows UAE domestic law. — [Justice Laws C-10.55 p.2](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-10.55/page-2.html); [Finance Canada reference copy](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/tax-policy/tax-treaties/country/united-arab-emirates-convention-2002.html)

#### Departure tax and forms (CRA, primary)
- "Leaving Canada (emigrants)" (page date 2026-09-17): on emigration you are "considered to have sold certain types of property (even if you have not sold them) at their fair market value (FMV)" and to have reacquired them at that amount; examples given are shares, jewellery, paintings, collections; T1161 required if total FMV of all property owned on departure exceeds $25,000; a TFSA can be kept and continues to benefit from the Canadian tax exemption, but "You cannot contribute while you are a non-resident, and your contribution room does not increase". — [CRA Leaving Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/leaving-canada-emigrants.html)
- "Dispositions of property for emigrants of Canada" (page date 2026-01-20): excepted from the deemed disposition are Canadian real or immovable property, Canadian resource and timber resource property, Canadian business property of a permanent establishment, "excluded rights or interests" under s.128.1(10) (the page lists RRSPs, RESPs, RDSPs, TFSAs and pension-type arrangements; FHSAs are not mentioned), employee security options, and property of short-term residents (resident "60 months or less" in the preceding 10 years). T1243 computes the deemed gain (to Schedule 3); T1244 elects under s.220(4.5) to defer the departure tax without interest until actual disposition, deadline "April 30 of the year after you emigrate", security required if federal tax owing exceeds $16,500. T1161 exclusions: cash and bank deposits, registered and pension-type plans, short-term-resident property that is not TCP, personal-use property under $10,000 FMV; late-filing penalty $25/day, minimum $100, maximum $2,500. On returning: if you ceased residency after 1 October 1996, re-establish residency and still own the property, you can elect to reduce the gain reported on departure (for non-TCP, by the least of the gain reported, the FMV on the return date, or another specified amount) by written request by the filing due date for the return year; "Previously deferred tax may become payable when you immigrate." — [CRA Dispositions for emigrants](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/dispositions-property.html)

#### TFSA and RRSP as a non-resident (CRA, primary)
- "If you owe tax on non-resident TFSA contributions" (page date 2025-10-14): non-resident contributions "are taxed in Canada at a rate of 1% per month" for as long as the contribution remains; the tax stops in "The month you withdraw the entire non-resident contribution" or "The month you become a resident of Canada again"; "Withdrawing only part of the non-resident contribution does not reduce your tax owing"; reported on RC243 with Schedule B. — [CRA TFSA non-resident tax](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/tax-free-savings-account/owing-tax/non-resident.html)
- "Tax rates on withdrawals" (RRSP; page date 2026-01-29): residents 10%/20%/30% by amount; for non-residents "withholding is 25% unless reduced by a treaty". — [CRA RRSP withdrawal rates](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsps-related-plans/making-withdrawals/tax-rates-on-withdrawals.html)
- Advisor/brokerage summaries (secondary, consistent with the CRA pages): TFSA room does not accrue for years in which the holder is a non-resident; withdrawals while non-resident are re-added to room but cannot be used until residency resumes; income and withdrawals remain tax-free in Canada. — [Sun Life GI](https://www.sunlifeglobalinvestments.com/en/resources/trust-gics-advisor-resources/tfsa-held-by-non-residents-max/) (secondary); [Advisor.ca](https://www.advisor.ca/tax/tax-news/the-cost-of-tfsa-contributions-for-non-residents/) (secondary)

#### Part XIII on Canadian-source income (CRA, primary)
- "Rates for Part XIII tax" (page date 2025-11-14): "Non-residents have to pay a 25% tax on amounts that are taxable under Part XIII"; the rate can be reduced or exempted under the Act or a bilateral treaty; treaties are renegotiated so check the current rate. — [CRA Part XIII rates](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/payments-non-residents/nr4-part-xiii-tax/part-xiii-withholding-tax/rates-part-xiii-tax.html)
- IC76-12R8 (dated 12 Jan 2022, law as of 1 Jan 2022; page date 2022-10-26): "Although Part XIII generally specifies a withholding tax rate of 25%, there are some exceptions"; payers must withhold 25% unless they obtain certification of treaty eligibility; the circular's web page carries no per-country table and refers readers to the treaty text and the Part XIII calculator. — [CRA IC76-12](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/ic76-12/applicable-rate-part-xiii-tax-on-amounts-paid-credited-persons-countries-which-canada-a-tax-convention.html)
- Non-resident investments in Canadian mutual funds: non-residents "may be assessed non-resident withholding tax" (index page, 2016-12-22); Part XIII.2 taxes "assessable distributions" from "Canadian property mutual funds" (exchange-listed funds deriving more than 50% of value from Canadian real/resource property) at 15% (page date 2025-11-14); capital-gains distributions are taxable to non-residents only to the extent they derive from taxable Canadian property and only if more than 5% of the fund's capital-gains distributions go to non-residents (page date 2025-05-20). None of these CRA pages states the treatment of ordinary income distributions of a broad-equity mutual fund trust. — [CRA mutual funds index](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/information-been-moved/non-resident-s-investments-canadian-mutual-funds.html); [Assessable distributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/information-been-moved/non-resident-s-investments-canadian-mutual-funds/assessable-distributions.html); [TCP gains distributions](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/information-been-moved/non-resident-s-investments-canadian-mutual-funds/taxable-canadian-property-gains-distributions.html)
- RBC Wealth Management summary (secondary): capital-gains distributions, capital-gains dividends and return of capital from Canadian mutual funds are generally exempt from non-resident withholding; ordinary income distributed by a mutual fund trust "is simply considered trust income subject to a 25% withholding tax rate unless reduced by a tax treaty". — [RBC WM blog](https://wmc.rbcwealthmanagement.com/aaron-fennell/blog/canadian-non-resident-withholding-tax-3833656252557770559) (secondary)

#### Re-entry
- "Completing your return for newcomers" (page date 2026-09-17): for property owned on arrival "the CRA considers you to have sold the property and to have immediately reacquired it... at a cost equal to the fair market value (FMV) on the date that you became a resident of Canada"; "The FMV will be your cost when you calculate your gain or loss from disposing of the property in the future". — [CRA newcomers return](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/newcomers-canada-immigrants/completing-return-newcomers.html)
- T1135 first-year exemption: tax-advisory sources cite s.233.7 ITA, under which individuals (not trusts) need not file T1135 for the year in which they first become resident (secondary; statute not read). — [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en-ca/expert-insights/canadian-t1135-reporting-for-part-year-residents) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Because Article 4(1)(b)(i) requires UAE nationality, a Canadian citizen living in the UAE cannot be "a resident of both Contracting States" under Article 4(1), so Article 4(3) and s.250(5) never engage. The owner is either a factual resident of Canada (worldwide taxation continues, TFSA/RRSP unaffected, the original XEQT plan stands) or a factual non-resident (all significant ties severed: no Canadian dwelling available, no spouse/dependants in Canada). There is no treaty "safe harbour" for a Canadian in the UAE; the Egypt-style tie-breaker analysis in round 2 does not transfer.
- The same nationality clause means the owner is not a treaty-protected UAE resident for Part XIII purposes: Canadian-source dividends and trust-income distributions paid to them as a non-resident are withheld at the statutory 25%, not the 15% treaty cap (the 15% figure in secondary aggregators assumes a UAE-national recipient). This directly penalises holding XEQT (see Q4(c)).
- Article 13(6) is moot for a Canadian in the UAE (it only matters when the treaty assigns the person to the UAE), but Canada's domestic departure tax still crystallises gains on non-registered holdings at departure; with the T1244 deferral, tax is payable only on actual disposition.
- Practical sequence if the owner is (or becomes) a factual non-resident: file the departure-year return with T1161 (if FMV > $25,000 across all property including the Sarwa and IBKR holdings) and T1243; consider T1244 to defer; stop all TFSA contributions (1%/month tax); leave the RRSP untouched (25% non-treaty withholding on any withdrawal); tell IBKR and any Canadian broker the new residence so Part XIII withholding and NR4 reporting are correct.
- NR73 is optional; given that a CRA opinion is non-binding and the facts (no treaty tie-breaker) are clear-cut, the owner may prefer to document ties severed rather than request an opinion, but this is a judgement call for a cross-border accountant.

### Gaps

## Key question 2: UAE side

### Takeaway
The UAE levies no personal income tax, no capital-gains tax on individuals, and imposes no individual tax registration or reporting of foreign investments (PwC, reviewed 9 Sep 2026); domestic tax residency under Cabinet Decision 85/2022 (183-day, 90-day or centre-of-interests tests, effective 1 March 2023) matters only for obtaining a tax residency certificate and has no effect on the Canadian analysis because the Canada-UAE treaty's residence article is limited to UAE nationals.

### Cited Findings
- PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, UAE individual taxes on personal income (last reviewed 9 September 2026): "There is currently no personal income tax in the United Arab Emirates"; "There is no federal or Emirate-level personal income tax"; "As such, there are no individual tax registration or reporting obligations"; natural persons conducting a business with turnover above AED 1 million are subject to 9% corporate tax, but "wages, personal investment income, and real estate investment income" are excluded from that turnover test. The page does not mention capital gains tax (there is none to describe). — [PwC UAE personal income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/united-arab-emirates/individual/taxes-on-personal-income)
- PwC UAE individual residence (last reviewed 9 September 2026): Cabinet Decision No. 85 of 2022, effective 1 March 2023; a natural person is UAE tax resident if their "usual or primary place of residence and... centre of financial and personal interests" is in the UAE; or physically present "183 days or more during a consecutive 12-month period"; or present 90+ days in 12 months and a UAE/GCC national or holder of a valid UAE residence permit with a permanent place of residence or employment/business in the UAE. — [PwC UAE residence](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/united-arab-emirates/individual/residence)
- IRS "United States income tax treaties - A to Z" (page updated 3 Jan 2026): the United Arab Emirates is not on the list (U entries: Ukraine, USSR, United Kingdom, United States Model, Uzbekistan). — [IRS treaty list](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z)

### Inferences
- A Canadian citizen in the UAE who is a factual non-resident of Canada pays no tax anywhere on investment income and gains from foreign (non-Canadian) ETFs; the only taxes that bite are withholding at source inside or on the fund (US 30% on direct US-ETF distributions, 15% US-to-Irish-fund withholding inside UCITS funds, foreign L1 inside any fund) and Canadian Part XIII on Canadian-source distributions.
- UAE domestic tax residency (Decision 85/2022) does not create Canadian consequences: it neither triggers the treaty (nationality clause) nor creates UAE reporting. A UAE tax residency certificate is useful only vis-a-vis third countries with which the UAE has treaties that do not carry a nationality restriction; it is irrelevant for the US (no treaty) and for Canada (nationality clause).

### Gaps
- No UAE government (Federal Tax Authority) primary page was read; the PwC pages are a Big Four summary as requested. The text of Cabinet Decision 85/2022 was not retrieved.
- Whether any UAE free-zone or visa rule requires disclosure of foreign brokerage holdings (e.g., for Golden Visa financial criteria) was not researched; PwC states there are no tax reporting obligations.

### Inferences
- For a US$2,500 monthly buy of an LSE USD-line UCITS ETF, IBKR Tiered pricing costs about USD 1.70 (0.068%) per order versus USD 4.00 (0.16%) on Fixed; over a year that is roughly USD 20 vs USD 48. ## Key question 6: Re-entry plan and 2026 context

### Takeaway
On re-establishing Canadian residency, UCITS holdings are deemed reacquired at FMV on the arrival date (no latent gain carried in), become "specified foreign property" for T1135 once total cost exceeds C$100,000 (first-year filing exemption for individuals), and their income is reported as foreign income without T3/T5 slips (accumulating funds produce no annual income to report; distributing funds' dividends are foreign non-business income with no dividend tax credit); PFIC is irrelevant (not a US person). Whether to switch to XEQT on return depends on account: inside a TFSA/RRSP, XEQT (or XEF/XIC/XUU) is better because UCITS funds cannot be held in Canadian registered accounts on Canadian platforms and the look-through FWT is similar; in a taxable account, VWRA's accumulation defers tax and a switch would itself be a disposition (tax-free only if done before arrival). No 2026 CRA or Budget proposal targeting Canadian expats in the Gulf was found.

### Cited Findings
- CRA newcomers return page (2026-09-17): property owned on arrival is deemed sold and reacquired "at a cost equal to the fair market value (FMV) on the date that you became a resident of Canada"; "The FMV will be your cost when you calculate your gain or loss from disposing of the property in the future". — [CRA newcomers return](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/newcomers-canada-immigrants/completing-return-newcomers.html)
- CRA T1135 Q&A (round 1): T1135 required when total cost of specified foreign property exceeds $100,000 at any time in the year; shares of non-resident corporations are specified foreign property; property in an RRSP or TFSA is excluded; Canadian mutual fund trusts are not specified foreign property. — [CRA T1135 Q&A](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/information-been-moved/foreign-reporting/questions-answers-about-form-t1135.html)
- First-year T1135 exemption for individuals (s.233.7 ITA) per tax-advisory sources (secondary). — [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en-ca/expert-insights/canadian-t1135-reporting-for-part-year-residents) (secondary)
- Dispositions page (2026-01-20): on return, an election can unwind the departure deemed disposition for property still held; deferred departure tax may become payable on immigration. — [CRA Dispositions for emigrants](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/dispositions-property.html)
- Searches for 2026 CRA or Budget measures aimed at Canadian non-residents in the Gulf, departure-tax changes, or treaty renegotiation with the UAE returned only general guides; a 2026 blog notes the proposed two-thirds capital-gains inclusion rate was cancelled and the rate remains one-half (secondary). — [Motley Fool CA, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.fool.ca/2026/07/02/the-exit-tax-exposing-the-cras-penalty-for-canadians-moving-abroad/) (secondary); [MoneySense expat guide, 1 Apr 2025](https://www.moneysense.ca/save/taxes/canadian-expat-tax-guide-if-i-work-abroad-where-do-i-pay-taxes-and-other-questions-answered/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Because an Irish UCITS ETF is a share of a non-resident corporation (an Irish plc/ICAV), it is specified foreign property; a VWRA position whose deemed cost on arrival exceeds C$100,000 triggers T1135 from the second year of residency. The deemed-FMV cost base also means the C$100,000 test is measured against arrival-date value, so a US$75,000+ position will cross it after a few years of growth or contributions.
- Canadian taxation of VWRA in a taxable account after return: no annual income inclusion for accumulating funds (Canada has no "notional distribution" rule for foreign accumulating ETFs in the way the UK or Germany do); gains are taxed on sale at the inclusion rate; there is no PFIC-style regime. Distributing VWRD pays foreign dividends taxed as ordinary income with a foreign tax credit only for tax actually withheld to the holder (none, since Ireland withholds nothing), so the 15% US withholding inside the fund is permanently lost either way; this is the same structural loss a Canadian-listed wrap fund suffers, so VWRA is not worse than XEQT on FWT in a taxable account, and it is better on MER (0.14% vs 0.19%).
- Decision on return: (1) do nothing to the taxable VWRA position (switching crystallises a gain for no FWT benefit); (2) direct new TFSA/RRSP/FHSA contributions to XEQT or the XEF/XIC/XUU trio on a Canadian platform (UCITS funds are generally not available on Canadian discount brokers, not verified this round); (3) if a Canadian broker cannot hold LSE-listed VWRA, keep the IBKR account open as a Canadian resident (IBKR Canada can carry LSE lines) rather than selling. If the owner expects to return within a year or two, selling VWRA before re-entry is pointless (the arrival-date FMV step-up already wipes the gain).
- Transition out of IBKR LLC to IBKR Canada on return: IBKR typically migrates accounts between entities on a change of residence; the mechanics were not researched.
