## R6. What are the most-used open-source trading bot frameworks today, and what do their maintainers actually claim?

### Takeaway
The high-star "bot" ecosystem splits into (a) crypto-only execution bots (freqtrade, hummingbot, OctoBot, Jesse, passivbot, Superalgos) whose own docs say "educational purposes only / always start in dry-run / it will always be a gamble", (b) multi-asset institutional-style engines (QuantConnect Lean, NautilusTrader, vn.py) that can in principle touch US/Canadian ETFs through Interactive Brokers or Alpaca, and (c) a 2025-2026 wave of LLM "agent trading" repos (TradingAgents ~120k stars, ai-hedge-fund 64k, Vibe-Trading 37k, QuantDinger 13k, CloddsBot 3k) that almost uniformly disclaim being "for research/educational purposes only" and make no verifiable live-profit claims. No maintainer of any repo inspected claims the software is profitable; the closest is freqtrade's FAQ: running it "will always be a gamble."

### Inferences
- Of the 14 named frameworks, only Lean, NautilusTrader, vn.py (via IB), TradingAgents (via Yahoo tickers, simulated exchange only), Vibe-Trading (Alpaca/IBKR, TSX listed), QuantDinger (IBKR/Alpaca) and ALTA can touch US/Canadian ETFs at all; freqtrade, hummingbot, OctoBot, Jesse, passivbot, Superalgos, CloddsBot and intelligent-trading-bot are crypto/prediction-market only. The "most used" bots by stars are therefore structurally irrelevant to a Canadian ETF investor's asset class.
- Maintainer language is consistent: nobody in the set claims live profitability; the bots that have been around longest (freqtrade, OctoBot) use the strongest "educational only / gamble" wording, while the newest LLM repos (CloddsBot, hummingbot) have the weakest or no disclaimers.
- The star explosion of 2025-26 is in LLM-agent *research scaffolds* (TradingAgents, ai-hedge-fund, Vibe-Trading) whose own READMEs position them as research/education; the only quantitative edge claim in that family is a 3-month, 3-stock paper table.

## R7. What is the evidence that bots and strategies decay, and how did LLM trading agents fare in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
Three independent evidence streams converge: (1) backtest overfitting is near-certain when many configurations are tried and is rarely reported (Bailey-Borwein-López de Prado-Zhu; Harvey-Liu-Zhu's t>3 hurdle), (2) published anomalies lose roughly 26% out-of-sample and 58% post-publication (McLean-Pontiff), with newer factors decaying faster (Falck-Rej-Thesmar), and (3) retail active traders lose at ~85-93% rates in every large administrative dataset (Taiwan, Brazil, India), while BIS finds >80% of simulated retail bitcoin DCA buyers lost money by end-2022. The 2025-26 LLM-agent record is similarly thin: in StockBench the best agent made 1.9% vs 0.4% buy-and-hold over four months, all agents underperformed in the Jan-Apr 2025 downturn, and in nof1's real-money Alpha Arena four of six models lost money with GPT-5 down >62%.

### Cited Findings

**Backtest overfitting and selection bias**
- Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu, "Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism" (Notices of the AMS, May 2014): a backtest can be made to look strong after trying only a modest number of configurations; because the number of configurations tried is rarely reported, investors cannot evaluate the degree of overfitting in most proposals; the authors say this likely explains why many strategies that look good on paper fail — [WMU scholarworks copy](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/40/); [Mathematical Investor blog](https://mathinvestor.org/?p=1632).
- Bailey & López de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio" (Journal of Portfolio Management 40(5), 2014, pp. 94-107): corrects Sharpe for selection bias under multiple testing and for non-normal returns; acts as a hurdle test comparing the observed SR against the best SR expected from luck across N trials — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551).
- "Probability of Backtest Overfitting" (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu; working paper revised Nov 2014): rank-based estimate of the probability that the configuration selected in-sample underperforms out-of-sample; requires the full trial-by-trial record — [carmamaths copy](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest2.pdf); [pbo R vignette](https://cran.hafro.is/web/packages/pbo/vignettes/pbo.html). Note: QuantDinger ships both PBO and DSR in its strategy-evolution reports — [QuantDinger README](https://github.com/OpenByteInc/QuantDinger).
- Harvey, Liu, Zhu, "...and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns" (NBER WP 20592, 2014; RFS 2016): because hundreds of factors have been tested, a newly discovered factor should clear a t-statistic above 3.0 rather than 2.0; authors conclude most claimed findings in financial economics are likely false — [NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592).
- López de Prado, "The 10 Reasons Most Machine Learning Funds Fail" (SSRN 3031282, Dec 2017; JPM 2018): names the "Sisyphus paradigm", backtest overfitting, and "take some data, run it through an ML algorithm, backtest the predictions, and repeat the sequence until a nice-looking backtest shows up" as core failure modes — [GARP white paper](https://www.garp.org/white-paper/the-10-reasons-most-machine-learning-funds-fail); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/why-machine-learning-funds-fail/).
- QuantConnect's own research guide: "Overfitting occurs when you fine-tune the parameters of an algorithm to fit the detail and noise of backtesting data"; recommends a central cause-and-effect hypothesis, no more than "8-32 hours" exploring one idea, walk-forward optimization and live paper trading; for best-performing factors out-of-sample "will almost always underperform the in-sample period"; lists look-ahead bias, survivorship bias and ignored short availability as reasons backtests fail live — [QC research guide](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/key-concepts/research-guide).

**Backtest-vs-live gap (from the bot docs themselves)**
- freqtrade backtesting assumptions: "All orders are filled at the requested price (no slippage) as long as the price is within the candle's high/low range"; exchange limits/precision use *current* values because historic ones are unavailable; stoploss evaluated before ROI within a candle so backtests can show more stoploss exits than live; "One big limitation of backtesting" is not knowing intra-candle price paths; backtesting "will never replace running a strategy in dry-run mode", "past results don't guarantee future success", only "forward-testing (dry-mode) can really confirm a strategy"; no slippage parameter exists, only `--fee` — [freqtrade backtesting docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/).
- freqtrade hyperopt docs' only overfitting warning: parameter values more precise than 3 decimals "will usually result in overfitted results"; no out-of-sample validation guidance is given — [freqtrade hyperopt docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/hyperopt/).
- freqtrade strategy docs list look-ahead leaks to avoid (negative `shift`, `.iloc[-1]`, whole-column aggregates, left-labelled resampling) and warn that a clean `lookahead-analysis` result "doesn't guarantee the strategy is free of these errors" — [freqtrade strategy customization](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/strategy-customization/).
- NautilusTrader README: "Live execution can differ from backtests because of venue, transport, timing, persistence, and reconciliation behavior" — [NautilusTrader README](https://github.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader).
- QC reality models default to "highly liquid assets", so illiquid or high-volume strategies need custom fee/fill/slippage models — [QC reality modeling](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/reality-modeling/key-concepts).

**Alpha decay, crowding, regime change**
- McLean & Pontiff, "Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?" (Journal of Finance 71(1), Feb 2016, pp. 5-32, DOI 10.1111/jofi.12365): across 97 predictors, out-of-sample returns are ~26% lower and post-publication returns ~58% lower than in-sample; the 32-point difference is attributed to publication-informed trading; declines are larger for predictors with larger in-sample returns and those concentrated in liquid, low-idiosyncratic-risk stocks; post-publication, predictor portfolios become more correlated with other published-predictor portfolios — [gwern PDF mirror](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf); [BC summary](https://www.bc.edu/content/bc-web/schools/carroll-school/news/2017/pontiff-paper.html); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/effects-of-market-adaptation). (SSRN and Wiley pages returned 403 in this session.)
- Falck, Rej, Thesmar, "Why and how systematic strategies decay" (arXiv 2105.01380, May 2021): publication year alone explains ~30% of cross-factor variance in Sharpe decay; "Every year, the Sharpe decay of newly-published factors increases by 5ppt"; overfitting proxies (number of operations to compute the signal, outlier sensitivity of in-sample Sharpe) add ~15%; arbitrage-capital proxies are statistically significant but marginal — [arXiv 2105.01380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01380).

**Retail active-trader outcomes (administrative data)**
- SEBI press release PR No.22/2024 (23 Sep 2024), text extracted from the PDF: "93% of over 1 crore individual F&O traders incurred average losses of around ₹2 lakh per trader (inclusive of transaction costs)" FY22-FY24; aggregate losses >₹1.8 lakh crore; "Only 1% of individual traders managed to earn profits exceeding ₹1 lakh, after adjusting for transaction costs"; proprietary traders and FPIs booked gross profits of ₹33,000 crore and ₹28,000 crore in FY24 with "97% of FPI profits and 96% of proprietary trader profits coming from algorithmic trading"; individuals spent ~₹50,000 crore on transaction costs over three years (51% brokerage, 20% exchange fees); "more than 75% of loss-making traders continued trading in F&O" despite consecutive years of losses — [SEBI PR 22/2024 PDF](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2024/1727086965496.pdf).
- SEBI follow-ups: FY25 study says ~91% of individual traders incurred net loss in equity derivatives and net losses widened 41% to ₹1,05,603 crore; an Aug-2026 study covering FY25-FY26 reports ~₹3.85 lakh crore aggregate net losses across FY22-FY26 — [SEBI PR 39/2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2025/1751900802566.pdf); [SEBI Aug-2026 study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf) (figures from search snippets; the Aug-2026 PDF was not opened).
- Chague, De-Losso, Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?" (FGV-EESP WP 525, Feb/Jun 2020): of Brazilian individuals who began day-trading mini-Ibovespa futures 2013-2015, only 1,551 (7.9%) persisted >300 sessions; of those, 17 (1.1%) earned more than minimum wage and 8 (0.5%) more than a bank teller's starting salary; top trader earned US$310/day with a US$2,560 daily standard deviation; no evidence of learning by day trading — [SSRN 3423101](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fgv:eesptd:525).
- Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean (Taiwan, 1992-2006): fewer than 1% of day traders (the ~4,000 most profitable from the prior year) earn reliably positive abnormal returns the next year; unprofitable traders with 50+ days of experience still have a 95.3% probability of day-trading again within 12 months — [Berkeley working paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/do-day-traders-make-money/). A secondary source reports 84.3% of ~360,000 day traders lost money (not verified against the primary).
- BIS Bulletin 69, "Crypto shocks and retail losses" (Cornelli, Doerr, Frost, Gambacorta, 20 Feb 2023): using download data from 200+ exchange apps in 95 countries (2015-2022), 73% of users first downloaded an app when bitcoin was above $20,000; under a simulated $100/month DCA from the download month, "over four-fifths of users would have lost money" (81%) by Dec 2022, with the median investor down $431 on $900 invested — [BIS Bulletin 69 PDF](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull69.pdf); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/crypto/about-75-of-retail-buyers-of-bitcoin-lost-money-bis-study-says).

**LLM-driven trading agents, 2025-2026**
- **StockBench** (Chen et al., arXiv 2510.02209, v1 Oct 2025, v2 Mar 2026): contamination-free benchmark; agents trade the 20 largest DJIA stocks by weight from $100k over Mar 3–Jun 30 2025 (82 trading days) with daily prices, fundamentals and news. Equal-weight buy-and-hold returned 0.4% with -15.2% max drawdown; best agent Kimi-K2 returned 1.9% with -11.8% MDD (Sortino 0.042); worst GPT-OSS-20B -2.8%; 8 of 13 models beat the 0.4% baseline but by at most ~1.5 points; in the Jan–Apr 2025 downturn **all** LLM agents underperformed passive and in the May–Aug rally all outperformed — a bullish bias; the abstract says "most models struggle to outperform the simple buy-and-hold baseline" and that static financial QA performance "does not necessarily translate into effective trading behavior" — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02209); [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v2). Note the abstract and the results table disagree on "most"; I report both.
- **Agent Market Arena / "When Agents Trade"** (Qian et al., arXiv 2510.11695, Oct 2025): a "lifelong, real-time benchmark" running four agent architectures (InvestorAgent, TradeAgent, HedgeFundAgent, DeepFundAgent) over five backbones (GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, Claude-3.5-haiku, Claude-sonnet-4, Gemini-2.0-flash) on crypto and stocks; headline finding: agent framework design drives behaviour more than backbone choice; the abstract reports no profitability numbers — [arXiv 2510.11695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11695).
- **TradeTrap** (Yan et al., arXiv 2512.02261, Dec 2025): stress-tests LLM trading agents' market intelligence, strategy, portfolio/ledger and execution components under controlled perturbations in closed-loop US-equity backtests; "small perturbations at a single component can propagate through the agent decision loop", producing extreme concentration, runaway exposure and large drawdowns; concludes current autonomous trading agents "can be systematically misled at the system level" — [arXiv 2512.02261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02261).
- **TrustTrade** (arXiv 2603.22567, Mar 2026) proposes selective consensus to reduce decision instability in LLM trading agents — evidence that run-to-run non-determinism is a recognized failure mode — [arXiv 2603.22567](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22567v1).
- **nof1 Alpha Arena Season 1** (real money, crypto perps, Oct 18–Nov 3 2025, $10,000 per model): Qwen3-Max finished +22.32%, DeepSeek also positive, the other four of six models lost, GPT-5 lost >62%; nof1 itself called the season exploratory, citing short duration, modest capital and no statistical controls; Season 1.5 was reportedly won by Grok 4.2 (Dec 2025) — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3329784/deepseek-outperforms-ai-rivals-real-money-real-market-crypto-showdown); [Forklog S1](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/); [Forklog S1.5](https://forklog.com/en/ai-model-grok-4-2-triumphs-in-trading-tournament/). nof1.ai's site describes itself as an "AI research lab" and makes an unsupported "proto scaling law for financial intelligence" claim — [nof1.ai](https://nof1.ai/). alphaarena.ai returned HTTP 429 in this session so the official leaderboard was not read.
- TradingAgents' own paper admits ~11 LLM calls and 20+ tool calls per decision limited backtests to 3 months — i.e., the agents are expensive to evaluate, which is itself why long-horizon evidence is scarce — [arXiv 2412.20138v7](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138v7).

### Inferences
- "Works for a month, breaks the next" has three documented mechanisms, none of which a retail bot can fix: (i) the parameter set was selected from many trials on one regime (PBO/DSR, López de Prado), (ii) the regime itself shifts — StockBench shows the same agents flipping from last to first between a downturn and a rally, and (iii) any genuine edge is crowded away (McLean-Pontiff 58%, Falck et al. +5ppt decay per publication year).
- The bots' own backtesters are structurally optimistic: freqtrade assumes zero slippage and current exchange limits; QC defaults assume high liquidity. The documented backtest-to-live gap is therefore a floor, not a tail risk.
- The only parties documented to profit consistently from active trading in the SEBI data are algorithmic proprietary desks and FPIs (97%/96% of profits from algos) — the counterparties a retail bot would trade against.
- LLM agents in 2025-26 show no robust edge: single-digit outperformance over four months (StockBench), 4-of-6 losers in real money (Alpha Arena), high sensitivity to perturbation (TradeTrap), and framework-dependent behaviour (AMA). For a monthly ETF contributor, the base rates (85-93% of active retail traders lose; >80% of simulated retail BTC DCA buyers lost) argue strongly against autonomy.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of retail crypto *bot* (grid/DCA/martingale) outcomes was found; only Binance Square anecdotes surfaced, which I excluded. The BIS bulletin is about retail crypto buyers generally, not bots.
- Alpha Arena official per-model statistics and any Season 2 (2026) results could not be retrieved (nof1.ai links to alphaarena.ai, which rate-limited; no Season 2 coverage found).
- Agent Market Arena's actual returns were not in the abstract; the full paper was not read.
- McLean-Pontiff was cited via mirrors and summaries because SSRN/Wiley blocked fetches; the 26%/58% figures are consistent across three secondary sources and the gwern PDF mirror.
- No explicit regulator (SEC/FINRA) statistic on retail algorithmic-bot performance was found.

## R13. Which architecture and risk-management patterns from the best bot repos are reusable in a Claude-Code-based ETF research and monitoring toolkit that never trades?

### Takeaway
The transferable parts are the *scaffolding*, not the strategies: strict signal → risk → execution separation with the execution stage replaced by a report; a small strategy/analyzer interface with a startup-warmup contract and explicit anti-lookahead rules; backtests that charge realistic costs and are followed by out-of-sample and paper stages; portfolio-level kill switches recast as "stop issuing recommendations / escalate to human" rules; deterministic Python for every number with the LLM confined to narration and evidence gathering (the agentic-trading-desk and Vibe-Trading "grounding gate" pattern); cached, versioned data pipelines; config-with-secrets-in-env; audit logs; and the TradingAgents/ai-hedge-fund role decomposition (analysts → bull/bear debate → risk debate → manager) reused as a *report-generation* graph whose terminal node is a human.

### Cited Findings

**Signal → risk → execution separation**
- NautilusTrader's kernel separates DataEngine (subscriptions and routing), RiskEngine (pre-trade validation of order fields, balances, quantities, notionals, reduce-only, trading state, plus configurable order rate limits), ExecutionEngine (routes commands, tracks order/position state, processes fills) and Portfolio (derives balances, PnL and exposure from events); backtest, sandbox and live "share the same kernel and core" with only the data source and execution client swapped; single-threaded core for dispatch/strategy/risk/cache (LMAX-style) with networking/logging on other threads; "crash-only" recovery under an external supervisor — [NautilusTrader architecture](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/latest/concepts/architecture/).
- TradingAgents pipeline: analysts (fundamentals, sentiment, news, technical) run in parallel → bull/bear researchers debate → trader proposes → risk management (aggressive/conservative/neutral debators in `agents/risk_mgmt/`) → `managers/portfolio_manager.py` approves or rejects → simulated exchange; a `managers/research_manager.py` consolidates the research debate; `memory/`, `dataflows/`, `graph/`, `llm_clients/`, `backtest.py`, `portfolio.py`, `reporting.py`, `report_html.py` are separate top-level modules — [README](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents); [tradingagents tree](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents/tree/main/tradingagents); [risk_mgmt tree](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents/tree/main/tradingagents/agents/risk_mgmt); [managers tree](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents/tree/main/tradingagents/agents/managers). (Role descriptions are from file names and README; code not read.)
- ai-hedge-fund package layout (observed 2026-11-02): `hedge_fund/{backtesting, brokers, data, features, fund, llm, paper, pipeline, portfolio, risk, scorecard, signals, strategies, tui, validation}`; investor-persona agents live under `signals/` (README cites `signals/buffett.py`); fund "mandates" live outside the package in `~/.hedge-fund/mandates/`, research outputs in `~/.hedge-fund/research/`, and paper-trading sessions are recorded in a hash-chained ledger — [hedge_fund tree](https://github.com/virattt/ai-hedge-fund/tree/main/hedge_fund); [README](https://github.com/virattt/ai-hedge-fund).
- intelligent-trading-bot enforces that offline (batch) and online (stream) modes "must use the same derived features", with the pipeline download → merge → features → labels → train → predict → signals → output; `labels` uses future data and is therefore unavailable online — a clean statement of the lookahead boundary — [README](https://github.com/asavinov/intelligent-trading-bot).
- agentic-trading-desk splits deterministic stdlib Python (`indicators.py`, `macro_pillar.py` scoring RSP/SPY, HYG/LQD, IWM/SPY, SPY/TLT, XLY/XLP ratios and the 10Y-2Y spread; `score.py` producing one decision from three -2..+2 pillars; `prepare_inputs.py` dropping interpolated/stale bars) from the LLM, which only fetches data, assembles JSON, runs scripts and narrates; a `PreToolUse` hook (`mandate-order-guard.sh`) enforces order rules "outside the model and fails closed" — [README](https://github.com/Oft3r/agentic-trading-desk).
- Vibe-Trading's "grounding gate" checks model-stated figures against tool results and cuts figures that fail; live trading is fail-closed (refuses unverifiable sizing); mandate is bound to a single account — [Vibe-Trading README](https://github.com/HKUDS/Vibe-Trading).

**Strategy interface design**
- freqtrade `IStrategy` (INTERFACE_VERSION 3): required `populate_indicators`, `populate_entry_trend`, `populate_exit_trend` (must not modify OHLCV columns); attributes `minimal_roi` (minutes-since-open → profit ratio), `stoploss` (negative ratio), `timeframe`, `can_short`, `startup_candle_count` (warm-up before indicators are valid; backtests start after it); optional `enter_tag`/`exit_tag` for signal attribution; callbacks (`custom_exit`, `custom_stoploss`, `confirm_trade_entry`, `adjust_trade_position`) documented separately — [freqtrade strategy customization](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/strategy-customization/).
- freqtrade's enumerated lookahead rules (no negative `shift`, no `.iloc[-1]` in `populate_*`, no whole-column aggregates, `label='right'` on resample, use informative-pair helpers) and the `lookahead-analysis`/`recursive-analysis` sub-commands — [same page](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/strategy-customization/).
- Hummingbot V2 "Controllers" are reusable strategies whose configs can be backtested, deployed and tuned while running, built from "Executors" that encapsulate order lifecycles (position, DCA, grid, arbitrage, TWAP) — a config-as-data pattern — [hummingbot README](https://github.com/hummingbot/hummingbot).
- QC's research guide: express the hypothesis as "A change in {cause} leads to an {effect}" before coding; stop if work drifts from the hypothesis — [QC research guide](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/key-concepts/research-guide).

**Backtesting with realistic costs, walk-forward and paper stages**
- QC reality models (fee, fill, slippage, buying power, settlement, short availability, margin interest, dividend yield; portfolio-level margin-call and risk-free-rate models) exist so that "backtests [are] as realistic as possible to how the strategy would perform in live trading" — [QC reality modeling](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/reality-modeling/key-concepts).
- freqtrade applies `--fee` twice (entry and exit) and defaults to the exchange's listed fee; no slippage model — so any reuse must add one — [freqtrade backtesting](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/).
- QuantDinger's signal-only virtual accounts apply a fixed 0.05% commission + 0.05% adverse slippage per fill; Strategy Evolution uses random/grid/TPE search with walk-forward validation and a final blind holdout and reports PBO, Deflated Sharpe, block-bootstrap Monte Carlo and transaction-cost stress; Factor Lab uses a time-ordered holdout, out-of-sample IC and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR — [QuantDinger README](https://github.com/OpenByteInc/QuantDinger).
- Jesse: Optuna optimization with cross-validation, Monte Carlo analysis, "rule significance testing", a benchmark tool — [Jesse README](https://github.com/jesse-ai/jesse).
- trend-allocation-research-bot (Faber 10-month SMA across SPY/EFA/IEF/TLT/GLD/DBC/VNQ, cash to BIL; 2007-01-03 to 2026-10-02): charges 0.05% per side including BIL; signals use only prior closes and fill at next open; a test suite mutates future prices and asserts signals/orders/equity are unchanged, with a planted leak that must make the tests fail; results after costs: 5.2% CAGR / -9.9% MDD vs SPY 10.9% / -55.2% and 60/40 8.1% / -32.5% (Sharpe 0.60 vs 0.63); README states the strategy was picked among five on the same history, so "it is in-sample model selection"; paper-trading on Alpaca began 2026-10-05 — [README](https://github.com/aidaman01/trend-allocation-research-bot).
- Stage gating in the bot docs: freqtrade "Always start by running a trading bot in Dry-run"; OctoBot "Always start by running a trading bot in simulation mode"; QuantDinger agent trading is paper-only by default and live requires a trading-scoped token, `paper_only=false`, a server flag and operator allowlists; ALTA has a Shadow mode (internal fills, no broker orders) before Broker API mode — [freqtrade docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/); [OctoBot README](https://github.com/Drakkar-Software/OctoBot); [QuantDinger README](https://github.com/OpenByteInc/QuantDinger); [ALTA README](https://github.com/kyky2347/ALTA).

**Risk limits, position sizing, drawdown kill switches**
- freqtrade Protections: `StoplossGuard` (stop trading after N stoploss exits in a lookback), `MaxDrawdown` (stop when drawdown across recent closed trades exceeds `max_allowed_drawdown`, `calculation_mode: equity` recommended), `LowProfitPairs` (lock a pair whose profit over the lookback is below a floor), `CooldownPeriod`; all with `lookback_period`, `trade_limit`, `stop_duration`, `unlock_at`; plus pairlist filters (`SpreadFilter` default 0.5% max spread, `VolatilityFilter`, `RangeStabilityFilter`, `AgeFilter`, `PrecisionFilter`, `DelistFilter`) — [freqtrade plugins](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/plugins/).

- CloddsBot risk engine: circuit breaker, VaR/CVaR, volatility-regime detection, stress testing, Kelly sizing, daily loss limits, kill switch; dry-run default — [CloddsBot README](https://github.com/alsk1992/CloddsBot).

- Vibe-Trading: kill switch cancels orders and flattens positions during halts; margin/liquidation checks; A-share price-limit and T+1 rules encoded — [Vibe-Trading README](https://github.com/HKUDS/Vibe-Trading).

### Inferences (what to reuse in a never-trades ETF monitor)
- Keep the three-stage bus but rename the stages: **Data → Analyzers (deterministic Python) → Risk/Policy gate → Report**. The "execution client" becomes a report sink (Markdown/HTML/email), and the Nautilus/QuantDinger "sandbox = real data, simulated execution" mode is the *permanent* mode.
- Reuse freqtrade's `IStrategy` shape as an `Analyzer` protocol: `warmup_bars`, `compute(df) -> df`, `signal(df) -> Signal(tag, value, evidence)`; enforce its anti-lookahead rules with a mutation test like trend-allocation-research-bot's (perturb future prices, assert outputs unchanged).
- Risk layer for a monitor = policy checks instead of order checks: drift bands per holding (richfolio), spread/liquidity filters on candidate ETFs (freqtrade `SpreadFilter`/`VolatilityFilter`), data-reconciliation halt (agentic-trading-desk "halt if data does not reconcile"), a "Wait/insufficient evidence" outcome (ALTA), and a MaxDrawdown-style escalation rule that changes *who is told*, not what is traded.
- Keep every number deterministic (agentic-trading-desk, Vibe-Trading grounding gate); let Claude write narrative, collect evidence with hashes/timestamps (ALTA), and run the bull/bear/risk debate from TradingAgents only as a *reasoning* layer feeding a human decision — since StockBench/TradeTrap show the LLM layer is the least reliable component.
- Backtest discipline to port: charge explicit costs (0.05%/side is the convention in both QuantDinger and trend-allocation-research-bot; Canadian ETF spreads should be measured, not assumed), report DSR/PBO when many parameters are tried, hold out a blind period, and label any result where model selection used the reported period as "in-sample".
- Ops: config as JSON/YAML with secrets in env/`.env`; cache raw data locally with a stable schema; append-only run log (hash-chained like ai-hedge-fund's ledger if auditability matters); scheduled runs via a reliable external trigger rather than GitHub `schedule:` cron (richfolio's lesson); alerting only on change-vs-last-brief.
