# Data sources, signal evidence and backtesting frameworks for ETF monitoring (R10 / R11 / R12)

Research date: 2026-11-02. All prices, limits and library versions were observed on that date unless a source date is given. "Observed" means a direct check from this sandbox (PyPI JSON API, HTTP probe); "reported" means a third-party page. Where the vendor page could not be fetched (403/503/JS-only), the best secondary source is cited and flagged.

## R10. Data sources and APIs for ETF monitoring (prices, holdings, flows, macro, news, web search)

### Takeaway
For a personal, non-trading Claude Code toolkit, the pragmatic 2026 stack is: **yfinance 1.x (now on curl_cffi, still unofficial and rate-limited by Yahoo) as the zero-cost default for US and TSX (".TO") daily prices and basic ETF holdings; EODHD Historian ($19.99/mo, 30+ yrs, explicit "TO" exchange code) or Tiingo Power ($30/mo, US-only) as the paid, licensed fallback; FRED + Bank of Canada Valet + Statistics Canada (all free, keyless or free-key) for macro; SEC EDGAR N-PORT via edgartools for US ETF holdings (1–2 month lag, US funds only); Google News RSS (free, undocumented limits) plus a cheap web-search API (Brave $5/1k, Exa $4–7/1k, Tavily $0.008/credit) for news.** No single vendor gives free TSX ETF holdings/flows; Canadian holdings come from issuer CSVs or paid EODHD/Twelve Data add-ons.

### Inferences
- For a daily/monthly ETF watch that touches perhaps 20–60 tickers, Tiingo Starter (1,000/day, 500 symbols/month) or FMP free (250/day) cover price history comfortably; yfinance remains viable if calls are batched (`yf.download` with a ticker list), cached to disk and limited to one refresh per day, since every documented block came from loops of many per-ticker calls.
- Only EODHD (explicit "TO" code, $19.99/mo, 30+ years) and Twelve Data (Grow tier + Cboe Canada add-on) are documented as licensed TSX sources; Tiingo and Polygon/Massive should be treated as US-only for ETF prices. For Canadian holdings, the toolkit should scrape issuer CSVs (iShares pattern verified only for the US site) or accept the N-PORT-equivalent gap.
- N-PORT's 1–2 month lag makes it suitable for monthly context (drift of top-10 weights, sector exposure), not for anything "daily".
- NewsAPI's free tier is unusable for a production personal tool (dev-only, 24 h delay); Google News RSS + GNews free (12 h delay, non-commercial) + a $5–$10/month web-search budget (Brave/Exa) is the realistic free-to-cheap news layer for a personal toolkit. Any commercial deployment changes the licensing of nearly every free tier listed.
- OpenBB's corporate wind-down (Aug 2026) makes the `openbb` 5.x package a stewardship risk; `financetoolkit`/`financedatabase` (MIT, releasing monthly) and direct vendor clients are safer dependencies.

## R11. Signal evidence: news/LLM sentiment, macro regimes, trend rules, valuation

### Takeaway
The robust, replicated findings are: (1) LLM/news sentiment predicts single-stock returns for only one to two trading days, at magnitudes (tens of bps/day) only market makers can harvest, with the strategy Sharpe collapsing from 6.5 (2021Q4) to 1.2 (2024) and part of the apparent signal attributable to training-data memorization — so sentiment is **context, never a trigger** for an ETF report; (2) 10-month SMA / 12-month momentum rules reliably cut maximum drawdown roughly in half with roughly zero-to-negative return cost, but the post-2007 out-of-sample return gap versus buy-and-hold is 1–5 percentage points per year depending on data granularity and the defensive asset, with 2020 (no protection, intra-month crash) and 2022 (whipsaw, bonds fell with stocks) as the canonical failure modes; (3) macro regime indicators (yield curve, Sahm rule) have strong recession-forecasting records but both produced their first clear false signals in 2022–2024, and AQR explicitly warns against using the curve as a market-timing tool; (4) CAPE has real 10-year predictive content but its out-of-sample RMSE since 1985 (7.8%) was *worse* than a naive historical mean (6.2%) unless adjusted for real yields.

### Cited Findings

#### (a) News / LLM sentiment
- Lopez-Lira & Tang, "Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements?" (arXiv v6, 2025-10-28): sample Oct 2021–May 2024 (post-training-cutoff), 4,123 companies; GPT-4 captures the initial reaction with ~90% portfolio-day hit rates (non-tradable); for the tradable drift, hit rates are 58% (overnight news) and 55% (intraday), prices "continue drifting ... over the next one to two trading days"; a daily-rebalanced long-short on overnight news earns 34 bps/day pre-cost, annualized Sharpe 2.97 (overnight) and 2.63 (intraday), cumulative ~700% pre-cost, >300% at 5 bps round-trip cost and >100% at 10 bps; the authors state exploiting it "is only feasible for market participants whose transaction costs are sufficiently low, such as market makers"; predictability is stronger for small stocks and negative news; the strategy's annualized Sharpe "drops from 6.54 in 2021Q4 to 3.68 in 2022, 2.33 in 2023, and to 1.22 over January–May 2024", read as adoption-driven efficiency — [arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619) (figures from the v6 PDF text)
- A replication package exists on Mendeley Data — [Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/f39x226htv/2)
- Gao, Jiang & Yan, "A Test of Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts" (arXiv, submitted 2025-12-29): using a membership-inference "Lookahead Propensity" score on Bloomberg headlines 2012–2023 with Llama-3.3, their LLM-only specification replicates Lopez-Lira & Tang; a 1-SD rise in the LLM prediction predicts +0.197% next-day return, but a 1-SD rise in memorization propensity adds +0.077 pp (~37% of the effect) and the standalone coefficient becomes insignificant once the interaction is included; a placebo on post-release data (Sep 2023–Dec 2024) shows no interaction; prompt-level mitigations (masking names/dates) do not remove the contamination — [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847v1)
- Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu (2025), "The Memorization Problem: Can We Trust LLMs' Economic Forecasts?" argues apparent LLM forecasting ability "often reflects memorization rather than genuine prediction" — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390924734_The_Memorization_Problem_Can_We_Trust_LLMs'_Economic_Forecasts); an earlier GPT-3.5 S&P 500 headline study found look-ahead bias was "not a concern" out-of-sample but "distraction remains possible" — [arXiv 2309.17322 via IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2309.17322.html)
- FinBERT, pre-registered tests (US stocks, daily and intraday): +9.7 bps per unit sentiment at daily horizon (p = 0.30) and +3.95 bps intraday (p = 0.56), "far below cost at every horizon from 5 minutes to 5 hours" — [news-sentiment-research repo](https://github.com/NakulPillai2407/news-sentiment-research)
- A 2026 QLoRA benchmark of seven sentiment models finds all have positive but small one-day rank ICs; FinBERT's is the largest at 0.0143 — [arXiv 2608.04200](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04200)
- A 2026 hedge-fund-perspective review concludes most LLM sentiment pipelines yield "regime- and source-dependent mappings from text to returns and, consequently, fragile out-of-sample performance" — [arXiv 2605.05211](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05211)
- Index level: Bond, Klok & Zhu find a "noteworthy negative correlation" between ChatGPT sentiment on daily S&P 500 news summaries and short-term market returns, with ChatGPT having only "a slight edge in out-of-sample performance" over conventional classifiers — [SSRN 4584928](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4584928)
- ChatGPT's own return forecasts extrapolate recent returns and are more optimistic when shown return charts than price charts (AEA 2026) — [arXiv 2409.11540](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.11540)
- A small Substack test found FinBERT "positive" days were followed by negative average next-day returns (n≈68, anecdotal) — [Tommi Johnsen](https://tommijohnsen.substack.com/p/can-llms-beat-finbert-for-stock-sentiment)

#### (b) Macro regime indicators
- AQR "Inversion Anxiety" (3Q 2019): the US 10y–3m slope predicts next-year real GDP growth with a coefficient of 0.62 over 1969–2019 (a 1-point steeper curve → 0.62% higher growth), statistically significant at 1–2 year horizons; AQR states the slope "may be more useful for country allocation decisions than for market timing" and warns against being "overconfident in using the yield curve as a market timing indicator" — [AQR Alternative Thinking 3Q19 (PDF)](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking-3Q19-Inversion-Anxiety-Yield-Curves-Economic-Growth-and-Asset-Prices.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- The 2s10s inversion that began in early July 2022 became the longest on record in March 2024, exceeding the 624-day 1978 episode — [Reuters via Kitco, 2024-03-21](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2024-03-21/us-treasury-key-yield-curve-inversion-becomes-longest-record); a dataset page with data to Feb 2026 states the episode "has not been followed by any recession at all" — [eco3min 2s10s history](https://eco3min.fr/en/yield-curve-inversion-deinversion-recession-signal/)
- AQR "A Half Century of Macro Momentum" (2017): macro-momentum strategies show some equity-timing skill but "very limited" bond-timing skill — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/White-Papers/A-Half-Century-of-Macro-Momentum.pdf)
- AQR "Market Timing: Sin a Little" (Asness, Ilmanen, Maloney, JOIM 2017): value-only timing shows "encouraging in-sample long-horizon evidence and directionally right but weak and disappointing out-of-sample performance"; adding 12-month momentum to value timing adds ~1.2 pp/yr over 115 years for equities (Validea's summary) or ~50 bps/yr for a 50/50 portfolio after 1950 (Morningstar's summary; Validea says 0.8%); advice is modest tilts, "sin a little (perhaps very little)" — [AQR paper page](https://www.aqr.com/library/journal-articles/market-timing-sin-a-little); [Validea summary](https://blog.validea.com/?p=15318); [Morningstar/Rekenthaler](https://www.morningstar.com/columns/rekenthaler-report/case-market-timing)
- Sahm rule: every trigger since 1970 (Mar 1974, Apr 1980, Nov 1981, Nov 1990, Jun 2001, Apr 2008, Apr 2020) coincided with an NBER recession within 2–4 months of the peak; the July 2024 trigger (peak 0.57 in Aug 2024, down to 0.13 by Apr 2026) is "the first false signal since 1970" (FRED SAHMREALTIME, page updated 2026-10-30) — [eco3min Sahm history](https://eco3min.fr/en/sahm-rule-false-signals-history/); Claudia Sahm herself said in Aug 2024 the signal was "likely overstating the labor market's weakening" due to labor-supply shifts — [Fortune, 2024-08-02](https://fortune.com/2024/08/02/recession-indicator-claudia-sahm-rule-trigger-unemployment-rate-jobs-report)
- Fidelity's business-cycle framework separates tactical (1–12 months), business-cycle (1–10 years) and secular (10–30 years) horizons; it reports the mid-cycle phase has "the smallest sector performance differentiation of any business cycle phase"; data 1962–2016, Fidelity's own (not independent) — [Fidelity Business Cycle Sector Approach (2020 PDF)](https://www.fidelity.com/webcontent/ap101883-markets_sectors-content/21.01.0/business_cycle/Business_Cycle_Sector_Approach_2020.pdf); an academic paper is "the first to question the underlying assumption that the cycle creates profitable rotation opportunities" — [Sector rotation over business cycles](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228425439_Sector_rotation_over_business-cycles)
- ISM PMI: a 1948–Aug 2023 study pairs monthly PMI with contemporaneous S&P 500 closes (conclusions paywalled) — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/economic-indicators/ism-manufacturing-pmi-and-stock-returns/); the only academic test found is a 2018 master's thesis regressing 1–3-month-ahead excess returns on PMI (results not visible) — [BI Norwegian thesis](https://biopen.bi.no/bi-xmlui/handle/11250/2579152); a practitioner note shows the ISM index is correlated with year-on-year S&P 500 changes (contemporaneous, not forecasting) — [Investment Officer](https://cms.investmentofficer.com/en/news/chart-week-dont-be-blindsided-recession)
- Credit spreads: a retrospective of eight equity dislocations since 1997 finds ICE BofA HY OAS began widening before the S&P 500 peak with a median 7-month lead (descriptive, no false-alarm accounting) — [eco3min HY spread dataset](https://eco3min.fr/en/us-hy-credit-spread-leading-indicator-dataset/); peer-reviewed work (Huang & Kong 2003) shows equity factors explain HY spread *changes*, i.e., co-movement rather than forecasting power — [NYU archive](https://archive.nyu.edu/handle/2451/26752)

#### (c) Trend rules: 10-month SMA (Faber), 12-month momentum (Antonacci)
- Faber's published model: hold an asset when its month-end price is above its 10-month SMA ("corresponds closest to the 200 day moving average"), else cash; evaluated "only ... once a month on the last day of the month"; data from Global Financial Data, total return — [mebfaber.com timing model](https://mebfaber.com/timing-model/); the 2018 JPM "Revisited 10 Years Later" update re-tested 1972–2005 in-sample plus 11 years out-of-sample and found the equal-weighted multi-asset timing version raised return from 9.8% to 11.3% with ~0.2 pts more volatility (Swedroe's summary) — [ETF.com / Swedroe](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-why-financial-trends-persist); original paper page (SSRN, fetch blocked 403) — [SSRN 962461](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=962461)
- Independent monthly replication (Shiller monthly S&P 500, Jan 1950–Sep 2026, price return, trade next month, 0.1% per switch): 10-month SMA CAGR 8.6%, return/vol 0.99, max DD −19.2% vs buy-and-hold 8.3%, 0.73, −50.8%; with a 2-month execution delay: 7.0%, 0.81, −27.3%; with dividends and 0% cash: 10.9% vs 11.7% CAGR; post-publication 2007–present: SMA 10.0%/−19.2% vs B&H 8.9%/−50.8%; every lookback 6–14 months beats B&H on return/vol; COVID Feb–Mar 2020: −19.1% for both (monthly signal cannot react within a month); rate shock Jan–Sep 2022: −5.2% vs −17.6%; author warns monthly-average prices "likely flatter a trend rule" — [sp500-trend-filter-backtest](https://github.com/lotushansstudio-maker/sp500-trend-filter-backtest)
- Independent daily replication (S&P 500 daily, Jan 1987–Oct 2026, US aggregate bonds as the defensive asset, published 2026-10-23): 10-month SMA CAGR 9.38% vs 11.24% B&H; max DD ~34% vs 55%; 48 switches (239% annual turnover, average hold 209 days) vs 27 for 12-month absolute momentum, costing ~1.2 pts/yr; 2022: SMA −26.6%, absolute momentum −19.2%, index −24.1%, flipping in and out three times "as bonds fell alongside stocks"; post-2007 split: 7.36%/yr vs 10.85% (a 3.5-pt gap) — [algostrategylab SMA trend filter](https://algostrategylab.github.io/sma-trend-filter/)
- The two replications disagree sharply on 2022 (−5.2% vs −26.6%) and on the post-2007 gap (+1.1 pts vs −3.5 pts); the differences are consistent with monthly-average vs daily prices, cash vs bonds as the defensive asset, and price vs total return (see Inferences).
- Faber himself flagged the model going to cash in Feb 2022 ("Red Light") — [mebfaber.com 2022-02-28](https://mebfaber.com/2022/02/28/red-light/)
- Antonacci GEM, clean-room replication (SPY/VEU/AGG/BIL, monthly, 12-month lookback, published 2026-07-31): 2008–2026 GEM CAGR 9.1%, Sharpe 0.74, max DD −20% vs SPY 11.6%/0.77/−42% and static 60/40 8.3%/0.84/−27%; out-of-sample since the 2014 book: GEM 8.4%/0.70/−20% vs SPY 13.6%/0.94/−24% and 60/40 9.1%/0.95/−20%; 30 switches in 18 years; moved to bonds in 2008–09, 2020 and 2022; whipsawed in 2011 and 2015–16; verdict "Not broken — just beaten by boring" — [quant4free Dual Momentum](https://quant4free.com/analysis/dual-momentum/)
- Petit (SSRN, 2026), "Global Equities Momentum, 1971–2026": GEM trailed a passive S&P 500 by about 4.8 pts/yr since 2010; its deepest drawdown in that span came in 2021–2023 while holding the defensive asset — [SSRN 7427878](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7427878) (abstract via search; page fetch blocked)
- Decomposition 2008–2026: at the 12-month lookback GEM returned 9.3%/yr with a 33.7% worst drawdown; "the absolute momentum gate did the work, the international leg cost 1.4 to 3.2 points a year at every lookback tested" — [quanterlab](https://quanterlab.com/research/antonacci-s-dual-momentum-taken-apart-gate-relative-leg-and-blends-across-four-l)
- Vendor/author-linked pages report stronger numbers (13.8% CAGR, −21.9% DD over 1980–2026), which rely on pre-publication data — [dualmomentumsystems](https://dualmomentumsystems.com/strategies/global-equities-momentum); [Antonacci's extended backtest](https://medium.com/@garyantonacci_30463/extended-backtest-of-global-equities-momentum-dual-momentum-eb12902612e0)
- Broader TSMOM evidence: Moskowitz, Sabbatucci, Tamoni & Uhl (Dec 2025) report a nonlinear time-series-momentum variant that improves out-of-sample results over 2000–2024 — [SSRN 5933974](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5933974); Suominen & Hjalmarsson (2026) find equity TSMOM "breaks down, and can even reverse, when valuations reach historical extremes", holding out of sample — [Swedroe summary](https://larryswedroe.substack.com/p/when-trend-following-hits-a-wall)

#### (d) Valuation (CAPE) at 10-year horizons
- Vanguard (Davis, Aliaga-Díaz et al.), "Improving U.S. stock return forecasts: a fair-value CAPE approach": Table 1 — for 10-year nominal returns, out-of-sample forecasts since 1960 using Shiller CAPE have RMSE 5.5% vs 5.8% for the naive historical mean (lower, *), but since 1985 RMSE 7.8% vs 6.2% (HIGHER, ***); real returns since 1985: 7.8% vs 5.7% (HIGHER, ***); their real-yield-conditioned "fair-value CAPE" cuts the since-1985 error to 4.1% vs 7.8% ("reducing out-of-sample forecast errors by as much as 50%") — [Vanguard paper (PDF mirrored on mebfaber.com)](https://mebfaber.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Improving-U.S.-Stock-Return-Forecasts.pdf)
- A June 2026 "Component CAPE" (constituent-aligned earnings) reports out-of-sample R² of 57.5% for 10-year returns vs 46.7% for aggregate CAPE — [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/financial-advisors/better-way-predict-long-term-stock-returns); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/cape-ratios/)
- An MDPI paper concludes "poor out-of-sample performance of the CAPE Ratio as a tactical allocation tool" and defines Excess CAPE Yield as 1/CAPE minus the 10-year real rate — [MDPI JRFM 16(9):410](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/16/9/410)
- A blog backtest on 1881–2013 Shiller data reports correlation ≈ −0.52 between starting CAPE and subsequent 10-year real returns (non-peer-reviewed) — [1KAlpha](https://1kalpha.com/blog/cape-ratio-predicts-returns-backtest.html)

### Inferences
- **Context vs trigger for a monthly ETF report:**
  - *Context only (never a trigger):* news/LLM sentiment (1–2 day horizon, decaying, memorization-contaminated); ISM PMI (no rigorous forward-looking evidence found); credit-spread widening (descriptive, 8 episodes); yield-curve level/inversion and Sahm rule (both misfired in 2022–2024; useful as recession-probability context); CAPE / fair-value CAPE (10-year expected-return framing only; Vanguard's own numbers show raw CAPE underperformed a historical mean since 1985).
  - *Eligible as a trigger, if the owner accepts the trade-off:* month-end 10-month SMA or 12-month absolute momentum on the equity sleeve. The replicated effect is drawdown halving (−50% → −19% to −34%) with a return cost of roughly 0 to 3.5 pts/yr depending on implementation, plus 1–3 whipsaws per decade (2011, 2015–16, 2020 late re-entry, 2022 three flips). The daily-data study is the more conservative and realistic estimate for an ETF investor.
  - *If used as a trigger:* evaluate only on the last trading day of the month (Faber), use total-return series, model the defensive asset explicitly (cash vs bonds changed the 2022 outcome by ~20 pts), and prefer the absolute-momentum gate over the relative (international) leg, which cost 1.4–3.2 pts/yr.
- Because the toolkit never trades automatically, "trigger" here means "flag for the human"; AQR's "sin a little" evidence suggests presenting trend/valuation as tilt recommendations (e.g., ±10–20% equity weight) rather than all-in/all-out.
- LLM sentiment is still useful in the report for *explaining* what moved an ETF (summarising headlines), as long as it is labelled as non-predictive.

### Gaps
- No quantitative source was found for how the 10-month SMA or GEM behaved in the 2025 episode (April 2025 tariff drawdown and recovery); the two 2026 replications stop at "not covered".
- Faber's 2018 JPM revisit numbers for the S&P 500 sleeve (Sharpe, drawdown 2006–2017) could not be retrieved (SSRN/JPM blocked); only the multi-asset headline (9.8% → 11.3%) via Swedroe.
- No peer-reviewed, out-of-sample test of ISM PMI or inflation surprises as monthly allocation signals surfaced; no Man Group study on macro regimes was found; Vanguard regime-timing research was not found beyond the CAPE paper.
- No study of LLM sentiment at the ETF or index level with a tradable horizon beyond a few days was found; all evidence is single-stock or S&P 500 daily.
- Lopez-Lira & Tang's published-version status (journal) was not confirmed; the arXiv v6 (Oct 2025) is cited.

## R12. Backtesting and validation frameworks (libraries, overfitting controls, DCA portfolio backtests)

### Takeaway
As of 2026-11-02 the actively released Python options are **vectorbt 1.2.0 (Apache-2.0 + Commons Clause, Python 3.11–3.14), backtesting.py 0.6.6 (AGPL-3.0), zipline-reloaded 3.2.1, bt 1.4.1, pybroker 2.0.2, quantstats 0.0.86, skfolio 1.12.5 and PyPortfolioOpt 1.6.0**; **backtrader is frozen at 1.9.78.123 (April 2023)** and **mlfinlab is closed-source/paid**. For a monthly-rebalanced ETF portfolio with contributions, the lightest defensible approach is a pandas/skfolio pipeline: skfolio supplies `WalkForward`, `CombinatorialPurgedCV`, `MultipleRandomizedCV`, `probabilistic_sharpe_ratio`, `deflated_sharpe_ratio` and `expected_max_sharpe_ratio` natively, so DSR/CPCV no longer require mlfinlab. Validate with (i) a strict publication-date out-of-sample split, (ii) CPCV or walk-forward with monthly folds, (iii) DSR against the number of variants tried, and (iv) Harvey–Liu–Zhu's t>3 hurdle. For long survivorship-free index history use testfol.io (US SIM tickers back to 1885/1926), Portfolio Visualizer (1972+, Ken French/AQR/FRED backfills) or Curvo (European, EUR, monthly contributions), and Shiller / Ken French data for raw series.

### Inferences
- **Framework choice for a monthly ETF portfolio with contributions:** none of the event-driven engines (backtrader frozen, zipline-reloaded heavy, LEAN needs Docker and a paid tier for local CLI) is justified for ~monthly rebalancing of a handful of ETFs. A pandas-based engine (positions × month-end total-return prices, explicit cash-flow ledger, fee drag, FX conversion using Bank of Canada daily rates) is simpler to audit, and skfolio supplies the CV/DSR layer. vectorbt is the fallback if parameter sweeps (lookbacks, bands) are needed; its Commons Clause is irrelevant for a personal tool but blocks selling it.
- **DCA correctness checklist (derived from testfol.io/Portfolio Visualizer behaviour):** contributions on a fixed schedule with an offset; dividends reinvested on ex-date (ETFs) or T+2 (mutual funds); expense ratio applied as a daily drag only when using index proxies (fund series already embed it); FX: convert CAD contributions at the Bank of Canada daily rate; report money-weighted (IRR) and time-weighted returns separately because contributions distort CAGR; use index-backed SIM series (1926+/1970+) for the pre-ETF era and switch to live fund series when they exist.
- **Overfitting controls to hard-code:** (1) freeze a publication-date split (e.g., Faber 2007, Antonacci 2014) and never tune on post-publication data; (2) `WalkForward` with monthly steps or `CombinatorialPurgedCV` with an embargo of at least one rebalance period; (3) compute `deflated_sharpe_ratio` with `expected_max_sharpe_ratio` fed the actual number of variants tried — with 5 years of monthly data (60 observations) and >45 variants, DSR will reject almost everything, which is the point; (4) require t>3 on any claimed signal; (5) stress with an execution delay of one month and with both cash and bonds as the defensive asset.
- Minimum backtest length matters more for monthly rules: 60 monthly observations is far too few to separate a 0.7 vs 0.9 Sharpe; prefer index-backed series from 1972 (Portfolio Visualizer) or earlier (testfol.io SIMs, Shiller) for the research stage, then confirm on live-ETF history.
