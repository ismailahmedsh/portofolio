# Index steadily, monitor daily, never autotrade

Research report. Date: 2026-11-02. Sources: eight research notes in `research_notes/ETF long term and AI research tools/`, `00-framework.md` (goal G0, pillars R1-R15) and `audits/research_audit_round2.md` (44/45, PASS). Written for the investor as reader; this is a synthesis of evidence and trade-offs, not personal financial advice.

**Bottom line.** For a Canada-based investor with Egyptian ties who deposits monthly and wants a high long-run return with bounded instability, the evidence points to one boring core and one smart monitor. The core is a single all-equity, auto-rebalancing Canadian-listed ETF (XEQT at **0.19% MER** on the Sep 30, 2026 fact sheet, or VEQT at 0.22% falling toward ~0.19%) bought in CAD with $0 commission at Wealthsimple or Questrade, held TFSA first, then RRSP, then FHSA/taxable, with every monthly deposit invested on receipt rather than held back; a 4-fund DIY mix (XUU/XEF/XEC/XIC) tracked XEQT within about C$1,000 on C$87,000 contributed over 2019-2026 and saves only ~7 bps/yr, so simplicity wins below a few hundred thousand dollars ([tech Q2 backtest](/home/user/portofolio/investing-research/data/verify/t2_backtest_output.md); [BlackRock XEQT fact sheet](https://www.blackrock.com/ca/investors/en/literature/fact-sheet/xeqt-ishares-core-equity-etf-portfolio-fund-fact-sheet-en-ca.pdf)). The stress case for a 100% equity core is a **-50% to -65% drawdown** (US total market -50.8% monthly, MSCI ACWI -58.4%, EM -65.3% in 2007-2009) and a lost decade (S&P 500 +1.5%/yr nominal in the 2000s); XEQT's live -17.6% (2022) is not that test ([MSCI ACWI factsheet, Sep 30, 2026](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/0ca6fd46-3b5e-410e-bbde-e3bdbea88ef3); [Motley Fool S&P 500 history](https://www.fool.com/research/sp-500-annual-returns/)). Autonomous trading bots are the wrong vehicle: the large administrative datasets show 93% of ten million Indian derivatives traders losing money over FY22-24 and only about 1% of persistent day traders in Brazil and Taiwan earning reliable profits (derivatives and day-trader populations; no source supports a broader figure for all active retail traders), published strategies decay 58% after publication, the 2025-2026 LLM-agent record is at best +1.5 points over buy-and-hold in a four-month benchmark and four of six models lost real money in Alpha Arena, and the bots' own docs say "it will always be a gamble" ([SEBI PR 22/2024](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2024/1727086965496.pdf); [McLean & Pontiff 2016](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf); [StockBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02209); [freqtrade FAQ](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/faq/)). What is worth reusing from bot repos is their scaffolding: signal-risk-execution separation with execution replaced by a report, anti-lookahead rules, cost-charged backtests, and TradingAgents' report tree and "REVIEW instead of Hold" rating parser. A Claude Code toolkit that produces a daily watch note and a monthly review can run on a **keyless data path verified on 2026-11-02** (yfinance batch for TSX tickers, Bank of Canada Valet FX, FRED CSV, iShares holdings CSV), a committed `.mcp.json` of free MCP servers, deterministic Python for every number, and a cloud scheduled task or local cron, for **$20/month on a Claude Pro subscription** (or ~$24/month with licensed EODHD data on the API route), with the main risks being data licensing (personal use only), yfinance fragility, LLM error rates of roughly 36-40% on expert finance tasks, and prompt injection via fetched content ([tech Q1/Q4](/home/user/portofolio/investing-research/data/verify/verify_log.md); [Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing); [Vals AI benchmark reporting](https://the-decoder.com/benchmark-shows-ai-agents-cant-yet-replace-human-analysts-in-finance/)).

## One all-equity ETF captures the whole evidence base (R1, R2, R3)

The ETF universe has become so cheap that fee shopping no longer decides anything. Broad exposure to the whole world ex-Canada now costs 0.03-0.07% in US-listed form (VTI 0.03%, VOO 0.03%, VT 0.06%, VXUS 0.05%) and 0.05-0.28% in Canadian-listed form, so a 0.10% fee difference on C$100,000 is C$100 a year, while the structural cost of foreign withholding tax by account type is a comparable or larger lever ([Vanguard VTI fact sheet](https://workplace.vanguard.com/iippdf/pdfs/FS970R.pdf); [etf_core R1 inference](/home/user/portofolio/investing-research/research_notes/ETF%20long%20term%20and%20AI%20research%20tools/etf_core_R1_R2_R3.md)). What still matters in selection is the index (total-market, cap-weighted, global), the fund's structure (direct holdings vs a wrap of a US-listed ETF, which adds a second withholding layer in registered accounts), liquidity measured by spread rather than AUM alone, and a minimum size (one guide suggests US$100 million) to avoid the record **146 active-ETF closures of 2025**, where the typical casualty was about 1.75 years old ([Schwab Asset Management, How to evaluate ETFs](https://www.schwabassetmanagement.com/content/how-to-evaluate-etfs); [Morningstar launches and liquidations 2025](https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/blog/active-etf-launches-and-liquidations-2025)). ### Returns of 9-15% a year came with 50-65% drawdowns

On one consistent basis (issuer and index-provider sheets dated Sep 30, 2026, NAV total return), the broad candidates compounded at 9-15% over ten years, but the only way to read those numbers honestly is next to their worst losses. The US total market lost **50.8%** peak to trough in 2007-2009 on monthly data, MSCI ACWI lost **58.4%** and MSCI EAFE **60.4%** on daily data, and emerging markets lost **65.3%** in a single year (Oct 2007 to Oct 2008); the S&P 500 then delivered a decade of +1.48%/yr nominal and -1.13% real (the 2000s), the only negative real decade since 1928 ([MSCI ACWI factsheet](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/0ca6fd46-3b5e-410e-bbde-e3bdbea88ef3); [MSCI EM factsheet](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/c0db0a48-01f2-4ba9-ad01-226fd5678111); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/research/sp-500-annual-returns/)). Since 1928 the S&P 500 compounded at 9.92% nominal and 6.64% real, with an average intra-year drawdown of -13.9% even in years that finished positive ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/research/sp-500-annual-returns/); [Lord Abbett](https://www.lordabbett.com/content/dam/lordabbett-captivate/documents/market-volatility/Volatility-Brochure.pdf)).

### Forward returns are forecast at 1-4% real, which argues for breadth, not bets

Every major house expects the next decade to pay far less than the last one. Vanguard's model run of Sep 30, 2026 puts US equities at **4.8-6.8% nominal** over ten years and developed ex-US at 5.3-7.3%, with US aggregate bonds at 5.1-6.1%, so the equity premium over bonds is barely a point; JPMorgan's 2026 assumptions (Sep 30, 2025) give US large cap 6.7%, EM 7.8% and a global 60/40 6.4%; AQR (Dec 31, 2025) gives 3.9% real for US equities and 5.3% real for developed ex-US, with a global 60/40 at 3.4% real; Research Affiliates sits at the bottom with ~0.8% real for US stocks ([Vanguard VCMM](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/vemo/vemo-return-forecasts.html); [JPM 2026 LTCMA](https://am.jpmorgan.com/us/en/asset-management/institutional/about-us/media/press-releases/jp-morgan-releases-2026-long-term-capital-market-assumptions/); [AQR 2026 CMAs](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Alternative-Thinking/2026-Capital-Market-Assumptions-for-Major-Asset-Classes); [Wealthtender on Morningstar's 2026 compilation](https://wealthtender.com/insights/analysis-of-2026-expert-market-predictions/)). The dispersion (US real 0.8-4.3%) is itself the finding: a 15-year plan should be stress-tested at about 1% real equity returns, where a C$1,000/month contributor ends up roughly 15% poorer than at 3% real, and every house puts developed ex-US 1-2 points above the US, which is the strongest quantitative reason in the notes to hold ex-US and EM at full market weight ([numbers Q5](/home/user/portofolio/investing-research/research_notes/ETF%20long%20term%20and%20AI%20research%20tools/gapfill2_etf_numbers_overlays.md)).

### Alternatives to the plain core cost return without buying much stability

The allocation question has an unusually clear answer in the literature. Anarkulova, Cederburg and O'Doherty's lifecycle study over 2,600+ country-years in 39 developed markets finds an all-equity portfolio of roughly **33% domestic / 67% international** "vastly outperforms" age-based stock-bond glide paths on wealth, retirement consumption and bequests, and that a target-date investor would need 61% more savings to match it ([PWL Capital summary](https://parkyndoyonlarochelle.pwlcapital.com/?p=10218); [Rational Reminder ep. 350](https://rationalreminder.libsyn.com/website/episode-350-scott-cederburg-a-critical-assessment-of-lifecycle-investment-advice)). Vanguard Canada's home-bias paper lands on 30% Canadian / 70% foreign for a Canadian ([Vanguard Canada CHBP 2023](https://www.vanguard.ca/content/dam/intl/americas/canada/en/documents/CHBP_062023_V8_secure.pdf)). XEQT (26% Canada) and VEQT (30% Canada) already implement both findings, and Cboe's comparison of the pair concludes "neither consistently beats the other" ([Cboe Canada](https://etfmarket.cboe.com/canada/en/news/etf-comparison-xeqt-vs.-veqt)).

The tilts and overlays each come with a documented bill. Defensive multi-asset designs (All Weather, Golden Butterfly, Permanent) returned 6.4-7.4%/yr over Jul 2007-Oct 2026 against 10.6% for 100% US equity, halving the worst drawdown (-16% to -21% vs -51%) at a cost of 3-4 points a year, and they all had their worst year in 2022 (-13% to -19%) because long bonds fell with stocks; the one live risk-parity ETF, RPAR, compounded at **3.40%/yr** from Dec 2019 to Sep 2026 while VTI did 14.6% ([numbers Q3 replication](/home/user/portofolio/investing-research/research_notes/ETF%20long%20term%20and%20AI%20research%20tools/gapfill2_data/allweather_results.csv); [RPAR fund page](https://www.rparetf.com/rpar)). Factor tilts have long-run support but AVUV returned 14.1%/yr vs VTI's 17.1% over the three years to Jul 2026 at a 0.25% fee and a -49% maximum drawdown ([bestetf](https://www.bestetf.net/compare/VTI-vs-AVUV); [myplaniq AVUV](https://myplaniq.com/invest/quote/AVUV/)). Dividend screens lagged: SCHD trailed VTI by about 1.6-2 points a year since 2011, and for a Canadian the dividend is exactly the cash flow exposed to 15% US withholding ([etfsavant](https://etfsavant.com/schd-vs-vti/)). Daily-reset leveraged ETFs are flagged by FINRA as typically unsuitable beyond one session; TQQQ fell about 81% in 2022 against -33% for its index ([FINRA 09-31](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/09-31); [Nasdaq.com](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/should-investors-use-leveraged-etfs-or-avoid-them-altogether)). Trend overlays are treated in the signals section below, with the verdict that they belong in the report as a flag, not in the portfolio as a rule. Practitioners cap satellites at **5-20%** of a portfolio and warn the structure "is in no way a guarantee of outperformance"; a 5-10% satellite is small enough that a 50% loss in it costs 2.5-5% of the whole ([Morningstar Australia](https://www.morningstar.com.au/personal-finance/young-invested-can-this-investing-strategy-help-you-beat-market)).

## Invest on receipt, rebalance with deposits, fill the TFSA first (R4, R5)

The contribution and rebalancing evidence is primary and consistent. Vanguard's 2023 study of MSCI World data from 1976-2022 found lump-sum investing beat a three-month cost-averaging drip **68% of the time** and beat cash 70% of the time, with cost averaging only better in the worst 5% tail; for a monthly contributor this means each deposit goes in the day it lands, not after a dip ([Vanguard, Cost averaging](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/cost_averaging_invest_now_or_temporarily_hold_your_cash.pdf)). On rebalancing, Vanguard's December 2024 paper recommends a 200 bps threshold with a 175 bps "destination" and finds it worth 15-22 bps a year over monthly calendar rebalancing for an accumulator, while its 2022 paper says annual rebalancing is optimal for many investors and its 2025 perspective concedes the approaches converge before costs; Daryanani's 2008 study found a 20% relative band checked every two weeks optimal ("rebalance less frequently, but look more frequently"), which for a 25% sleeve is the "5% absolute" rule Questrade and Wealthsimple publish ([Vanguard, The Rebalancing Edge](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/the_rebalancing_edge_optimizing_target_date_fund_rebalancing_through_threshold_based_strategies.pdf); [Vanguard Workplace, Jan 2025](https://workplace.vanguard.com/insights-and-research/perspective/vanguards-approach-to-target-date-fund-rebalancing.html); [Kitces on Daryanani](https://www.kitces.com/blog/best-opportunistic-rebalancing-frequency-time-horizons-vs-tolerance-band-thresholds/); [Questrade](https://www.questrade.com/learning/stocks-etfs/rebalancing-investment-portfolios)). With monthly inflows the first lever is to direct each deposit to the most underweight sleeve, which kept the DIY mix within 0.5 points of target over 141 months with zero sales in the gap-fill backtest ([tech Q2](/home/user/portofolio/investing-research/data/verify/t2_backtest_output.md)). An all-in-one ETF removes the job entirely, and behaviour matters more than mechanics: fewer than 0.5% of Vanguard's self-directed investors went to cash in Feb-May 2020, and more than 80% of those who did were worse off by the end of May ([Vanguard, Cash panickers](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/Cash-panickers-Coronavirus-market-volatility-US-CVMV_072020_online.pdf)).

### Account placement and withholding tax decide more than the MER

Canadian contribution room for 2026 is **TFSA $7,000, RRSP $33,810** (18% of 2025 earned income, less pension adjustments), **FHSA $8,000/yr to $40,000 lifetime**, with the TFSA cumulative room for someone eligible since 2009 at $109,000 and a 1%/month penalty on excess ([CRA limits table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/registered-plans-administrators/pspa/mp-rrsp-dpsp-tfsa-limits-ympe.html); [CRA FHSA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/first-home-savings-account/contributing-your-fhsa.html)). Vanguard Canada's June 2025 guide gives the structural rule for foreign withholding tax: a US-listed ETF held directly in an RRSP pays no US level-1 tax, but every Canadian-listed structure (direct or wrap) pays level 1 in both RRSP and TFSA, and a Canadian ETF that reaches international stocks through a US-listed ETF pays a second, unrecoverable level in registered accounts ([Vanguard Canada withholding-tax guide](https://www.vanguard.ca/content/dam/intl/americas/canada/en/documents/INAEE_CA_062025_WithholdingTax_Guide.pdf)). Applied to today's weights and yields, that drag is about **15 bps/yr for XEQT or VEQT in a TFSA or RRSP** (the Canadian Portfolio Manager calculator, via a May 2025 blog, says 22 bps; treat 15-22 as the range) and 0-2 bps in a taxable account; a DIY Canadian-listed mix does not reduce it at all because XUU and VUN wrap US funds exactly as XEQT and VEQT do ([egypt KQ2 table](/home/user/portofolio/investing-research/research_notes/ETF%20long%20term%20and%20AI%20research%20tools/gapfill2_egypt_canada_accounts.md); [Tawcan XEQT review](https://www.tawcan.com/xeqt-review/)). The only structure that cuts it is US-listed VTI and VXUS held directly in an RRSP, worth roughly 8-9 bps of withholding plus 10-12 bps of MER, about **20 bps/yr**, which at Wealthsimple's 1.5% FX fee takes about seven years to pay back per converted dollar and at IBKR's 0.20 bp (US$2 minimum) pays back within a year ([Wealthsimple pricing](https://www.wealthsimple.com/en-ca/pricing); [IBKR Canada FX commissions](https://www.interactivebrokers.ca/en/pricing/commissions-spot-currencies.php)). Holding only Canadian-listed ETFs also keeps a non-registered account out of Form T1135 scope, which otherwise applies once the cost of US-listed holdings exceeds $100,000 ([CRA T1135 Q&A](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/information-been-moved/foreign-reporting/questions-answers-about-form-t1135.html)). The practical order for this investor is therefore TFSA (XEQT or VEQT), then FHSA if a first home is plausible, then RRSP (same fund, with a VTI/VXUS switch only if the balance is large and the broker is IBKR or the conversion uses Norbert's Gambit), then taxable.

### The Egypt tie changes nothing unless residence changes

Egypt matters in two scenarios, and the primary sources now read (the 1983 Canada-Egypt convention's synthesised text, Deloitte's 2025 highlights, PwC's August 2026 summaries, IBKR's country list) separate them cleanly. In **Scenario A**, a factual Canadian resident with Egyptian family ties, no permanent home in Egypt and under 183 days a year there, Egyptian tax residence is not triggered, non-residents are taxed only on Egyptian-source income, and Canadian-held ETFs are outside Egyptian scope entirely ([Deloitte Egypt Highlights 2025](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-shared/docs/services/tax/2025/dttl-tax-egypthighlights-2025.pdf); [PwC Egypt residence](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/individual/residence)). In **Scenario B**, 183+ days in Egypt in any 12-month period or a permanent home there, Egyptian residence is triggered under domestic law, the treaty's Article 4(2) tie-breaker (permanent home, then centre of vital interests, habitual abode, nationality) decides which country is home, and Egypt taxes foreign-source income only if Egypt is the person's "centre of commercial, industrial or professional activities", a test the regulator-facing pages leave undefined and an EY summary describes as where decisions are taken and vital interests lie; if in scope, foreign gains are taxed at progressive rates up to 27.5% with a credit for Canadian tax under Article 22(4), and the treatment of foreign dividends is stated nowhere found ([Egyptian Tax Authority synthesised treaty text](https://eta.gov.eg/sites/default/files/2024-09/canda.pdf); [PwC income determination](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/individual/income-determination); [EY alert](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/egypt-introduces-amendments-to-the-income-tax-law)). Because Article 13 has no residual "other property" clause, the treaty does not bar Egypt from taxing a Canadian treaty-resident's gains; for Canadian-listed ETFs this is moot in Scenario A. The plan should therefore encode one trigger: consult an adviser before spending 183 days in Egypt or acquiring an Egyptian home.

For an Egypt-resident family member who wants the same strategy, the options are materially worse and now documented: Egypt appears on IBKR's own "Available Countries and Territories" list (read 2026-11-02, entity unconfirmed); FRA Decision 37/2014 still bars local brokers from dealing in foreign unlisted securities for residents with no reversing decision found through 2026; Thndr routes its US-market wallet through an ADGM-licensed entity; the EGX has one listed ETF (Beltone's EGX30 ETF at EGP 55.49 on Nov 2, 2026, 47,599 units traded, AUM unpublished) plus nine open-ended gold and silver funds holding EGP 9.35 billion at end-June 2026; and since Law 151/2026 (effective July 29, 2026) gains on EGX-listed securities are exempt from capital gains tax while listed dividends carry 5% withholding ([IBKR Canada country list](https://www.interactivebrokers.ca/en/accounts/open-account-country-list.php); [Daily News Egypt on Decision 37/2014](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/2014/03/05/egypt-lifts-ban-brokerage-firms-trading-foreign-stocks/); [StockAnalysis EGX30ETF](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/egx/EGX30ETF/); [Arab Finance on gold funds](https://arabfinance.com/en/news/newdetails/egypt-gold-silver-investment-fund-assets-rise-end-june); [PwC corporate income determination](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/corporate/income-determination)). On moving money, the CBE's US$100,000 cap on individual transfers abroad was removed in June 2017 and a September 2026 law-firm note describes no general prohibition, only AML source-of-funds checks; no written rule on outward portfolio transfers was found ([Egyptian Streets 2017](https://egyptianstreets.com/2017/06/15/egypts-central-bank-removes-limits-on-currency-transfers/); [Legal500 / Youssry Saleh](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/egypt/finance-and-banking/how-to-get-your-money-out-of-egypt-legally)).

### Three independent evidence streams say the edge is not there

The reason bots "work for a month and break the next" has three documented mechanisms, and none is fixable by a retail user. The first is selection bias in the backtest. Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu showed that trying a modest number of configurations is enough to produce a strong-looking backtest, that the number tried is almost never reported, and that with five years of data and more than about 45 configurations an in-sample Sharpe of 1 with an expected out-of-sample Sharpe of 0 is almost guaranteed; Harvey, Liu and Zhu's conclusion from hundreds of tested factors is that "most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false" and that a new factor should clear a t-statistic above 3 ([Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/40/); [Probability of Backtest Overfitting](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); [NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). The second is decay. McLean and Pontiff found across 97 published predictors that returns were **26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication**, and Falck, Rej and Thesmar found each additional year of publication adds about 5 points to a factor's Sharpe decay ([McLean & Pontiff 2016](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf); [arXiv 2105.01380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01380)). The third is the base rate for the humans running them. SEBI's study of over ten million Indian derivatives traders found **93% lost money** over FY22-FY24 with 97% of foreign-investor profits and 96% of proprietary-desk profits coming from algorithmic trading, which is to say the counterparties a retail bot trades against; in Brazil only 1.1% of persistent day traders earned more than minimum wage; in Taiwan fewer than 1% earned reliably positive abnormal returns; and the BIS found that over four-fifths of simulated retail bitcoin dollar-cost averagers from 2015-2022 had lost money by December 2022 ([SEBI PR 22/2024](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2024/1727086965496.pdf); [Chague, De-Losso, Giovannetti](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf); [BIS Bulletin 69](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull69.pdf)).

Adding a language model to the loop has not changed this in 2025-2026. StockBench, a contamination-free benchmark that let thirteen LLM agents trade the twenty largest Dow stocks from March to June 2025, found the best agent (Kimi-K2) returned **1.9% against 0.4% for buy-and-hold**, the worst lost 2.8%, every agent underperformed passive during the January-April 2025 downturn and every agent outperformed in the May-August rally, a bullish bias rather than skill; the abstract concludes "most models struggle to outperform the simple buy-and-hold baseline" ([arXiv 2510.02209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02209)). TradeTrap showed that small perturbations to one component of an LLM trading agent propagate into extreme concentration and runaway exposure, so the agents "can be systematically misled at the system level" ([arXiv 2512.02261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02261)). In nof1's real-money Alpha Arena (crypto perpetuals, Oct 18-Nov 3, 2025, US$10,000 per model) four of six models lost money and GPT-5 lost more than 62%, which the organisers themselves called exploratory ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)). TradingAgents' own paper admits that about eleven LLM calls and twenty-plus tool calls per decision limited its backtest to three months, which is why long-horizon evidence for these agents barely exists ([arXiv 2412.20138v7](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138v7)). For a monthly contributor whose core already compounds at the market rate, the only thing autonomy adds is a documented way to lose it.

### What survives from the bot repos is the scaffolding, not the strategies

The reusable material is architectural. NautilusTrader's kernel separates a DataEngine, a RiskEngine that validates every order before it reaches an ExecutionEngine, and a Portfolio that derives exposure from events, with backtest, sandbox and live sharing the same core and only the data source and execution client swapped; a monitor keeps that bus but renames the stages **Data, Analyzers (deterministic Python), Policy gate, Report**, and makes "real data, simulated execution" the permanent mode ([NautilusTrader architecture](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/latest/concepts/architecture/)). freqtrade's `IStrategy` contract (a `startup_candle_count` warm-up, `populate_*` methods that may not touch future rows, an enumerated list of look-ahead leaks such as negative `shift` and whole-column aggregates) becomes an `Analyzer` protocol, and the trend-allocation-research-bot's test that mutates future prices and asserts outputs are unchanged, with a planted leak that must fail, is the cheapest anti-lookahead guarantee found ([freqtrade strategy customization](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/strategy-customization/); [trend-allocation-research-bot](https://github.com/aidaman01/trend-allocation-research-bot)). Risk limits turn into policy checks: freqtrade's `SpreadFilter` and `VolatilityFilter` screen candidate ETFs, richfolio's drift bands replace position limits, agentic-trading-desk's "halt if data does not reconcile" and ALTA's explicit "Wait, insufficient evidence" outcome replace circuit breakers, and a MaxDrawdown-style rule changes who is told rather than what is traded ([freqtrade plugins](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/plugins/); [richfolio](https://github.com/furic/richfolio); [agentic-trading-desk](https://github.com/Oft3r/agentic-trading-desk); [ALTA](https://github.com/kyky2347/ALTA)). The cost conventions to port are 0.05% per side (QuantDinger and the trend bot both use it, though Canadian spreads should be measured), a blind holdout period, and a Deflated Sharpe and PBO report whenever more than a handful of variants are tried ([QuantDinger README](https://github.com/OpenByteInc/QuantDinger)).

From TradingAgents, read at source level, the parts worth lifting into a never-trades monitor are `PortfolioContext` (a broker-neutral book with a fingerprint so a resumed run cannot use a stale one), `rating.py` with its `REVIEW` sentinel ("a decision nobody can read is not a Hold"), the Pydantic schemas minus the trader proposal, and `reporting.write_report_tree`, which writes a numbered tree of analyst, research, risk and portfolio markdown files with a provenance header naming the date, models, data vendors and debate rounds. What should not be reused is the risk layer itself: the three risk "debators" and the portfolio manager are prompt-only LangGraph nodes with no deterministic exposure, drawdown or sizing logic, and `backtest.py` scores single-stock ratings against later prices rather than simulating a portfolio ([tech Q3 module map](/home/user/portofolio/investing-research/research_notes/ETF%20long%20term%20and%20AI%20research%20tools/gapfill2_technical_verification.md)). Vibe-Trading's "grounding gate", which strips any model-stated figure that does not match a tool result, and agentic-trading-desk's rule that "the scripts perform the deterministic calculations; the user decides" are the two design principles that follow directly from StockBench and TradeTrap: the LLM is the least reliable component, so it narrates and collects evidence while Python owns every number ([Vibe-Trading README](https://github.com/HKUDS/Vibe-Trading); [agentic-trading-desk](https://github.com/Oft3r/agentic-trading-desk)).

### Signals: sentiment is context, trend is a flag, nothing is a trigger (R11)

The signal evidence divides cleanly into what a report may show and what it may act on. News and LLM sentiment predict single-stock returns for one to two trading days at magnitudes only market makers can harvest: Lopez-Lira and Tang's GPT-4 long-short earned 34 bps/day before costs, but the authors say exploiting it "is only feasible for market participants whose transaction costs are sufficiently low", its annualized Sharpe fell from 6.54 in late 2021 to 1.22 by early 2024, and Gao, Jiang and Yan attribute about 37% of the apparent effect to training-data memorization ([arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619); [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847v1)). FinBERT's pre-registered effect of +9.7 bps per unit sentiment was not significant (p = 0.30) and "far below cost at every horizon" ([news-sentiment-research](https://github.com/NakulPillai2407/news-sentiment-research)). Macro regime indicators have strong recession records and recent false alarms: the 2s10s inversion of July 2022 became the longest on record without a recession, the Sahm rule's July 2024 trigger is "the first false signal since 1970", and AQR warns against using the yield curve "as a market timing indicator" ([eco3min Sahm history](https://eco3min.fr/en/sahm-rule-false-signals-history/); [AQR Inversion Anxiety](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking-3Q19-Inversion-Anxiety-Yield-Curves-Economic-Growth-and-Asset-Prices.pdf?sc_lang=en)). CAPE has ten-year predictive content, yet Vanguard's own test shows raw CAPE's out-of-sample error since 1985 (7.8%) was worse than a naive historical mean (6.2%) unless adjusted for real yields ([Vanguard fair-value CAPE](https://mebfaber.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Improving-U.S.-Stock-Return-Forecasts.pdf)).

### Backtesting: pandas plus skfolio, with bt for cash flows (R12)

For a handful of ETFs rebalanced monthly with contributions, none of the event-driven engines earns its weight: backtrader has had no release since April 2023, zipline-reloaded 3.2.1 is heavy, Lean needs Docker and a paid tier for local CLI use, and mlfinlab is now closed-source ([PyPI backtrader](https://pypi.org/project/backtrader/); [quantconnect.com/pricing](https://www.quantconnect.com/pricing); [mlfinlab](https://github.com/hudson-and-thames/mlfinlab)). The verified choice is a plain-pandas engine (the ~100-line routine behind Table C, which already handles CAD contributions, FX and separate XIRR/TWR) with `bt 1.4.1` as the library alternative, because `bt.algos.CapitalFlow(amount)` models contributions as flows that do not distort the price index, whereas open-source vectorbt 1.2.0 exposes no `cash_deposits` parameter anywhere in the installed package (that feature lives in the paid PRO edition) and its Apache-plus-Commons-Clause licence bars selling anything built on it ([tech Q2](/home/user/portofolio/investing-research/research_notes/ETF%20long%20term%20and%20AI%20research%20tools/gapfill2_technical_verification.md); [bt docs](https://pmorissette.github.io/bt/)). The validation layer no longer needs mlfinlab: `skfolio 1.12.5` ships `WalkForward`, `CombinatorialPurgedCV`, `probabilistic_sharpe_ratio`, `deflated_sharpe_ratio` and `expected_max_sharpe_ratio` natively ([skfolio API](https://skfolio.org/api.html)). The controls to hard-code are a frozen publication-date split (Faber 2007, Antonacci 2014) with no tuning after it, walk-forward or purged CV with at least one rebalance period of embargo, a Deflated Sharpe fed the actual number of variants tried, Harvey-Liu-Zhu's t > 3 hurdle, and stress runs with a one-month execution delay and with both cash and bonds as the defensive asset (a two-month delay cut the SMA rule's CAGR from 8.6% to 7.0% in one replication) ([sp500-trend-filter-backtest](https://github.com/lotushansstudio-maker/sp500-trend-filter-backtest)). For survivorship-free long history, testfol.io's simulated tickers reach back to 1885 (US only) and Portfolio Visualizer's asset-class series start in 1972 with Canadian data from Statistics Canada and the Bank of Canada; the free PV tier is capped at 15 assets and $30/month buys the Basic plan ([testfol.io/help](https://testfol.io/help); [Portfolio Visualizer pricing](https://www.portfoliovisualizer.com/pricing)).

### Scheduling, delivery and cost (R14)

Four schedulers are viable and the documentation now says what each requires. Claude Code cloud scheduled tasks (research preview; Pro, Max, Ultra, Team, Enterprise) run a prompt against a fresh clone of the repo with a one-hour minimum interval, UTC cron unless prefixed `CRON_TZ=`, a few minutes past the hour to avoid late starts, push to a `claude/`-prefixed branch unless the prompt names another, and draw down subscription usage with "no separate charge for the cloud VM" ([web-scheduled-tasks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/web-scheduled-tasks)). The catch found in the gap-fill is the network: the Default environment's "Trusted" access allows a 204-domain allowlist of Anthropic, GitHub, package-registry and cloud-provider hosts with no `query1.finance.yahoo.com`, `fred.stlouisfed.org`, `bankofcanada.ca` or `blackrock.com`, so a data-fetching task needs a Custom allowlist (one domain per line, wildcards allowed) and any vendor key belongs in a "network secret", which Anthropic's proxy attaches outside the VM, rather than an environment variable that "anyone who uses the environment can read" ([cloud-environments](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cloud-environments)). A workable spec is `CRON_TZ=America/Toronto 7 17 * * 1-5` for the daily note after the TSX close and `17 13 1 * *` for the monthly review, with a GitHub ruleset restricting the run's push rights to a `reports` branch ([tech Q5](/home/user/portofolio/investing-research/research_notes/ETF%20long%20term%20and%20AI%20research%20tools/gapfill2_technical_verification.md)). Desktop scheduled tasks run only while the app is open and the machine awake and give exactly one catch-up run for a missed fire, so the prompt must check the date; `/loop` and `CronCreate` need an open session and expire after seven days ([desktop-scheduled-tasks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/desktop-scheduled-tasks.md); [scheduled-tasks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/scheduled-tasks)). GitHub Actions `schedule:` is documented as best-effort, "can be delayed... some queued jobs may be dropped", is disabled after 60 days of inactivity on public repos, and the one real ETF-digest project found (richfolio) abandoned it after runs "ran 5-8h late and dropped runs" in favour of a free Cloudflare Worker hitting `workflow_dispatch` ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/writing-workflows/choosing-when-your-workflow-runs/events-that-trigger-workflows#schedule); [richfolio](https://github.com/furic/richfolio)). Local cron on an always-on machine calling `claude -p --bare --permission-prompts none --max-budget-usd 1 --output-format json` is the simplest of all and the only one that records cost per run.

Delivery limits are confirmed for Telegram (about one message per second per chat, 20 per minute in groups, 50 MB uploads), Discord webhooks (2,000 characters, 10 embeds, file upload) and Gmail (`messages.send` costs 100 quota units of a 6,000-per-minute per-user budget), and committing Markdown to the repo is the lowest-friction store because cloud tasks already clone and push it ([Telegram Bots FAQ](https://core.telegram.org/bots/faq); [Discord webhook docs](https://docs.discord.com/developers/resources/webhook); [Gmail API quota](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/quota)). Idempotency patterns from the sources are to key each run by trade date and skip if `reports/daily/YYYY-MM-DD.md` exists, cache raw vendor JSON per date before any LLM call so re-runs do not re-spend quota, write a run manifest with `total_cost_usd` and provider status, and mark a report "degraded" when a provider fails rather than failing the run ([ai R14 inferences](/home/user/portofolio/investing-research/research_notes/ETF%20long%20term%20and%20AI%20research%20tools/ai_tooling_R8_R9_R14.md)).

## The toolkit's risks are licences, hallucination and injection, not market risk (R15)

The regulatory and operational evidence points the whole design toward read-only, personal, human-in-the-loop use. On licensing, Yahoo's terms prohibit collecting data "using any automated means" and any commercial use, yfinance's own README says the API "is intended for personal use only", Alpha Vantage's licence turns "commercial" the moment information is provided to anyone other than the user "directly or indirectly", Tiingo's plans are "Internal Use Only", and the TSX non-professional agreement excludes anyone using market data "for any purpose for profit outside trading a personal account" with back-billing at professional rates for misclassification ([Yahoo ToS](https://legal.yahoo.com/us/en/yahoo/terms/otos/index.html); [Alpha Vantage ToS](https://www.alphavantage.co/terms_of_service/); [TSX non-professional agreement](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/data/CEGea.pdf)). Nothing derived from the free tiers may be published, shared or sold; the moment a dashboard reaches another person, the project needs a business-tier feed. On registration, Companion Policy 31-103CP's business trigger (activities similar to a registrant, intermediating trades, repetition for others, remuneration, solicitation) is not met by an individual producing research for their own TFSA and RRSP, and the line is crossed by publishing advice, monetising it or holding oneself out as an adviser; the quoted text is the 2014 consolidation, with the current CP effective 2026-01-01 unread ([31-103CP consolidated 2014](https://www.fcnb.ca/sites/default/files/2020-02/31-103-CP-CONS-2014-09-22-E.pdf); [BCSC 31-103 index](https://www.bcsc.bc.ca/securities-law/law-and-policy/instruments-and-policies/3-registration-requirements-related-matters/current/31-103)).

On accuracy, every LLM-produced number must be reproduced by deterministic code: GPT-4-Turbo with retrieval answered incorrectly or refused **81% of FinanceBench questions** in 2023, and on the Vals AI Finance Agent benchmark the best 2026 scores are about 60-64% (Claude Opus 4.7 at 64.37% in June 2026), so roughly 36-40% of expert finance tasks still fail ([arXiv 2311.11944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11944); [benchlm.ai](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/valsfinanceagentv11)). Regulators say the same in plainer words: AI output "can be inaccurate, outdated, manipulated, or fabricated" ([FINRA alert](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/artificial-intelligence-and-investment-fraud)). On security, Claude Code's documentation stores credentials in the macOS Keychain or a 0600 file on Linux, does not auto-approve network commands, states that MCP servers are not security-audited by Anthropic, and recommends running scripts that touch external web services in virtual machines; OWASP's 2025 LLM Top 10 (prompt injection, sensitive information disclosure, excessive agency) applies to every fetched page, README and MCP tool description, which the toolkit must treat as untrusted input ([Claude Code security](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/security); [OWASP GenAI](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/)). The design rules that follow are: no broker credentials in the agent's context at all; any data key in a secrets manager, a network secret or the OS keychain, scoped read-only; an outbound allowlist; and every output labelled research, not recommendation.

The scam landscape is a separate reason to keep "AI trading" out of the plan's vocabulary. The SEC, NASAA and FINRA joint alert of January 2024 lists unregistered platforms promoting AI trading systems ("Our proprietary AI trading system can't lose!") and guaranteed returns as red flags; SEC enforcement in 2025-2026 covered about $15 million (TSAI and Cryptoaiml, September 2025), over $14 million (December 2025) and $12.3 million from about 150 investors in fabricated AI bots (May 2026); the CSA's June 2025 release warns that "imagery, video and voices can be easily generated with AI to look and sound real"; and New York's DFS counted over $8 billion in reported US investment-scam losses in 2025, up 38% ([FINRA copy of the alert](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/artificial-intelligence-and-investment-fraud); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/sec-charges-texas-man-with-123m-crypto-fraud-fake-ai-trading-bots); [FCNB/CSA](https://fcnb.ca/en/news-alerts/canadian-securities-administrators-encourage-canadians-to-look-out-for-deceptive-social-media); [NY DFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/reports_and_publications/press_releases/pr20260826)). Survivorship and backfill bias complete the list: Research Affiliates found 125 smart-beta indices averaged 2.8%/yr backtested excess return but 0.5%/yr live over ten years, "insignificantly different from zero", with two-thirds of their track records being backtests, and an Aalto study found a median 73% Sharpe deterioration from backtest to live; the rule for any AI-suggested tilt is to use only live post-launch history and discount anything mostly backtested ([ETF.com on Research Affiliates](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/research-affiliates-live-newport-beach-its-smart-beta); [Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/product/backtests-of-smart-beta-indices-can-be-misleading-report/)).

## Conclusion

The research collapses a large question into a small plan. The portfolio part is close to solved by a single product because the three strongest findings in the literature, an all-equity lifecycle allocation of roughly one-third domestic, a 30/70 Canadian home bias, and threshold rebalancing done for you, are already what XEQT and VEQT implement at 0.19-0.22%; the investor's real decisions are account order, invest-on-receipt discipline, and refusing to let a -50% year change the rule. The one structural edge left on the table, about 20 bps a year from US-listed funds inside an RRSP, is worth taking only at IBKR or via Norbert's Gambit and only once the RRSP balance makes the conversion cost trivial.

The toolkit part inverts the usual AI-finance pitch. Every piece of evidence about autonomy points the same way: the edge is not there, the backtests that say otherwise are selected, and the language model is the least reliable component in any pipeline that contains one. What the open-source ecosystem does offer, for free and verified on 2026-11-02, is excellent plumbing: a keyless TSX data path, a contribution-aware backtest that already matches the issuer's numbers, MCP servers for macro and filings, and report scaffolding lifted from the very repos that should not be allowed to trade. The resulting monitor costs the price of a Claude Pro subscription, writes a Markdown note after each TSX close and a review each month, shows trend and sentiment signals beside their own failure record, and ends every run with a human decision. Its two open questions, the measured cost of a live run and the behaviour of free data from a shared cloud egress, are the first two milestones of the plan.
