### The gating question: "Am I a factual non-resident of Canada?"

The plan has two branches and one question, answered in writing in `plan/changes.md` during Phase 1 (P14) before any money moves. The question is whether the owner is a **factual non-resident of Canada** under the residential-ties test of Income Tax Folio S5-F1-C1: no dwelling in Canada kept available for the owner's use, no spouse or common-law partner in Canada, no dependants in Canada (the three ties that "will almost always be significant", ¶1.11); secondary ties (bank accounts, driver's licence, provincial health card, memberships) rarely suffice on their own (¶1.14); there is no minimum time abroad (¶1.17); and non-residency begins on the date all ties are severed (¶1.22). The treaty route is closed: the Canada-UAE Convention's Article 4(1)(b)(i) defines a UAE-resident individual as "an individual who is a national of the United Arab Emirates", so a Canadian citizen on a UAE residence visa is never "a resident of both Contracting States", the Article 4(3) tie-breaker and the s.250(5) deemed-non-resident rule never engage, and the treaty's 15% dividend cap and residence-only taxation of gains are unavailable (UAE notes Q1, Justice Laws C-10.55). The CRA's NR73 opinion is optional and non-binding (Folio ¶1.54-1.55); the owner may instead document the ties severed and confirm the facts with a cross-border accountant (P4 step 1).

- **Branch A, factual non-resident (the expected case):** the owner pays no tax anywhere on investment income and gains from non-Canadian funds (UAE notes Q2); Canadian-source income is withheld at the statutory 25% Part XIII rate with no treaty reduction; the TFSA and RRSP are kept but not contributed to (P4 step 3). The primary portfolio below applies.
- **Branch B, still a factual resident because of ties:** the owner is taxed in Canada on worldwide income while living in the UAE, the TFSA, RRSP and FHSA keep their room and their shelter, and the **v4 plan stands unchanged** (XEQT in FHSA/TFSA/RRSP at Wealthsimple, `plan/plan_v4.md` P2-P4, with the USD contribution converted to CAD at the broker). Because Branch B means Canadian tax on UAE salary with no treaty relief and a residency position that can be challenged either way, a **cross-border adviser is engaged immediately**, before the first buy, not at the P4 adviser triggers.

**Why an Irish-domiciled accumulating all-world UCITS ETF.** For a non-resident of Canada with no home-country tax, the only costs that differ between wrappers are withholding and estate exposure, and the UAE notes' drag table (Q4, 2026-11-03) prices them: the Irish UCITS route costs about **34-40 bps/yr all-in** (TER 12-15 bps, US dividends withheld at the US-Ireland treaty rate of 15% inside the fund, about 11 bps, non-US level-1 about 11 bps, nothing on distributions because the fund accumulates and Ireland withholds nothing from non-resident holders), against about **65-80 bps/yr for US-listed VT** and **55-80 bps/yr for XEQT held through IBKR as a non-resident**. The UCITS wrapper is non-US-situs, so there is no US estate-tax exposure, and non-Canadian, so there is no Part XIII. It is accumulating, so there is no DRIP, no residual cash and no withholding event to record (UAE notes Q4 inference). Both candidates hold a global cap-weighted basket (FTSE All-World 3,821 stocks; MSCI ACWI), which is the same market-cap, all-equity, no-home-bias exposure the report's allocation findings support (Anarkulova-Cederburg-O'Doherty, Vanguard Canada 30/70, report "Alternatives to the plain core"), minus the Canadian home bias that v4's XEQT carried for a Canada-based owner and that a UAE-based owner with USD earnings has no reason to keep.

**Why VWRA primary and SPYY/ACWD the alternative.** SPYY is the cheaper line (TER 0.12% vs 0.14%, about 34 vs 36 bps all-in in the notes' table) and is the choice for an owner who weighs 2 bps above everything else. VWRA is chosen as primary for three reasons from the notes: its share class holds about US$62bn (justETF EUR 55.7bn; about US$75.7bn at fund level per a secondary source) against US$22.6bn for the whole SPYY fund, it is the line the notes describe as having the deepest LSE USD liquidity (no spread data was measured, UAE notes Q4 gap, so this is the notes' characterisation, not a figure), and its TER was cut to 0.14% on 28 July 2026 so the gap to SPYY is 2 bps, inside the uncertainty of the drag estimate's own yield and withholding assumptions (Assumption 23). The two are interchangeable in every rule below; the choice is made once at setup, recorded in `config/portfolio.yaml`, and is not a rotation. FWRA (Invesco, 0.15%, EUR 4.2bn, launched 2023) is acceptable but fails the P5 stability constraint on size and record relative to the two above; IWDA plus EIMI costs about 0.20% weighted and adds a manual rebalance for nothing (UAE notes Q4).

**Why not VT (US-listed).** With no US-UAE income-tax treaty, every VT distribution is withheld at 30% (about 57 bps/yr on a 1.9% yield) and the holding is US-situs property, so an estate above US$60,000 of US assets owes a Form 706-NA filing and up to 40% marginal estate tax with no treaty relief (UAE notes Q4(a), IRS pages). The 6 bps expense-ratio advantage cannot recover that, and the notes' all-in estimate is about 75 bps/yr before the estate risk.

**Why not XEQT through IBKR.** As a non-resident the owner loses every reason v4 chose XEQT: income distributions are withheld at 25% Part XIII with no treaty cut (about 36 bps/yr on the income share of its 1.61% yield), the fund's own look-through foreign withholding (about 15 bps) stays uncreditable, and the holding is CAD-denominated for an owner who earns and spends in USD-pegged AED (UAE notes Q4(c): 55-80 bps/yr against 34-40 bps). XEQT remains the right fund only inside a TFSA or RRSP, which is Branch B.

**Why 100% equity and no bond sleeve** is unchanged from v4: the report prices the defensive designs at 3-4 pts/yr of CAGR (All Weather, Golden Butterfly 6.4-7.4% vs 10.6% for US equity, Jul 2007-Oct 2026), RPAR at 3.40%/yr live, and a 20% bond sleeve would have lost 11.8% in 2022 alongside stocks (XBB, Table B); the lifecycle study says a target-date investor needs 61% more savings to match all-equity. The price is M3: a 50-65% drawdown must be survivable, a behavioural constraint handled in P3 and P5. **Phase 1 gate (P14 milestone 1), not an assumption:** before the first buy the owner writes in `plan/changes.md` that the horizon is 15+ years, that no withdrawal is planned within 5 years, that an emergency fund of 3-6 months of expenses sits outside this plan (in Branch A, in a UAE bank account or Sarwa Save, P4 step 5), and what they did in 2020 and 2022; if any of the first three fails, or the last answer is "sold" or "never invested", the **80/20 fallback** is the core from the first buy.

**Branch A 80/20 fallback (the XGRO equivalent).** 80% VWRA (or SPYY) plus 20% of an Irish-domiciled accumulating global aggregate bond UCITS ETF on the LSE USD line: **AGGU** (iShares Core Global Aggregate Bond UCITS ETF, USD hedged, accumulating) or **VAGU** (Vanguard Global Aggregate Bond UCITS ETF, USD hedged, accumulating). ## P3 Contribution and rebalancing rules

Rules 1 and 4-7 apply to both branches. Rule 2 is the Branch A procedure; Branch B uses v4's Rule 2 (Wealthsimple recurring XEQT buy, `plan/plan_v4.md` P3) unchanged.

**Rule 1, invest on receipt.** Each monthly deposit (about **US$2,500**, Assumption 1) is invested on the first trading day on or after it lands in the IBKR USD balance, never held for a dip. Evidence: lump sum beat a 3-month drip 68% of the time and cash 70% of the time, 1976-2022 (report R4 section). The only exception is the deposit landing on a non-trading day; LSE holidays (not TSX or NYSE) are the relevant calendar in Branch A.

**Rule 2, the monthly procedure (Branch A, about 10 minutes).**
1. On payday, transfer the fixed amount from the UAE bank (or from Sarwa Save if that is the cash buffer, P4 step 5) to the IBKR account **in USD**. If the transfer can only arrive in AED, convert once at IBKR: spot FX at 0.20 bp of trade value with a USD 2.00 minimum (UAE notes Q3, IBKR FX page), which on US$2,500 is the USD 2 minimum, 8 bps of that month's deposit and immaterial on assets. Never route the deposit through Sarwa Invest or a bank FX desk; the IBKR conversion is the only FX in the plan.
2. **Recurring order if eligible, otherwise a manual order.** IBKR Recurring Investments runs on "available US, Canadian and European shares" daily, weekly or monthly using fractional shares (UAE notes Q3), but whether VWRA (or ACWD) is in the eligible set is unverified (Assumption 22). Phase 1 tests it: search the ticker in the Recurring Investments tool. If eligible, set a monthly recurring buy of the deposit amount on the LSE USD line and the owner's only task is confirming the cash arrived. If not eligible, place one **market order on the USD line during LSE hours** (08:00-16:30 London, 11:00-19:30 or 12:00-20:30 Dubai depending on UK daylight time) for the largest whole number of shares the deposit covers; at roughly USD 150-160 per VWRA share (note-level, unverified) the residual is under USD 160 and rolls into next month's buy. The commission on either route under **Tiered pricing** is 0.05% of trade value with a USD 1.70 minimum, so a US$2,500 buy costs USD 1.70 (0.068%, about **6.8 bps** of the deposit; the Fixed schedule would cost USD 4.00, 16 bps, so Tiered is the setting, UAE notes Q3); LSE exchange and clearing pass-throughs are additional and unquantified (small). Over a year that is about USD 20 against USD 30,000 contributed.
3. Record the trade (date, account, ticker, `kind=buy`, **USD amount**, units, the broker's confirmed fill price, `ccy=USD`) in `etf-monitor/data/ledger.csv`, one row per buy (schema in P6). The fund accumulates, so there are **no `drip` rows and no `phantom` rows** in Branch A: no distribution is ever paid, and no Canadian T3 exists for an Irish fund. The ledger is the cost record the owner will need if Canadian residency is ever re-established (P4 step 7: the arrival-date FMV becomes the new cost base, but the pre-arrival ledger still evidences the holding period and the T1161 values). It is gitignored and never leaves the owner's machine (P8, P13); a copy lives in the owner's normal backup, not in the repository.
4. Do nothing else. The fund rebalances internally; nothing is sold, no DRIP is set, and no cash sleeve exists.

**Rule 2b, the 80/20 fallback procedure (Branch A, about 15 minutes).** Steps 1 and 3 as above. For step 2, direct the whole deposit to the more underweight of the two lines until it is back inside its band (equity 72-88%, bonds 16-24%, a 20% relative band on 80/20); the contribution-only rule kept v4's DIY mix within 0.5 pts of target over 141 months with zero sales (report R4 section, Table C) and two lines are easier than four. Whole shares if fractional trading is not available on the line; the remainder carries to next month.

**Rule 3, drift bands (80/20 fallback only).** Check the bands at the monthly buy (the monitor computes them, P10); rebalance by selling only if a line is outside its band and two consecutive months of contributions cannot bring it back; annual calendar check each January regardless. A band repair is one of the permitted sale types under P5 guardrail (2); in Branch A a sale has no tax consequence anywhere (UAE notes Q2), so the only cost is the USD 1.70 commission, but the 48-hour cooling rule and the written entry in `plan/changes.md` still apply. In Branch B, v4's Rule 3 (DIY bands, registered accounts repaired first) applies unchanged.

**Rule 4, drawdown protocol.** A drawdown changes nothing in rules 1-3. When the core is 20% or more below its high-water mark (the monitor reports this, P10), the owner re-reads the P5 behavioural clause and the Table B stress numbers and confirms the next deposit. There is no other lever (v3's "bring forward up to 3 months of deposits" was struck in v4 as a timing rule), the emergency fund (3-6 months of expenses, confirmed at the Phase 1 gate) is never drawn into the portfolio, and no selling, no switching to a bond fund and no "waiting for the bottom": fewer than 0.5% of Vanguard's self-directed investors went to cash in Feb-May 2020 and more than 80% of those who did were worse off by the end of May (report R4 section), and an SMA exit on XEQT in April 2025 came after the trough and cost 6.5 pts (Table H text).

**Rule 5, distributions.** Branch A: none. VWRA and SPYY are accumulating share classes; the fund reinvests dividends net of the withholding it suffers (US 15% under the US-Ireland treaty, non-US L1 at treaty rates), and nothing reaches the holder, so there is no DRIP to enable, no cash to sweep and no slip to file (UAE notes Q4). If the owner ever holds the distributing VWRD instead, v4's Rule 5 (DRIP on, `drip` rows) applies, minus the Canadian `phantom` rows. Branch B: v4's Rule 5 unchanged (DRIP on; `drip` and yearly `phantom` rows in a taxable account).

**Rule 6, cash.** No cash sleeve inside the portfolio. Cash earmarked for the portfolio never sits more than one month uninvested at IBKR or at Sarwa; the emergency fund is a separate balance (P4 step 5) and is not counted.

**Rule 7, contribution changes.** Raise the monthly amount with income; never lower it in response to a market move. Any one-off lump sum (bonus, gift, the Sarwa Invest liquidation proceeds in Phase 1) is invested in full on receipt (Rule 1) in the same line.

## P4 Account, tax and platform setup

**Broker (Branch A).** The owner's **existing Interactive Brokers account**, on **Tiered** pricing: LSE USD-line stocks and ETFs at 0.05% of trade value, USD 1.70 minimum and USD 39.00 maximum per order; spot FX at 0.20 bp with a USD 2.00 minimum; no account minimum and no inactivity fee (UAE notes Q3, IBKR commission, FX and minimums pages read 2026-11-03). No other broker is opened in Branch A: Wealthsimple and Questrade are Canadian-resident platforms and the v4 broker-change ladder applies only to Branch B. Sarwa is exited as an investment platform (step 5). **Broker (Branch B):** Wealthsimple as in `plan/plan_v4.md` P4, with IBKR Canada in reserve for the RRSP US-listed option at C$250,000.

**Ordered setup steps (Branch A).**
1. **Confirm the residency facts and the date of departure.** Write in `plan/changes.md`: the date the last significant tie was severed (dwelling given up or no longer available, no spouse or partner or dependants in Canada), the secondary ties that remain (bank accounts, driver's licence, health card, memberships) and the plan to close those that can be closed, and the calendar-year day counts in Canada since departure (a non-resident who sojourns 183 days or more in a year is deemed resident for that year, Folio ¶1.32). This is the P2 gating answer. Confirm it with a **Canadian cross-border accountant** before the departure-year return is filed; filing NR73 is optional and its opinion is non-binding (Folio ¶1.54-1.55), so the default is to document rather than to ask. If the facts put the owner in Branch B, stop here and follow v4's P4 with the adviser engaged first.
2. **Departure-tax checklist** (UAE notes Q1, CRA "Dispositions of property for emigrants", 2026-01-20; "Leaving Canada", 2026-09-17). On the departure date the owner is deemed to have sold non-registered property at fair market value and reacquired it: this covers the Sarwa holdings and any IBKR or Canadian non-registered positions, but **not** the TFSA, RRSP or Canadian real property (excluded rights and interests, s.128.1(10); whether an FHSA is excluded is not on the CRA page, Assumption 24). File with the departure-year return: **T1161** if the total FMV of all property owned on departure exceeded **C$25,000** (cash, bank deposits and registered plans excluded; late-filing penalty C$25/day, minimum C$100, maximum C$2,500); **T1243** to compute the deemed gain to Schedule 3; and **T1244** to elect, by April 30 of the year after emigration, to defer the departure tax without interest until actual disposition (security required if the federal tax exceeds C$16,500). Capital-gains inclusion remains one-half (secondary, UAE notes Q6). Tell IBKR and any Canadian broker the new country of residence so Part XIII withholding and NR4 reporting are correct.
3. **TFSA: keep, never contribute while non-resident.** The balance keeps its Canadian tax exemption and the UAE taxes nothing, but no new room accrues for non-resident years and any contribution is taxed at **1% per month** until the entire non-resident contribution is withdrawn or residency resumes (CRA, 2025-10-14). The recurring buy inside the TFSA, if any, is cancelled on the day of departure. **RRSP: keep, no contributions** (no Canadian earned income); any withdrawal while non-resident is withheld at **25% with no treaty reduction** (CRA, 2026-01-29; UAE notes Q1), so the RRSP is left untouched until return or retirement. **FHSA:** if one exists, stop contributions on the same day; its emigration treatment is unverified (Assumption 24) and goes on the accountant's list. Record `tfsa_contributions_allowed: false`, `rrsp_contributions_allowed: false` and the departure date in `config/accounts.yaml` so the monitor flags any registered-account row in the ledger as REVIEW (P10).
4. **Confirm the IBKR entity and that the LSE USD line is orderable.** Read the contracting entity off the client agreement or a monthly statement (IB LLC, Interactive Brokers (U.K.) Limited DIFC branch, or IB Ireland; the UAE-equities release says "countries served by IB LLC" and the only UAE licence found is IB UK's DFSA branch of 28 Aug 2024, so the entity is an inference until read, UAE notes Q3, Assumption 21) and record it in `accounts.yaml`. Then **test the order ticket**: enter, without transmitting, a one-share order for VWRA on the LSE USD line and, separately, for VT on NYSE Arca. PRIIPs is an EU/UK retail rule, so a UAE-resident account under IB LLC should accept both; an account that blocks VT for want of a KID is under IB UK or IB Ireland, which changes nothing in the product choice (VWRA is the plan either way) but is worth knowing for the entity record. If the VWRA USD line itself is not orderable, the GBP line (VWRL/VWRP) with FX is the fallback and the FX cost is re-stated in P13; this did not occur in the notes and is not expected.
5. **Sarwa: liquidate Invest, move the cash to IBKR, optionally keep Save as the cash buffer.** Sarwa Invest costs 0.4-0.85%/yr (or US$7/month) plus the underlying ETFs' TERs against about 0.14% plus about USD 20/yr of commissions at IBKR, and no in-kind transfer out is documented (UAE notes Q5, Sarwa pricing page 2026-11-03, help-centre snippets). Withdraw the Invest balance (liquidation 1-2 business days, bank credit 3-5 business days, no Sarwa withdrawal fee; the bank's international-transfer fee of about US$25-50 and any AED/USD conversion are the real costs) and invest the proceeds at IBKR on receipt (P3 Rule 7). If the account is a legacy "Sarwa Classic (IB)" account custodied at Interactive Brokers, ask Sarwa whether the positions can be transferred to the owner's own IBKR account instead (unverified, Assumption 28). **Sarwa Save** (0.5%/yr, bank-yield pass-through, ADGM/FSRA-regulated) may be kept as the AED/USD emergency fund and monthly staging balance; nothing in the plan depends on it, and a UAE bank savings account is an equal substitute. Close Sarwa Invest once empty (balance under US$1, e-mail to Sarwa for a sole account).
6. **Egypt triggers: unchanged from v4.** The Egypt decision table and adviser trigger below carry over verbatim; Branch A adds nothing to them, because an Irish fund bought on the LSE has no Egyptian source and the owner's Egyptian tax status still turns on 183 days or a dwelling available for continuous use (report "The Egypt tie"; UAE notes decision table citing gapfill2 Q1).
7. **Re-entry to Canada, triggered "the month residency is re-established".** On the arrival date the CRA deems every property owned to be sold and reacquired at its fair market value, so the VWRA position carries no latent gain into Canada and its cost base resets (CRA newcomers page, 2026-09-17); any T1244-deferred departure tax becomes payable or is unwound by election for property still held (CRA dispositions page). From the **second year** of residency, **T1135** is required if the total cost of specified foreign property (an Irish ETF is a share of a non-resident corporation) exceeds **C$100,000** at any time in the year (first-year exemption for individuals, s.233.7, secondary). In that month the owner decides, in `plan/changes.md` with the adviser: (a) keep VWRA in the taxable IBKR account (no annual income inclusion for a foreign accumulating fund, gains taxed on sale at the inclusion rate, no PFIC-style regime, MER 0.14% vs XEQT's 0.19%; a switch would itself be a disposition) and direct new registered-account contributions to XEQT or the XEF/XIC/XUU trio on a Canadian platform, which is the notes' default (UAE notes Q6); or (b) sell VWRA **before** arrival (tax-free anywhere, the step-up makes it pointless if return is imminent) and restart in registered accounts under the v4 plan. Whether a Canadian broker can hold LSE-listed UCITS funds in a registered account is unresearched (UAE notes Q6 gap); IBKR Canada can carry LSE lines in a non-registered account, so the IBKR account is kept open through the move. Re-entry also restarts TFSA, RRSP and FHSA room from that year, and the P2 question is re-answered: the owner is in Branch B from that month.

**Withholding-tax placement (Branch A).** There is no placement decision: the fund absorbs US withholding at 15% and non-US level-1 at treaty rates (about 22 bps/yr together, M4), Ireland withholds nothing from non-resident holders, and the UAE taxes nothing, so the whole position sits in the one IBKR account. Branch B keeps v4's placement rule (FHSA first if eligible, then TFSA or RRSP by the marginal-rate rule, then taxable; XIC to taxable first in the DIY case).

**T1135.** Not required while the owner is a non-resident (Branch A). Becomes required from the second year after re-entry if specified foreign property exceeds C$100,000 cost (step 7). In Branch B it is not required while all holdings are Canadian-listed and becomes required if US-listed holdings in non-registered accounts exceed C$100,000 cost. The monitor emits the reminder in the monthly review whenever `accounts.yaml` carries `canada_resident: true` and the taxable ledger cost of non-Canadian-listed tickers exceeds C$100,000 (P10).

**Egypt decision table (report "The Egypt tie"; egypt KQ1; unchanged from v4, with the Canadian column reading for either branch).**

**Adviser trigger.** Book a cross-border tax consultation (a) before any 12-month period in which days in Egypt could exceed 150 (a 33-day buffer under 183), (b) before acquiring, inheriting, or having continuous use of a dwelling in Egypt (availability, not ownership, is the treaty test), (c) before any 12-month period in which days in **Canada** could exceed 150 (the same buffer under the 183-day sojourner rule, Folio ¶1.32; new in v5), (d) the month a return to Canada is decided or residency is re-established (step 7), and (e) in Branch B, before any taxable-account balance exceeds C$100,000 or before the RRSP US-listed switch at C$250,000 (v4 triggers (c) and (d), Assumption 14). The monitor carries "days in Egypt this rolling year" and "days in Canada this rolling year" fields in `accounts.yaml` that the owner updates quarterly and the monthly review flags at 120 days (P10). ## P5 Risk guardrails

**Concentration.** The core is a single product, but the look-through is thousands of securities (VWRA: 3,821 stocks at 30 Sep 2026, UAE notes Q4; XEQT holdings CSV: 8,353 lines, verify log). The real concentration is structural: US 64.2% of ACWI, top-ten S&P 500 names 39% of that index (report Table B text). The guardrails are: no single ETF outside the P2 lists; satellites capped at **10%** of invested assets in total and 5% per position, and only after year 2 (report: practitioners cap at 5-20%, a 50% loss on a 5-10% satellite costs 2.5-5% of the whole); no single-stock positions; the monitor reports the top-10 look-through weight and the US weight monthly as context, never as a trigger (P10).

**Leverage ban.** No margin, no leveraged or inverse ETFs, no options, no crypto perpetuals (FINRA 09-31 on daily-reset leverage; TQQQ -81% in 2022; Alpha Arena losses, report R3 and R7 sections). Margin is disabled at the broker at Phase 1 milestone 1c (Branch A, P14) or v4's P4 step 6 (Branch B).

**Overlay-as-flag rule.** The 10-month SMA and drawdown flags, and the yield-curve, Sahm-rule and manually entered CAPE context lines, appear in reports with their own failure record printed beside them (12-month absolute momentum and a computed CAPE series were dropped in v2 for simplicity, audit gap 10; Table H's ABSMOM12 row stays as a base-rate footnote, and its XEQT-to-ZAG numbers are printed there because they are the best-looking variant in `overlay_results.csv` and cut against the flag-only verdict: 13.50% vs 14.25% CAGR, 0.75 pts/yr cost, 2 switches, -12.8% vs -17.6% drawdown, on a window one year shorter than buy-and-hold's; reviewer 3 concern 1) (SMA10 on XEQT: 7 switches, 2022 loss deepened to -15.4% with ZAG, April 2025 whipsaw; Table H). They never change a contribution, a weight or an account. If the owner ever wants to act on one, the AQR "sin a little" bound applies: at most a 10-20% change in equity weight (report Table H text), and only after the P12 validation passes and P15's change procedure is followed. Assumption: the owner will not act on them at all in the first 24 months.

**Behavioural guardrails.** (1) The 20% drawdown protocol in P3 Rule 4. (2) No discretionary selling in any account; the only sales are P3 Rule 3 band repairs (DIY only), P15 life events and the P15 DIY switch, each written up in `plan/changes.md` first. (3) A 48-hour cooling rule on any manual order that is not the recurring buy. (4) The monthly review opens with the Table B stress line ("the index behind this fund has lost 50-65% before and spent a decade flat") so the owner is reminded while calm, not during the event. (5) Portfolio changes are decided in writing in `plan/changes.md` with the research citation, at least one month before execution.

**What the monitor must never do.** It must never place, modify or cancel an order; never hold broker credentials, session cookies or 2FA secrets; never connect to a broker API or MCP server that can trade; never compute a "buy/sell" recommendation (its outputs are HOLD-COURSE, REVIEW and DEGRADED, borrowed from TradingAgents' `rating.py` REVIEW sentinel without the trade rating, report R13 section); never publish, share or sell reports or data derived from personal-use feeds (Yahoo, Alpha Vantage, Tiingo, TSX non-professional terms, report R15 section); never state a number the deterministic layer did not produce (grounding gate, P10); never write a final report file (its only output is the staging prose file; Python assembles every committed report after the grounding check, P8). The leverage and order bans are also written into CLAUDE.md (P8) so the model sees them in every run.

**Stability constraints on the plan itself.** No new ETF enters either portfolio unless it has 3+ years of live history, AUM above C$500M (above the report's US$100M survival floor by a wide margin, given 146 ETF closures in 2025) and a direct-holding or known-wrap structure disclosed on the issuer's fact sheet (for a UCITS line, add: Irish domicile and an accumulating share class, P2). **Residency-change trigger (new in v5).** A change in the owner's residency status (Canadian residency re-established, P4 step 7; a Branch B owner severing ties, P4 step 2; or Egyptian residence under P4 Scenario B) is a review event under P15, written up in `plan/changes.md` in the month it occurs with the cross-border adviser consulted first. It is the only event that can move the core between the Branch A and Branch B portfolios, and it is never acted on by a sale before the adviser has priced the departure tax or the arrival-date step-up.

```bash

# cron_monthly.sh differs only in the entry point, skill, budget, model and commit message (below).

DATE=$(date +%F)                       # GNU date; on macOS install coreutils (gdate) or compute in Python

[ "$(git symbolic-ref --short HEAD)" = "$(git remote show origin | sed -n 's/.*HEAD branch: //p')" ] \
  || { echo "not on default branch $DATE" >> logs/run.log; exit 0; }   # never commit reports onto a working branch
out=$(python run_daily.py --date today)            # fetch, compute, flags.csv, <date>.tables.md; prints DONE or CHANGED: yes|no
case "$out" in
  *DONE*) exit 0 ;;                                # assembled note exists: idempotency keys on the assembled file only
  *"CHANGED: no"*) python run_daily.py --assemble --stub ;;        # Python writes the stub; no model call at all
  *"CHANGED: yes"*)
    run() { claude -p "/daily-etf-watch" --bare --output-format json --max-budget-usd 0.30 \
              --permission-prompts none --strict-mcp-config --mcp-config '{"mcpServers":{}}' \
              --model sonnet > "logs/$DATE.json" || true; }
    ok()  { python -c 'import json,sys; d=json.load(open(sys.argv[1]));
                       sys.exit(0 if d.get("subtype")=="success" and not d.get("is_error") else 1)' "logs/$DATE.json"; }
    run; ok || { sleep 900; run; ok || echo "claude failed $DATE" >> logs/run.log; }   # one retry after 15 min (P8 retry policy)
    python run_daily.py --assemble ;;              # repeats the grounding check; tmp + rename of reports/daily/<date>.md,
                                                   # or a tables-only stub marked DEGRADED (grounding) if the check fails
                                                   # or the staging file is missing (model failed); the day still gets a note
esac
git add "reports/daily/$DATE.md" "reports/daily/$DATE.tables.md" reports/flags.csv

git push origin HEAD || echo "push failed $DATE" >> logs/push.log

Exponential backoff (2, 8, 32 s) on any vendor 429 or 5xx, then DEGRADED for numeric sources or "n/a (source failed)" for news.

**Licensing posture: tolerated, not licensed (reviewer 3 concern 4).** The keyless yfinance path is an unofficial API in a contractual grey zone: Yahoo's terms bar collection by automated means, and a daily scheduled run is exactly that, personal use or not. The plan still uses it as the baseline because, for a private, unshared, personal-use monitor, the exposure is operational (a block, a crumb change, a 429), not legal, and the plan treats a block as expected rather than exceptional. **EODHD is the licensed, compliant route**; its adapter exists from Phase 2b so a block is a config change (P13). Alpha Vantage, Tiingo and TSX data are likewise personal-use only; FRED requires attribution; BoC under its terms (R15 section, Table G). Reports stay in a private repository, are never shared, and carry a footer naming sources. If any report is ever to be shared, the data path must move to a licensed vendor first (P15).

**Paid upgrade path.** Trigger: two or more DEGRADED daily runs from Yahoo in a rolling 30 days, or a Yahoo endpoint change that `yfinance` has not patched within two weeks (an existing patch is applied first under the P13 out-of-cycle rule), or a yfinance 429 from the cloud fallback's shared egress if that route is ever used (audit gap 6, untested). Upgrade: EODHD Historian at US$19.99/month (30+ years EOD, explicit "TO" codes, 100k calls/day, Table G; its coverage of the LSE USD lines `VWRA.LSE`/`ACWD.LSE` is unverified and is checked on the sandbox, Assumption 26), tested once in Phase 2b on its 20-calls/day sandbox so the adapter exists before it is needed. Not planned: EODHD Fundamentals ($59.99), Massive ($199), fund-flow data (no free source exists, R10 section).

## P10 Signal and analysis methodology

Everything below is computed by `src/analyzers/` from the cache; Claude adds prose only. The split follows the report's verdicts (R11 section, Table H): nothing is a trigger; the SMA10 trend flag, the 20% drawdown flag and (DIY) drift are flags; 2s10s, Sahm and the manually entered CAPE are context. ABSMOM12 and a computed CAPE series were removed in v2 (audit gap 10) because they added a second trend rule and a manual data chore without changing any decision.

**Daily note computes.** Prices and 1-day/1-month/YTD returns in the base currency (`base_ccy`: USD in Branch A, CAD in Branch B) for the held tickers and the reference set (VT, VTI, VXUS, plus ACWD or the bond line in Branch A, ZAG in Branch B); current drawdown of each held fund from its all-time high (the **drawdown flag** turns on at -20%, the P3 Rule 4 threshold; account-value drawdown needs the ledger and is monthly only); in Branch B the USD/CAD level and 1-month change (currency is the unhedged shock absorber, Table B text), and in Branch A no FX line at all in the daily note (the core path is USD-based, P9); the **trend flag** (month-end close vs 10-month SMA, evaluated only on the last trading day of the month and reported as "on"/"off" with the standing footnote "7 switches since 2019, cost 2.1 pts/yr vs buy-and-hold on XEQT, exited after the trough in Apr 2025", Table H); the policy state; and `CHANGED: yes|no` against the previous run, which decides whether the note is a stub or a full note (P11).

**Monthly review computes.** Ledger reconciliation in two checks, because recurring buys fill intraday and a close-based tolerance would raise REVIEW on most volatile days (reviewer 2 concern 5): (i) internal consistency, `units x unit_price` within 0.1% of `amount` in the ledger's `ccy` (rounding), using the broker's confirmed fill price recorded in the ledger; (ii) plausibility, `unit_price` inside that date's cached `[Low, High]` and the date a trading day; either failing is REVIEW; XIRR and TWR since inception and trailing 12 months, computed by `src/backtest.py` (the Table C engine, which separates the two deliberately); **drift** per sleeve vs `portfolio.yaml` bands (DIY) or the look-through regional weights vs the issuer's published weights (all-in-one; the Vanguard UK or SSGA holdings file, or the index factsheet, in Branch A and the XEQT CSV in Branch B, P9; drift > 2 pts from the issuer file is REVIEW because it would indicate a cache or parsing error, not a market event); **concentration**: US weight, top-10 look-through weight (from the issuer holdings file or factsheet; benchmark 64.2% and 39% from the report), EM weight; **macro context**: 2s10s spread, Sahm indicator, 10-year yield, each printed with its false-alarm record (2022 inversion without recession, Sahm July 2024 "first false signal since 1970"); **CAPE context** from `config/context.yaml` (the owner's quarterly re-scan entry, P9, P15) printed as a 10-year expected-return band only, with its as-of date, "n/a (stale)" if older than 100 days, and Vanguard's finding that raw CAPE forecast worse than the historical mean (7.8% vs 6.2% error) printed beside it; the **account-value drawdown** from `ledger.csv` units x price (local only); in Branch B the contribution-room remaining per account, in Branch A the registered-account contribution block (any TFSA/RRSP/FHSA ledger row while `canada_resident: false` is REVIEW), the USD/CAD context line and the T1135 cost test once `canada_resident` turns true, and in both the days-in-Egypt and days-in-Canada counters (P4); and the 12-month **flag record**: count of flag changes, hit rate (did the fund fall a further 5% within 3 months of an "off" flag), whipsaw count (off-then-on within 2 months).

**Policy states.** HOLD-COURSE (default), REVIEW (ledger mismatch, drift outside band, contribution-room exhausted (Branch B), days-in-Egypt >= 120, days-in-Canada >= 120, a registered-account ledger row while `canada_resident: false`, taxable US-listed ticker present (Branch B) or non-Canadian-listed taxable cost above C$100,000 while `canada_resident: true`, issuer fee or index change found in news), DEGRADED (a numeric source failed: prices, FX or issuer CSV; or the grounding check failed at assembly, shown as `DEGRADED (grounding)`; or the flag-log hash chain broke, `DEGRADED (flag log)`; a news-feed failure, or a FRED failure until the Python route is verified in Phase 2a, prints "n/a (source failed)" and leaves the state unchanged). None of these states implies a trade; REVIEW means "the owner reads this section this month".

**News summarization with grounding rules.** Input is `news.json` (headline, source, URL, published time) from Google News RSS; no page bodies are fetched in scheduled runs, which removes most prompt-injection surface (R15 section, OWASP LLM01). The model writes at most 120 words, cites only items in the file, and may not state numbers absent from the tables. **Grounding check, whitelist form (reviewer 2 concern 4):** `run_daily.py --check` (run by the skill) and `--assemble` (run by the wrapper) tokenise every number in the staged prose and accept it only if it appears in the whitelist: every number in `<date>.tables.md`, every number in the template text and the stress line (`templates/daily.md`: "50.8%", "2007-09", "S&P 500", "10-month SMA", "T1135", "12 months"), the headline strings in `news.json` (so a figure quoted from a cited headline passes), and the run's own date. A digit-grep against the tables alone would fail on the stress line and the template every day and the owner would disable it within a month. **Enforcement point:** Python, not the model, writes the final file; `--assemble` repeats the check and, on failure or on a missing staging file, writes a tables-only stub marked `DEGRADED (grounding)` with the rejected tokens in `logs/` (Vibe-Trading's grounding gate, R13 section). Sentiment is never scored: the report found 1-2 day horizons, memorization contamination of ~37% and effects "far below cost" (R11 section).

## P12 Validation

**Flag log and yearly scoring.** Every flag change is appended to `reports/flags.csv` with price and date. **Integrity controls (reviewer 3 concern 7):** the file is append-only with a per-row hash chain (`prev_hash`, `row_hash = sha256(prev_hash + the row's fields)`); `run_daily.py --check` and `--assemble` recompute the chain from the first row and mark the run `DEGRADED (flag log)` if any row was edited, reordered or removed; the wrapper commits the file every run, so git history is a second, independent record of every change. The 12-month hit rate is printed with its sample size and the label "n = <changes>: not a statistic" until the cumulative count reaches 30, since after a year the log will hold two to seven changes. Each January the review computes, per flag, over the trailing 12 and cumulative months: number of changes, hit rate (an "off" followed by a further -5% within 3 months), whipsaw count (off-then-on within 2 months), and the hypothetical CAGR cost of following it versus buy-and-hold with contributions. Base rates to beat come from Table H (XEQT SMA10: 7 switches, 12.14% vs 14.25%). A flag that whipsaws more than twice a year for two years is demoted from "flag" to "context".

**Baseline backtest.** `src/backtest.py` is the verified pandas engine behind Table C (`data/verify/t2_contribution_backtest.py`): C$1,000 on the first trading day of each month, dividends reinvested, FX at FRED DEXCAUS, XIRR and TWR separate. Phase 2b extends it with 0.05% per side (QuantDinger and trend-bot convention), the fund-level withholding drag (about 22 bps inside the Irish fund in Branch A, UAE notes Q4; 15 bps registered in Branch B) and the broker commission (IBKR Tiered USD 1.70 per buy on US$2,500 in Branch A; Wealthsimple $0 in Branch B), with the Branch A run contributing US$2,500 on the first trading day of each month in USD, then cross-checks against `bt.algos.CapitalFlow` (bt 1.4.1, source inspected but not executed, audit gap 8) and one Portfolio Visualizer free-tier run (15-asset cap). The three must agree on ending value within 1%; otherwise the engine is REVIEW until fixed. The same run derives the worst rolling 10-year and 20-year returns for VT, EAFE, EM and XIC from `gapfill2_data/monthly_adj_close_yfinance_2026-11-02.csv` (audit gap 3) and prints them in the January review.

**New-rule gate.** No new rule becomes a flag, and no flag becomes anything more, unless all of the following pass, implemented with `skfolio 1.12.5` (Table I), which is installed and pinned only at that point (audit gap 10): a frozen publication-date split (Faber 2007 for SMA10, Antonacci 2014 for momentum) with no tuning after it; `WalkForward` and `CombinatorialPurgedCV` with at least one rebalance period of embargo; `deflated_sharpe_ratio` fed the number of variants tried as **logged automatically** by the harness (`src/backtest.py` appends every parameter set it evaluates to the committed `experiments/variants.jsonl`; the DSR call reads the count from that file and there is no argument for the owner to type), above 0.95; Harvey-Liu-Zhu's t > 3 hurdle; stress runs with a one-month execution delay and with both cash and bonds as the defensive asset (a two-month delay cut the SMA rule from 8.6% to 7.0% CAGR in the report's cited replication); and live post-launch evidence only, discounting anything mostly backtested (Research Affiliates: 2.8%/yr backtested vs 0.5% live). The anti-lookahead test `tests/test_lookahead.py` mutates every price after the as-of date and asserts identical analyzer output, with one planted leak that must fail; CI runs it on every commit.

**Monitor accuracy.** Each monthly review samples three numbers from the daily notes and recomputes them by hand from the issuer fact sheet or cache; any mismatch is REVIEW. The grounding check (P10) is logged at assembly; M9 requires zero committed reports carrying an ungrounded number over the year (a `DEGRADED (grounding)` stub is a caught failure, not a breach, but two in a quarter without a fix is a P15 kill trigger).

## P15 Review, kill and update criteria

**Portfolio changes.** The portfolio changes only on life events, never on signals, news, forecasts or the monitor's flags: a planned withdrawal within 5 years (then shift new contributions to XGRO/VGRO or a bond sleeve for that portion), a first-home purchase (FHSA withdrawal), a **change of residency status** (the month Canadian residency is re-established, P4 step 7; a Branch B owner severing ties, P4 step 2; or Egyptian residence under P4 Scenario B), adviser first, which is the only event that moves the core between the Branch A and Branch B portfolios (P5 residency-change trigger), a marriage or dependant, or an income change that moves the contribution amount. Each change is written in `plan/changes.md` with the research citation one month before execution (P5). A fee or index change by the issuer is a REVIEW item, not a change, unless the MER rises above 0.30% or the fund announces termination.

**All-in-one vs DIY switch (Branch B).** In Branch A this clause is dormant: the core already is the index at 0.14% and the only unbundled alternative (IWDA plus EIMI) costs more, P2. In Branch B, move from XEQT to the DIY alternative only when total invested assets exceed **C$300,000** (**Assumption:** the owner's reading of the report's "few hundred thousand dollars" threshold; at that balance the 6-7 bps saving is about C$180-210/yr) and the owner has met M6 for twelve months, since the switch adds about ten minutes a month and the risk of a mistimed manual rebalance. **ACB and capital gains.** In registered accounts (TFSA, FHSA, RRSP) a sale of XEQT has no tax consequence, so the switch there may sell and rebuy. In a taxable account a sale realises a capital gain against the ledger's adjusted cost base (P3 Rule 2 step 3), so the switch is made by **redirecting new contributions** to the DIY sleeves and leaving the existing XEQT units in place until the adviser consulted under P4 trigger (e) confirms the tax cost; this means a taxable account may hold XEQT and DIY sleeves together during the transition, which is the one exception to the next sentence. Move back to all-in-one if a drift band is missed twice in a year. Otherwise never hold both in the same account.

**Broker-change trigger (audit gap 5).** Branch A: if IBKR withdraws the LSE USD lines, Tiered pricing or UAE-resident accounts, or migrates the account to an entity that blocks the chosen line, (1) do not move existing holdings; (2) at the next quarterly re-scan confirm a replacement broker's pricing and UCITS access from a primary page and redirect **new contributions only**; (3) record the move in `plan/changes.md` with the P5 one-month notice. Branch B: if Wealthsimple ends $0 commissions on Canadian ETFs, fractional shares or recurring buys (the three Table D features the monthly procedure relies on), or raises a fixed fee on a CAD-only Core account: (1) do not move existing holdings; (2) at the next quarterly re-scan confirm Questrade's pricing from a primary page (its $0 since Feb 2025 is secondary-sourced, Table D, audit gap 4) and, if confirmed, open the matching account type there and redirect **new contributions only**, rounding to whole units because Questrade's fractional shares are US-listed only (Table D); (3) if Questrade cannot be confirmed either, IBKR Canada is the third option at CAD 0.01/share with a CAD 1.00 minimum and no account minimum or inactivity fee (Table D, Canonical facts corrections): a C$1,000 buy of fewer than 100 units costs the C$1.00 minimum, about 10 bps per buy, or about C$4/month for four DIY sleeves (Assumption: unit prices above C$10, so the minimum binds). A transfer-out fee at the old broker (Questrade's is C$150, secondary, Table D) is accepted only after the new broker is confirmed, and the move is recorded in `plan/changes.md` with the P5 one-month notice.

**Kill the monitor** if any of: cost exceeds $50/month for two consecutive months (a move to Max is above this line and is therefore an owner decision recorded in `plan/changes.md`, never an escalation, P13); the owner stops reading the monthly review for three months; the grounding check fails twice in a quarter without a fix; the daily note's uptime falls below 70% of scheduled trading-day runs for a quarter (fewer than about 15 of 21 notes a month, stub or full, on the same denominator as M7) after the cloud and local routes have both been tried; or the owner notices the flags influencing a contribution decision (the monitor exists to inform, and the moment it tempts timing it has negative value). Killing the monitor changes nothing in the portfolio: the recurring buy continues.

**Quarterly re-scan procedure (about 1.5 hours, months 3, 6, 9, 12).** (1) Re-read the issuer fact sheets for the held tickers (TER or MER, AUM, structure, index, share-class currency and accumulating status, distribution frequency in Branch B) and update `portfolio.yaml`; in Branch A, patch the UAE notes' drag figures (M4) into the report's canonical table or restate them; look up the current Shiller CAPE on the Shiller data page and type it with its as-of date into `config/context.yaml` (P9; this is the only manual data input in the toolkit and it is stale after 100 days, which the next re-scan refreshes). (2) `gh api repos/{owner}/{repo}` for the used libraries and MCP servers: last push, latest release, open issues, licence; drop any dependency with no push in 12 months or a licence change. (3) Scan GitHub trending and the report's Table E/F set for new ETF-relevant tools; a candidate is adopted only if it is read-only, Claude-native or plain Python, maintained within 90 days, and replaces something rather than adding a layer. (4) Re-run the P12 flag scoring to date. (5) Record the scan in `plan/rescan_YYYY-QN.md`; bump pins (P13). New research on allocation is treated like a new rule: it must pass the P12 gate and the P5 overlay rule before it changes anything.

**No-advice posture.** Every report carries "personal research for the owner's own accounts, not advice". The owner is not advising for a business purpose under 31-103CP s.1.3 (no clients, compensation, solicitation, holding-out or intermediation; 2014 consolidation quoted, verify against the 2026 CP during the first re-scan, audit gap 7). Publishing, sharing or monetising the reports crosses that line and the data licences at once and is prohibited by CLAUDE.md.
