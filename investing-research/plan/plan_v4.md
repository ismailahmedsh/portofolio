## P2 Portfolio construction

**Why not trading bots (two sentences, evidence pointer).** The large administrative datasets give the base rate for active trading: 93% of ten million Indian derivatives (F&O) traders lost money over FY22-24 (SEBI), 1.1% of persistent Brazilian day traders earned more than minimum wage, and fewer than 1% of Taiwanese day traders earned reliably positive abnormal returns (report "Three independent evidence streams"; these are derivatives and day-trader populations, and no source supports a broader "85-93% of all active retail traders" claim, reviewer 3 concern 6); published strategies decay 58% after publication, and the 2025-2026 LLM-agent record is at best +1.5 pts over buy-and-hold in StockBench with four of six models losing real money in Alpha Arena (report "Three independent evidence streams", Table E). The maintainers themselves agree ("it will always be a gamble", freqtrade FAQ), eight of fourteen frameworks cannot even hold a TSX ETF, and no maintainer claims profitability, so the bots contribute scaffolding to the monitor (P7) and nothing to the portfolio.

## P3 Contribution and rebalancing rules

**Rule 1, invest on receipt.** Each monthly deposit (Assumption: C$500-2,000, the band Table D is built for) is invested on the first trading day on or after it lands, never held for a dip. Evidence: lump sum beat a 3-month drip 68% of the time and cash 70% of the time, 1976-2022 (report R4 section). The only exception is the deposit landing on a non-trading day.

**Rule 2, the monthly procedure (primary portfolio, about 10 minutes).**
1. On payday, transfer the fixed amount to the broker account that is next in the P4 order (FHSA first if eligible, until its $8,000 annual room is used; then TFSA or RRSP by the P4 step-3 marginal-rate rule until that year's room is used; then taxable).
2. At Wealthsimple, keep a recurring buy of XEQT set to the deposit amount, fractional shares on, DRIP on (Table D: fractional from $1, recurring weekly/bi-weekly/monthly, DRIP available). The recurring order is the "no-decision" mechanism; the owner's only task is confirming the deposit cleared.
3. Record the trade (date, account, ticker, `kind=buy`, CAD amount, units, the broker's confirmed fill price) in `etf-monitor/data/ledger.csv`, one row per buy, one `kind=drip` row per DRIP reinvestment and, for a taxable account, one `kind=phantom` row per holding per year for the reinvested (non-cash) capital-gains distribution from the T3 slip or the issuer's tax breakdown (schema in P6, Rule 5). This ledger is what the monthly review reads locally; it is also the T1135 and ACB record for a taxable account, and an ACB that omits the phantom rows understates cost and overstates the gain on any later sale (reviewer 1 concern 2). It is gitignored and never leaves the owner's machine (P8, P13); a copy lives in the owner's normal backup, not in the repository.
4. Do nothing else. The all-in-one fund rebalances internally.

**Rule 2b, the monthly procedure (DIY alternative, about 20 minutes).** Steps 1 and 3 as above. For step 2, direct the whole deposit to the most underweight sleeve(s) in order of absolute underweight until each is back inside its band; this "contribution-only" rule kept the DIY mix within 0.5 pts of target over 141 months with zero sales (report R4 section, Table C). Questrade is the alternative broker but fractional shares there are US-listed only (Table D), so at Questrade round each sleeve to whole units and carry the remainder as cash into next month.

**Rule 3, drift bands (DIY only).** Vanguard's 200 bps threshold with a 175 bps destination and Daryanani's 20% relative band both appear in the report; for four sleeves the 20% relative band is: XUU 36-54%, XEF 24-36%, XEC 8-12%, XIC 12-18%. Check bands at the monthly buy (the monitor computes them, P10); rebalance by selling only if a sleeve is outside its band and two consecutive months of contributions cannot bring it back; in practice, with C$500-2,000/month, this is expected to happen only after a very large relative move (report: zero sales in 141 months). Annual calendar check each January regardless. A band repair is one of the three permitted sale types under P5 guardrail (2); in a taxable account it realises a capital gain at the ledger ACB, so repair registered accounts first and let the taxable account drift until a contribution can fix it.

**Rule 4, drawdown protocol.** A drawdown changes nothing in rules 1-3. When the core is 20% or more below its high-water mark (the monitor reports this, P10), the owner re-reads the P5 behavioural clause and the Table B stress numbers and confirms the next deposit. There is no other lever: v3's "bring forward up to 3 months of deposits" was a timing rule in a plan that forbids timing, and after a -20% leg a further -30% is the historical pattern (reviewers 1 and 3), so it is struck; the only contribution rule is Rule 1, invest on receipt, and the emergency fund (3-6 months of expenses in a HISA, confirmed at the Phase 1 gate) is never drawn into the portfolio. No selling, no switching to a bond fund, no "waiting for the bottom": fewer than 0.5% of Vanguard's self-directed investors went to cash in Feb-May 2020 and more than 80% of those who did were worse off by the end of May (report R4 section), and an SMA exit on XEQT in April 2025 came after the trough and cost 6.5 pts (Table H text).

**Rule 5, DRIP and distributions.** DRIP on in every account. XEQT distributions are quarterly (**Assumption:** the frequency has no report source and is confirmed from the issuer fact sheet at the first re-scan; nothing depends on it) and small (the verify log's `Ticker.info` printed a yield of about 1.58%, which is log output rather than a report figure or a script result, so it is note-level and an Assumption until read from the issuer fact sheet at the first re-scan; nothing in the plan depends on its exact value); in the taxable account the DRIP purchases are logged to `ledger.csv` as `kind=drip` rows, and each year, when the T3 slip or the issuer's annual tax-breakdown page is published, the owner adds one `kind=phantom` row per taxable holding for the reinvested capital-gains distribution (non-cash, no units issued after the consolidation, ACB raised by the amount; the row a buys-only ledger misses, reviewer 1 concern 2). XEQT and each sleeve ETF pay such distributions in most years.

**Rule 6, cash.** No cash sleeve inside the portfolio. Cash earmarked for the portfolio never sits more than one month uninvested.

**Rule 7, contribution changes.** Raise the monthly amount with income; never lower it in response to a market move. Any one-off lump sum (bonus, gift) is invested in full on receipt (Rule 1), split across accounts in the P4 order.

**Ordered setup steps.**
1. Confirm 2026 contribution room on CRA My Account: TFSA $7,000 for 2026 (cumulative $109,000 if eligible since 2009), RRSP $33,810 ceiling (18% of 2025 earned income less pension adjustments), FHSA $8,000/yr to $40,000 lifetime (report "Account placement"). Room ($8,000/yr, $40,000 lifetime, at most $8,000 carried forward) accrues only from the year the account is opened, so delay is lost room. If not eligible, skip it; record `fhsa_eligible` in `accounts.yaml`.
3. **TFSA or RRSP by marginal rate (the rule).** Open a Wealthsimple **TFSA** and **RRSP**, same fund. Deposits beyond FHSA room go **RRSP first when the owner's current marginal tax rate is higher than the marginal rate expected on withdrawal in retirement** (rule of thumb: taxable income in or above the second federal bracket; the owner reads the current year's bracket from the CRA table at setup, Assumption 4), and **TFSA first when the current rate is at or below the expected retirement rate, or when in doubt**. Record the two rates and the choice in `plan/changes.md`; revisit at an income change (P15). The TFSA carries a 1%/month penalty on over-contributions and the same 1%/month on contributions made while a non-resident of Canada (Egypt table below), so the monitor tracks room (P10).
4. The VTI+VXUS switch inside the RRSP (saving roughly 20 bps/yr: 8-9 bps withholding plus 10-12 bps MER) is deferred until the RRSP balance exceeds **C$250,000** and is done **only at IBKR Canada** (**Assumption 14:** raised from v3's C$100,000 on reviewer 1 concern 4: at C$100,000 the saving is about C$200/yr against a USD account, FX conversions, a second ledger and a US-situs estate-tax exposure on US-listed holdings that the research never priced; Wealthsimple Core cannot do Norbert's Gambit cleanly, with no free USD account and journaling by request, so the switch is not attempted there). At a 1.5% FX fee it takes about seven years per converted dollar to pay back (report "Account placement"); at IBKR the FX cost is 0.20 bp (Table D). Until then every RRSP holds XEQT.
5. Open a **taxable** account only when all registered room is full. Hold XEQT (or XIC first in the DIY case); keep the ledger for adjusted cost base, including the yearly `phantom` rows (P3 Rule 5); Canadian-listed holdings keep the account outside T1135 scope, which otherwise applies when the cost of specified foreign property exceeds C$100,000 (report "Account placement"). Rule: never hold US-listed ETFs in the taxable account.
6. Enable DRIP and fractional buys in every account; turn off margin; do not link any broker credential to any software (P5).

**Withholding-tax placement.** XEQT/VEQT or the Canadian-listed DIY: ~15 bps/yr drag in TFSA/RRSP/FHSA (range 15-22 with the CPM calculator), ~0-2 bps taxable (egypt KQ2 table, report "Account placement"). | Question | Scenario A (base case): factual Canadian resident, Egyptian family ties, no dwelling in Egypt available for continuous use, under 183 days/yr there | Scenario B: 183+ days in Egypt in any 12 months, or a dwelling in Egypt available for the owner's continuous use (owned, rented, or a family apartment the owner can use at will: the treaty "permanent home" test turns on availability, not ownership, reviewer 3 concern 2) |

Audit gap 5 remains open: Scenario B foreign-dividend treatment, the IBKR entity for Egyptian residents and Canadian departure tax are not answered by the research, which is why the trigger is conservative.

## P5 Risk guardrails

**Concentration.** The core is a single product, but the look-through is 8,000+ securities (XEQT holdings CSV, 8,353 lines, verify log). The real concentration is structural: US 64.2% of ACWI, top-ten S&P 500 names 39% of that index (report Table B text). The guardrails are: no single ETF outside the P2 lists; satellites capped at **10%** of invested assets in total and 5% per position, and only after year 2 (report: practitioners cap at 5-20%, a 50% loss on a 5-10% satellite costs 2.5-5% of the whole); no single-stock positions; the monitor reports the top-10 look-through weight and the US weight monthly as context, never as a trigger (P10).

**Leverage ban.** No margin, no leveraged or inverse ETFs, no options, no crypto perpetuals (FINRA 09-31 on daily-reset leverage; TQQQ -81% in 2022; Alpha Arena losses, report R3 and R7 sections). Margin is disabled at the broker in P4 step 6.

**Overlay-as-flag rule.** The 10-month SMA and drawdown flags, and the yield-curve, Sahm-rule and manually entered CAPE context lines, appear in reports with their own failure record printed beside them (12-month absolute momentum and a computed CAPE series were dropped in v2 for simplicity, audit gap 10; Table H's ABSMOM12 row stays as a base-rate footnote, and its XEQT-to-ZAG numbers are printed there because they are the best-looking variant in `overlay_results.csv` and cut against the flag-only verdict: 13.50% vs 14.25% CAGR, 0.75 pts/yr cost, 2 switches, -12.8% vs -17.6% drawdown, on a window one year shorter than buy-and-hold's; reviewer 3 concern 1) (SMA10 on XEQT: 7 switches, 2022 loss deepened to -15.4% with ZAG, April 2025 whipsaw; Table H). They never change a contribution, a weight or an account. If the owner ever wants to act on one, the AQR "sin a little" bound applies: at most a 10-20% change in equity weight (report Table H text), and only after the P12 validation passes and P15's change procedure is followed. Assumption: the owner will not act on them at all in the first 24 months.

**Behavioural guardrails.** (1) The 20% drawdown protocol in P3 Rule 4. (2) No discretionary selling in any account; the only sales are P3 Rule 3 band repairs (DIY only), P15 life events and the P15 DIY switch, each written up in `plan/changes.md` first. (3) A 48-hour cooling rule on any manual order that is not the recurring buy. (4) The monthly review opens with the Table B stress line ("the index behind this fund has lost 50-65% before and spent a decade flat") so the owner is reminded while calm, not during the event. (5) Portfolio changes are decided in writing in `plan/changes.md` with the research citation, at least one month before execution.

**What the monitor must never do.** It must never place, modify or cancel an order; never hold broker credentials, session cookies or 2FA secrets; never connect to a broker API or MCP server that can trade; never compute a "buy/sell" recommendation (its outputs are HOLD-COURSE, REVIEW and DEGRADED, borrowed from TradingAgents' `rating.py` REVIEW sentinel without the trade rating, report R13 section); never publish, share or sell reports or data derived from personal-use feeds (Yahoo, Alpha Vantage, Tiingo, TSX non-professional terms, report R15 section); never state a number the deterministic layer did not produce (grounding gate, P10); never write a final report file (its only output is the staging prose file; Python assembles every committed report after the grounding check, P8). The leverage and order bans are also written into CLAUDE.md (P8) so the model sees them in every run.

**Stability constraints on the plan itself.** No new ETF enters either portfolio unless it has 3+ years of live history, AUM above C$500M (above the report's US$100M survival floor by a wide margin, given 146 ETF closures in 2025) and a direct-holding or known-wrap structure disclosed on the issuer's fact sheet.

**Wrappers (`scripts/cron_daily.sh` and `scripts/cron_monthly.sh`).** Both follow the same pattern (reviewer 2 simplification 1 and Blocking 1-3): Python runs first and writes the tables; the model is invoked only when Python printed `CHANGED: yes`; the wrapper decides success from `is_error` and `subtype` in the JSON, never from the exit code (reviewer 2 concern 2: `claude -p` exits 0 with `is_error: true`); Python assembles the final file after the model exits; the wrapper commits only the assembled files, on the default branch, and pushes.
```bash
#!/usr/bin/env bash
# cron_daily.sh: Python first; Claude only on change days; Python assembles; one writer, default branch.
# cron_monthly.sh differs only in the entry point, skill, budget, model and commit message (below).
set -euo pipefail
REPO=/path/to/portofolio/etf-monitor
DATE=$(date +%F)                       # GNU date; on macOS install coreutils (gdate) or compute in Python
cd "$REPO"
[ "$(git symbolic-ref --short HEAD)" = "$(git remote show origin | sed -n 's/.*HEAD branch: //p')" ] \
  || { echo "not on default branch $DATE" >> logs/run.log; exit 0; }   # never commit reports onto a working branch
out=$(python run_daily.py --date today)            # fetch, compute, flags.csv, <date>.tables.md; prints DONE or CHANGED: yes|no
case "$out" in
  *DONE*) exit 0 ;;                                # assembled note exists: idempotency keys on the assembled file only
  *"CHANGED: no"*) python run_daily.py --assemble --stub ;;        # Python writes the stub; no model call at all
  *"CHANGED: yes"*)
    run() { claude -p "/daily-etf-watch" --bare --output-format json --max-budget-usd 0.30 \
              --permission-prompts none --strict-mcp-config --mcp-config '{"mcpServers":{}}' \
              --model sonnet > "logs/$DATE.json" || true; }
    ok()  { python -c 'import json,sys; d=json.load(open(sys.argv[1]));
                       sys.exit(0 if d.get("subtype")=="success" and not d.get("is_error") else 1)' "logs/$DATE.json"; }
    run; ok || { sleep 900; run; ok || echo "claude failed $DATE" >> logs/run.log; }   # one retry after 15 min (P8 retry policy)
    python run_daily.py --assemble ;;              # repeats the grounding check; tmp + rename of reports/daily/<date>.md,
                                                   # or a tables-only stub marked DEGRADED (grounding) if the check fails
                                                   # or the staging file is missing (model failed); the day still gets a note
esac
git add "reports/daily/$DATE.md" "reports/daily/$DATE.tables.md" reports/flags.csv
git commit -m "daily $DATE" || true
git push origin HEAD || echo "push failed $DATE" >> logs/push.log
```

## P10 Signal and analysis methodology

Everything below is computed by `src/analyzers/` from the cache; Claude adds prose only. The split follows the report's verdicts (R11 section, Table H): nothing is a trigger; the SMA10 trend flag, the 20% drawdown flag and (DIY) drift are flags; 2s10s, Sahm and the manually entered CAPE are context. ABSMOM12 and a computed CAPE series were removed in v2 (audit gap 10) because they added a second trend rule and a manual data chore without changing any decision.

**Daily note computes.** Prices and 1-day/1-month/YTD returns in CAD for the held tickers and the reference set (VT, VTI, VXUS, ZAG); current drawdown of each held fund from its all-time high (the **drawdown flag** turns on at -20%, the P3 Rule 4 threshold; account-value drawdown needs the ledger and is monthly only); USD/CAD level and 1-month change (currency is the unhedged shock absorber, Table B text); the **trend flag** (month-end close vs 10-month SMA, evaluated only on the last trading day of the month and reported as "on"/"off" with the standing footnote "7 switches since 2019, cost 2.1 pts/yr vs buy-and-hold on XEQT, exited after the trough in Apr 2025", Table H); the policy state; and `CHANGED: yes|no` against the previous run, which decides whether the note is a stub or a full note (P11).

**Monthly review computes.** Ledger reconciliation in two checks, because recurring buys fill intraday and a close-based tolerance would raise REVIEW on most volatile days (reviewer 2 concern 5): (i) internal consistency, `units x unit_price` within 0.1% of `cad_amount` (rounding), using the broker's confirmed fill price recorded in the ledger; (ii) plausibility, `unit_price` inside that date's cached `[Low, High]` and the date a trading day; either failing is REVIEW; XIRR and TWR since inception and trailing 12 months, computed by `src/backtest.py` (the Table C engine, which separates the two deliberately); **drift** per sleeve vs `portfolio.yaml` bands (DIY) or the look-through sleeve weights vs XEQT's published weights (all-in-one; drift > 2 pts from the issuer file is REVIEW because it would indicate a cache or parsing error, not a market event); **concentration**: US weight, top-10 look-through weight (from the iShares CSVs; benchmark 64.2% and 39% from the report), EM weight; **macro context**: 2s10s spread, Sahm indicator, 10-year yield, each printed with its false-alarm record (2022 inversion without recession, Sahm July 2024 "first false signal since 1970"); **CAPE context** from `config/context.yaml` (the owner's quarterly re-scan entry, P9, P15) printed as a 10-year expected-return band only, with its as-of date, "n/a (stale)" if older than 100 days, and Vanguard's finding that raw CAPE forecast worse than the historical mean (7.8% vs 6.2% error) printed beside it; the **account-value drawdown** from `ledger.csv` units x price (local only); contribution-room remaining per account and the days-in-Egypt counter (P4); and the 12-month **flag record**: count of flag changes, hit rate (did the fund fall a further 5% within 3 months of an "off" flag), whipsaw count (off-then-on within 2 months).

**Policy states.** HOLD-COURSE (default), REVIEW (ledger mismatch, drift outside band, contribution-room exhausted, days-in-Egypt >= 120, taxable US-listed ticker present, issuer fee or index change found in news), DEGRADED (a numeric source failed: prices, FX or issuer CSV; or the grounding check failed at assembly, shown as `DEGRADED (grounding)`; or the flag-log hash chain broke, `DEGRADED (flag log)`; a news-feed failure, or a FRED failure until the Python route is verified in Phase 2a, prints "n/a (source failed)" and leaves the state unchanged). None of these states implies a trade; REVIEW means "the owner reads this section this month".

**News summarization with grounding rules.** Input is `news.json` (headline, source, URL, published time) from Google News RSS; no page bodies are fetched in scheduled runs, which removes most prompt-injection surface (R15 section, OWASP LLM01). The model writes at most 120 words, cites only items in the file, and may not state numbers absent from the tables. **Grounding check, whitelist form (reviewer 2 concern 4):** `run_daily.py --check` (run by the skill) and `--assemble` (run by the wrapper) tokenise every number in the staged prose and accept it only if it appears in the whitelist: every number in `<date>.tables.md`, every number in the template text and the stress line (`templates/daily.md`: "50.8%", "2007-09", "S&P 500", "10-month SMA", "T1135", "12 months"), the headline strings in `news.json` (so a figure quoted from a cited headline passes), and the run's own date. A digit-grep against the tables alone would fail on the stress line and the template every day and the owner would disable it within a month. **Enforcement point:** Python, not the model, writes the final file; `--assemble` repeats the check and, on failure or on a missing staging file, writes a tables-only stub marked `DEGRADED (grounding)` with the rejected tokens in `logs/` (Vibe-Trading's grounding gate, R13 section). Sentiment is never scored: the report found 1-2 day horizons, memorization contamination of ~37% and effects "far below cost" (R11 section).

**Change-only rule, for the note and the alert.** The daily note is a one-line stub plus tables on days when the policy state and every flag are unchanged (`CHANGED: no`), and a full note with prose and news only on change days; on a `CHANGED: no` day Python writes the stub itself and the model is not invoked at all, so most trading days cost no model turn (reviewer 2 simplification 1), which protects Pro's 5-hour and weekly windows (Canonical fact 8). The notifier sends a message only when the policy state or any flag changed since the previous run, or on the monthly review; otherwise nothing is sent. Expected volume: a few messages a month. The owner can set `daily_full_note: true` in `portfolio.yaml` to get the full note every day (Assumption 9). Channel: Telegram bot (about one message per second per chat, 50 MB uploads, R14 section) or Gmail (`messages.send` at 100 quota units); Assumption: Telegram, chosen in Phase 3.

## P12 Validation

**Flag log and yearly scoring.** Every flag change is appended to `reports/flags.csv` with price and date. **Integrity controls (reviewer 3 concern 7):** the file is append-only with a per-row hash chain (`prev_hash`, `row_hash = sha256(prev_hash + the row's fields)`); `run_daily.py --check` and `--assemble` recompute the chain from the first row and mark the run `DEGRADED (flag log)` if any row was edited, reordered or removed; the wrapper commits the file every run, so git history is a second, independent record of every change. The 12-month hit rate is printed with its sample size and the label "n = <changes>: not a statistic" until the cumulative count reaches 30, since after a year the log will hold two to seven changes. Each January the review computes, per flag, over the trailing 12 and cumulative months: number of changes, hit rate (an "off" followed by a further -5% within 3 months), whipsaw count (off-then-on within 2 months), and the hypothetical CAGR cost of following it versus buy-and-hold with contributions. Base rates to beat come from Table H (XEQT SMA10: 7 switches, 12.14% vs 14.25%). A flag that whipsaws more than twice a year for two years is demoted from "flag" to "context".

**Baseline backtest.** `src/backtest.py` is the verified pandas engine behind Table C (`data/verify/t2_contribution_backtest.py`): C$1,000 on the first trading day of each month, dividends reinvested, FX at FRED DEXCAUS, XIRR and TWR separate. Phase 2b extends it with 0.05% per side (QuantDinger and trend-bot convention), the 15 bps registered-account FWT drag and Wealthsimple's $0 commission, then cross-checks against `bt.algos.CapitalFlow` (bt 1.4.1, source inspected but not executed, audit gap 8) and one Portfolio Visualizer free-tier run (15-asset cap). The three must agree on ending value within 1%; otherwise the engine is REVIEW until fixed. The same run derives the worst rolling 10-year and 20-year returns for VT, EAFE, EM and XIC from `gapfill2_data/monthly_adj_close_yfinance_2026-11-02.csv` (audit gap 3) and prints them in the January review.

**New-rule gate.** No new rule becomes a flag, and no flag becomes anything more, unless all of the following pass, implemented with `skfolio 1.12.5` (Table I), which is installed and pinned only at that point (audit gap 10): a frozen publication-date split (Faber 2007 for SMA10, Antonacci 2014 for momentum) with no tuning after it; `WalkForward` and `CombinatorialPurgedCV` with at least one rebalance period of embargo; `deflated_sharpe_ratio` fed the number of variants tried as **logged automatically** by the harness (`src/backtest.py` appends every parameter set it evaluates to the committed `experiments/variants.jsonl`; the DSR call reads the count from that file and there is no argument for the owner to type), above 0.95; Harvey-Liu-Zhu's t > 3 hurdle; stress runs with a one-month execution delay and with both cash and bonds as the defensive asset (a two-month delay cut the SMA rule from 8.6% to 7.0% CAGR in the report's cited replication); and live post-launch evidence only, discounting anything mostly backtested (Research Affiliates: 2.8%/yr backtested vs 0.5% live). The anti-lookahead test `tests/test_lookahead.py` mutates every price after the as-of date and asserts identical analyzer output, with one planted leak that must fail; CI runs it on every commit.

**Monitor accuracy.** Each monthly review samples three numbers from the daily notes and recomputes them by hand from the issuer fact sheet or cache; any mismatch is REVIEW. The grounding check (P10) is logged at assembly; M9 requires zero committed reports carrying an ungrounded number over the year (a `DEGRADED (grounding)` stub is a caught failure, not a breach, but two in a quarter without a fix is a P15 kill trigger).

## P13 Operations

**Security.** No broker access of any kind (P5). Data keys (FRED API key optional; EODHD only if the paid path triggers) live in a 0600 `.env` locally or as cloud network secrets, scoped read-only, never in environment variables of a shared cloud environment and never committed (R15 section, tech Q5). Commits and pushes are made by the local cron wrapper on the default branch, as the shell user, after Python has assembled the report; the model never holds a git tool and never writes a final report file. Past reports under `reports/` show fund prices, flags and percentages, not balances. The repository stays private because the data licences are personal-use (R15 section), and nothing derived from a personal-use feed is redistributed by committing it, since the cache is never committed.

Stooq is not a fallback (unreachable in verification, TSX coverage unknown).

## P15 Review, kill and update criteria

**Portfolio changes.** The portfolio changes only on life events, never on signals, news, forecasts or the monitor's flags: a planned withdrawal within 5 years (then shift new contributions to XGRO/VGRO or a bond sleeve for that portion), a first-home purchase (FHSA withdrawal), a change of residence (Scenario B, adviser first), a marriage or dependant, or an income change that moves the contribution amount. Each change is written in `plan/changes.md` with the research citation one month before execution (P5). A fee or index change by the issuer is a REVIEW item, not a change, unless the MER rises above 0.30% or the fund announces termination.

**All-in-one vs DIY switch.** Move from XEQT to the DIY alternative only when total invested assets exceed **C$300,000** (**Assumption:** the owner's reading of the report's "few hundred thousand dollars" threshold; at that balance the 6-7 bps saving is about C$180-210/yr) and the owner has met M6 for twelve months, since the switch adds about ten minutes a month and the risk of a mistimed manual rebalance. **ACB and capital gains.** In registered accounts (TFSA, FHSA, RRSP) a sale of XEQT has no tax consequence, so the switch there may sell and rebuy. In a taxable account a sale realises a capital gain against the ledger's adjusted cost base (P3 Rule 2 step 3), so the switch is made by **redirecting new contributions** to the DIY sleeves and leaving the existing XEQT units in place until the adviser consulted under P4 trigger (c) confirms the tax cost; this means a taxable account may hold XEQT and DIY sleeves together during the transition, which is the one exception to the next sentence. Move back to all-in-one if a drift band is missed twice in a year. Otherwise never hold both in the same account.

**Broker-change trigger (audit gap 5).** If Wealthsimple ends $0 commissions on Canadian ETFs, fractional shares or recurring buys (the three Table D features the monthly procedure relies on), or raises a fixed fee on a CAD-only Core account: (1) do not move existing holdings; (2) at the next quarterly re-scan confirm Questrade's pricing from a primary page (its $0 since Feb 2025 is secondary-sourced, Table D, audit gap 4) and, if confirmed, open the matching account type there and redirect **new contributions only**, rounding to whole units because Questrade's fractional shares are US-listed only (Table D); (3) if Questrade cannot be confirmed either, IBKR Canada is the third option at CAD 0.01/share with a CAD 1.00 minimum and no account minimum or inactivity fee (Table D, Canonical facts corrections): a C$1,000 buy of fewer than 100 units costs the C$1.00 minimum, about 10 bps per buy, or about C$4/month for four DIY sleeves (Assumption: unit prices above C$10, so the minimum binds). A transfer-out fee at the old broker (Questrade's is C$150, secondary, Table D) is accepted only after the new broker is confirmed, and the move is recorded in `plan/changes.md` with the P5 one-month notice.

**Kill the monitor** if any of: cost exceeds $50/month for two consecutive months (a move to Max is above this line and is therefore an owner decision recorded in `plan/changes.md`, never an escalation, P13); the owner stops reading the monthly review for three months; the grounding check fails twice in a quarter without a fix; the daily note's uptime falls below 70% of scheduled trading-day runs for a quarter (fewer than about 15 of 21 notes a month, stub or full, on the same denominator as M7) after the cloud and local routes have both been tried; or the owner notices the flags influencing a contribution decision (the monitor exists to inform, and the moment it tempts timing it has negative value). Killing the monitor changes nothing in the portfolio: the recurring buy continues.

**Quarterly re-scan procedure (about 1.5 hours, months 3, 6, 9, 12).** (1) Re-read the issuer fact sheets for the held tickers (MER, AUM, structure, index, distribution frequency) and update `portfolio.yaml`; look up the current Shiller CAPE on the Shiller data page and type it with its as-of date into `config/context.yaml` (P9; this is the only manual data input in the toolkit and it is stale after 100 days, which the next re-scan refreshes). (2) `gh api repos/{owner}/{repo}` for the used libraries and MCP servers: last push, latest release, open issues, licence; drop any dependency with no push in 12 months or a licence change. (3) Scan GitHub trending and the report's Table E/F set for new ETF-relevant tools; a candidate is adopted only if it is read-only, Claude-native or plain Python, maintained within 90 days, and replaces something rather than adding a layer. (4) Re-run the P12 flag scoring to date. (5) Record the scan in `plan/rescan_YYYY-QN.md`; bump pins (P13). New research on allocation is treated like a new rule: it must pass the P12 gate and the P5 overlay rule before it changes anything.

**No-advice posture.** Every report carries "personal research for the owner's own accounts, not advice". The owner is not advising for a business purpose under 31-103CP s.1.3 (no clients, compensation, solicitation, holding-out or intermediation; 2014 consolidation quoted, verify against the 2026 CP during the first re-scan, audit gap 7). Publishing, sharing or monetising the reports crosses that line and the data licences at once and is prohibited by CLAUDE.md.
