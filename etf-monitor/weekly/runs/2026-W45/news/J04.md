## What mattered
- **Midterms (Nov 3):** Democrats won both chambers and the Texas governorship. On Nov 4 hospitals, managed care, homebuilders, airlines and renewables rallied, while defense, drones and data-centre power and capex names fell (CNBC, Reuters). Analysts (J.P. Morgan, Morgan Stanley) expect gridlock under the veto, so much of the move is sentiment.
- **Data-centre backlash:** Texas governor-elect Hinojosa backs a moratorium on top of the existing state permit pause. Voters rejected data centres in all but one of about 40 local measures. Vistra fell about 14% and was downgraded by KeyBanc; GE Vernova fell 11.5% and Comfort Systems 15.1%. Vistra then beat and raised guidance on Nov 6.
- **Rates:** October payrolls came in at +165k against about 65k expected. Odds of a Dec 9 Fed hike rose to about 78%, with the 10-year near 4.87% and the 30-year near 5.27%. Mortgage rates eased for a third week but stay above 7%.
- **Healthcare:** CMS made Medicare drug negotiation permanent from 2029 (Nov 5), and the GLOBE MFN model starts Nov 30. AstraZeneca reportedly offered about $10B for Rhythm; neither company has confirmed it.
- **Defense:** Jefferies sees the FY27 budget near $1.15T, against the $1.5T request. TD Cowen sees the Golden Dome and Golden Fleet programmes at risk. The stopgap funding runs to Dec 11.
- **Clean energy:** At least seven bills to restore IRA credits are pending but have had no hearings and lapse on Jan 3. Revolution Wind reached full commercial operation. First Solar cut sales guidance, and SolarEdge guided weak.
