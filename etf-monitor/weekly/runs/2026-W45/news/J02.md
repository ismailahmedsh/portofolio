## What happened
- **Midterms (Nov 3):** Democrats won the Senate and are projected to win the House. Expect gridlock and more oversight from January (XTB, Nov 4).
- **AI rout (Nov 5):** The S&P 500 fell 4.4%, the Nasdaq fell 5.5% into a correction and the semiconductor index fell about 12%. Data-center regulation fears were part of the trigger, and BofA says multiples, not estimates, drove the drop. For the week the S&P 500 fell 2.7%, the Nasdaq 3.4% and the Russell 2000 2.05%. XLK fell 8.2% and XLE 12%, while XLF rose 1.2%, XLY 1.9% and XLC 4.9% (CNBC, Closing Print).
- **Earnings:** Q3 S&P 500 blended EPS growth is 31.4% and revenue growth 14.3%. 84% beat EPS, but the average surprise was only 2.7%, well below average. The forward P/E is 17.7 and analysts expect 28.4% growth in Q4 (FactSet, Nov 6).
- **Style:** On Nov 5, IVE fell 2.8% and IVW 5.75%. Over one month IVE is +2.6% and IVW -11.6%. Staples, Health Care and Utilities are up 8-13% over one month (ETF Action).
- **Consumer:** October payrolls rose 165k (about 65k expected), unemployment is 4.1% and hourly earnings rose 0.4% m/m. UMich sentiment rose to 51 from 48. McDonald's US comps were -0.2% but improving, with the dividend raised 4%. Airbnb beat and raised its guide, and DraftKings missed.
- **Financials:** AIG beat on investment income (+42.5%), while catastrophe losses lifted its combined ratio to 91.3% (Reuters, Nov 3). The Fed's capital-rule overhaul now faces a Democratic Congress.

## Interpretation
Earnings are strong, but AI-concentration risk and a hawkish Fed are compressing multiples. Odds of a December 9 hike were about 70% before the jobs beat, with oil near $90-98. The rotation favors value, mid-caps and dividend payers. Hike risk caps the upside for small caps and the consumer.
