**Energy (-1).** The midterm sweep unwound the Iran war premium. Jan-27 Brent fell 13% in the week to $98.32, WTI fell to about $91 and XLE fell 12%. Physical signals agree: Aramco cut Asian OSPs by $2, Dubai moved into contango, and JPMorgan sees a 1.4 mb/d surplus. The score stays at -1 rather than -2 because the risk runs both ways: minor US strikes on Qeshm resumed on Nov 6, 11 ships were attacked during the week, FGE's limited-strike window runs from mid-November to mid-December, and US distillate stocks are at their lowest since 2003.

**Materials (0, leaning negative).** Copper hit a 17-week low (about -5.5% over the month) on fears about AI data-centre demand. FCX fell 7.5% and SCCO 6.5% on Nov 5. Against that, Chinese physical copper is at a record premium, and Grasberg is constrained into 2027. Copper miners now trade as an AI-capex proxy.

**Gold (0).** Gold futures rose from $3,899 to about $4,075 (about +4.5% for the week) but are still about 23% below the January peak. Strong payrolls put December hike odds back at about 75%, and Goldman cut its end-2026 target to $4,150. Miner margins are capped because spot is below the Q3 realised price while AISC is rising.

**Broad (-1).** Energy carries the most weight and lost its premium. Copper and grains are soft (soybeans at an 11-week low, wheat at a 6-month low), and gold is not enough to offset them.

**Data fixes.** $118 was the Oct 30 settle of the expiring Brent futures contract. $135 was the Oct 2 Dated spot price. The continuous BZ=F series overstates the week's drop by about $4.2 because of the contract roll. The Nov 10 rare-earth cliff looks defused (USTR proposes extending to Jan 9, 2027), but MOFCOM has not confirmed.

**Red flags.** Copper ETF basis distorted by tariff flows; Brent ETF roll and contango risk; PPLT rally versus WPIC surplus forecast.
