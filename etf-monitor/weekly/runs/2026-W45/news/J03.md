**What happened.** After the Democratic midterm sweep, Democrats called for a national data-center moratorium, voters rejected data centers in 40 of 41 local ballot measures, and Texas Governor-elect Hinojosa pledged to ban new approvals once she takes office on Jan 19. On Nov 5 the SOX fell about 12% (Nvidia -9.3%, AMD -17%) and the Nasdaq entered correction. The selloff spread to Korea (circuit breaker), Taiwan (stabilisation fund deployed) and Japan (Nikkei bear market). The funding side also cracked. Microsoft is winding down its Oracle rental and trimming its neocloud partners. Oracle raised $20B at 7-8.65% coupons. CoreWeave and Nebius roughly halved in a month.

**Why -1 and not -2.** Demand data holds: AMD data center +97%, Korea chip exports +259%, a TSMC Q4 guide of about +14% q/q, Nvidia's own $108B guide, and hyperscalers' own 2027 capex rising. A federal moratorium is a long shot: bills must be reintroduced in 2027, there are no 60 Senate votes, and a veto. State statute risk comes after the 1-3 month window. Chips rebounded 5-8% on Nov 6. The move reads as a policy, financing and valuation unwind, not a demand collapse.

**us_large_broad.** The S&P 500 fell 4.4% on Nov 5 and 2.7% on the week, and December hike odds are about 75% after payrolls of +165k. Breadth is healthy: equal-weight RSP, XLP and XLV hit records on Nov 4, and hyperscalers fell only 2-4%. That makes this a concentration drag on cap-weighted trackers, scored with low confidence.

**Correlation warning.** The -1 scores for tech, Korea/Taiwan, Japan, copper, IPPs, HY and small caps are a single AI-buildout bet, not independent views.

**Red flags.** SMH/SOXX/XLK/QQQ concentration and volatility. Leveraged SOXL is down about 73% since Oct 1. Neocloud and Oracle credit: CoreWeave bonds yield about 950bp or more. HACK/CIBR have no fund event but holdings are crowded. EWY moves to a 25% capped index on Nov 9.
