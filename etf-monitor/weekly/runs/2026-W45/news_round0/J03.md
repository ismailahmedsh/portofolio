## What happened
- **Midterm shock.** Democrats won both chambers on Nov 3. Leading Democrats then called for a "national data center moratorium" (CNBC, Nov 5). On Nov 5 the SOX fell more than 12%, the Nasdaq 5.5% and the S&P 500 4.4%, each the worst day since April 2025 (Fox Business, CNBC). Nvidia fell 9.3%, AMD 17.3%, Micron 17.5% and Broadcom 10.4%. XLK lost 8% on the week. Chips partly rebounded on Nov 6, with SOX up 4.2%.
- **Hyperscaler discipline.** Microsoft is winding down its Oracle capacity deal and cutting its neocloud partners (Bloomberg, CNBC, Nov 6). Some GPU contracts slipped into 1H27.
- **Fundamentals still strong.** AMD reported record Q3 revenue of $13.4B, with data center up 97%, and guided Q4 to about $17B (Nov 3). Apple posted $114.5B in revenue and a record September-quarter iPhone (Nov 2).
- **Macro.** October payrolls came in at +165k and markets price about 70% odds of a December Fed hike, which weighs on long-duration tech.

## Interpretation
The selloff hit valuation multiples, not earnings, so far (BofA). The policy overhang is real but slow-moving: any law needs Trump's signature, and Barclays says local opposition makes the buildout costlier rather than stopping it. Ives (Yorkville) now sees capex growth peaking in 2027. Net: a mild headwind with downside skew. This is not -2 because no rule or law has changed and reported numbers remain strong.

## Red flags
- **XLK, QQQ, SMH, SOXX: concentration risk has materialised.** The exposure is to a single factor, AI capex, which now carries policy risk. The Magnificent Seven lost more than $1T on Nov 5. The Magnificent Seven are about 32-35% of the S&P 500 (secondary source). Index weights probably fell this week, so this flag is about correlated drawdown risk, not a new weight high.
- **SMH, SOXX: volatility regime break.** Leveraged semiconductor products are exposed around Nvidia earnings on Nov 17.
