## What happened
- **Midterms changed how markets price the Iran war.** Democrats won both chambers and Trump acknowledged the war contributed to the losses. Gulf stocks rallied: Dubai +4%, Qatar +2.8% and Saudi +1.7% on Nov 4. MSCI All-World rose 1.38% (Reuters).
- **Oil lost part of its war premium.** Brent fell nearly 9% to $94.62 on Nov 4 and about 15% on the week to ~$99.
- **The conflict has not ended.** At least five vessels have been hit in Hormuz since Nov 1, after 35 in October. Drones were intercepted over Riyadh and Mecca on Nov 6. Trump warned the US may have to "destroy" the rest of Iran, while Qatari mediation continues.
- **Saudi fiscal position weakened.** Q3 oil revenue fell 23% y/y and the 9-month deficit reached SR244.3bn. The 2027 deficit is projected at 3.6% of GDP. Aramco beat Q3 estimates but its variable dividend stays suspended.
- **OPEC+ held December targets.** The capacity review is due by mid-November, and the full group meets on Nov 29 to set 2027 quotas.
- **Sanctions and trade.** OFAC sanctioned India- and Turkey-based firms for Iran ties (Nov 4). The Russia oil-buyer tariffs remain waived pending a ~60-day review. The US-China tariff pause runs to Jan 10.

## Interpretation
- Gulf equities are neutral overall. A deal brokered through Qatar would be a strong tailwind and renewed US strikes a strong headwind. Fiscal news is negative.
- For oil, the geopolitical risk now skews to the downside if Hormuz reopens, but the two-way tail risk is extreme.

## Red flags
- KSA/UAE/QAT: direct war exposure (attacks on shipping, drones over Saudi cities).
- QAT: the LNG force majeure has been rolled monthly.
- KSA: Aramco's variable dividend is still suspended.
- USO/XOP/OIH/XLE/VDE: gap risk on a deal or on new strikes.
- INDA/EPI/TUR: new Iran-related sanctions on local firms.
- INDA/TUR/China ETFs: Russia buyer tariffs could be reinstated.
