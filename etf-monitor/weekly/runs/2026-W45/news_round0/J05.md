## What mattered
- **Energy relief, but it can reverse.** After the Democratic midterm sweep, hopes for a US-Iran deal (with Qatar mediating) knocked TTF gas about 16% off its four-year high (about EUR 85/MWh on 5 Nov) and pushed Brent sharply lower. EU storage is only about 74% full (86% norm), and Qatari LNG force majeure runs to end-December.
- **Inflation and hikes.** Euro area flash HICP rose to 4.0% in October (energy +20.8%, core 2.5%). The BoE hiked 25bp to 4.0% (8-1, first rise since 2023) and sees CPI near 5% in mid-2027. The RBA hiked to 4.85% (6-3), and the BoC went to 2.5% on 28 Oct. The ECB held at 2.50% on 29 Oct, and December is live.
- **Growth is softening.** The euro area composite PMI is about 51.9 flash (down from 53.1), and Q3 GDP grew 0.4% q/q, with France contracting. Canada shed 4,700 jobs as unemployment rose to 6.7%.
- **Markets.** The STOXX 600 nearly hit a record on Thursday and ended the week about 0.2% lower, beating the Nasdaq (-5.5% on 5 Nov). The Hang Seng rose 5.1% for the week, its best since March 2025, but fell 1.3% on Friday as the AI selloff spread.
- **FX.** Sterling's trade-weighted index is at its highest since June 2016, GBP/EUR is above 1.20 and the dollar is weaker after the midterms.
- **Defence.** Rheinmetall cut its 2026 guidance for the second time (Boxer order slipped to 2027) and fell more than 17%. BAE, Leonardo and Saab also fell.
- **Singapore banks.** DBS and UOB missed Q3 estimates and OCBC beat, with margins compressing.
